# PING Social pricing market research

Researched 2026-09-25, public web only. Read only, no outreach, no purchases.

Confidence labels used below:
- **Well-sourced**: taken from a provider's own published pricing page.
- **Directional**: industry guides, directories, or multi-source synthesis; useful for ranges, not exact quotes.
- **Rough estimate**: single-source or lightly verified; treat as order of magnitude.

All prices USD unless noted. Nolan's standing rules apply: no em dashes, and the em dash character does not appear in this file.

## 0. Where Nolan's provisional $400 to $600 offer sits

Nolan's working offer language: roughly $400 to $600 for a website, setup, and several AI tools, depending on actual needs, with possible PING repo access and Muse setup.

Against the market:

- A **small-business website build** nationally runs $299 (templated) to $7,000 (full e-commerce), with contractor-focused builders landing at $497 to $1,500 and custom packages at $1,000 to $4,500. A $400 to $600 website component is low but inside the plausible range for a lean template or one-day build. It undercuts Colorado-agency custom work ($14,000+) by an order of magnitude.
- **One AI receptionist alone** costs the market $50 to $300/month, with the sweet spot around $150 to $250/month. **Missed-call text-back** runs $15 to $47/month standalone. **Review software** runs $75 to $399/month. **Social media management** runs $189/month at the absolute cheapest published tier, with most small-business plans $400 to $2,500/month.
- Put together as a basket (website + AI answering + text-back + reviews + a little social content), the going market rate is roughly **$1,000 to $3,500/month ongoing** plus a **$1,500 to $5,000 setup**. Nolan's $400 to $600 one-time-style framing is dramatically below every published basket on the market. That is either a positioning advantage (penetration pricing for the trial period, which matches his "limited-time trial while the service is being developed" framing) or a margin risk if it sticks after the trial. The research does not answer which; it only shows how far below market it is.

## 8. Home-service marketing spend benchmarks

The defensible planning range is **5% to 10% of gross revenue**. Sources explicitly call this planning guidance, not a measured industry average.

| Annual revenue | 5% budget | 10% budget | Monthly equivalent |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $500,000 | $25,000/year | $50,000/year | ~$2,083 to $4,167/month |
| $1,000,000 | $50,000/year | $100,000/year | ~$4,167 to $8,333/month |
| $2,000,000 | $100,000/year | $200,000/year | ~$8,333 to $16,667/month |

Notes:
- Fervor Studio uses 7% as its typical HVAC worksheet assumption; 5% to 10% is described as common.
- Revved Digital labels 5% to 10% as moderate-confidence planning guidance.
- Adwave: 5% to 10%, with 10% to 12% for newer or aggressively growing shops.
- HVAC Marketing Xperts recommends 12% to 15% for aggressive growth (agency advice; worked example references 2021).
- Older contractor commentary: 5% is maintenance, 10% is growth.

Interpretation for PING: a $2,000/month PING basket is roughly 4.8% of revenue for a $500K shop and 2.4% for a $1M shop, comfortably inside normal marketing spend. Even a $500/month basket is under 1.2% at $500K revenue. Marketing-spend framing is favorable to almost any basket Nolan prices.

## 9. What this means for basket design (no copy, analysis only)

1. The cheapest published per-service floors: social $189/month, website $299 one-time, review software $75/month, text-back $15 to $47/month, AI answering $25 to $65/month (budget) or $150 to $250/month (real). A bottom-up sum of cheapest floors is roughly $300 to $450/month before labor.
2. A realistic mid-market basket (website amortized + AI answering + reviews + light social) sits around **$1,000 to $2,000/month**. Full-service baskets run $2,500 to $5,000/month.
3. Nolan's $400 to $600 figure only works as a **setup or trial-entry price**, not as ongoing monthly for the full basket. If it is monthly, it undercuts the cheapest published social plan alone ($189/month) once any other service is added, and it would be roughly one quarter of the going mid-market basket.
4. The transparent-basket gap is real: no competitor found publishes a single price for website + AI answering + reviews + social together. That is the pricing page's opening.
5. Missing pieces worth one more pass before copy is written: primary vendor pages for AI receptionists (most numbers above come from comparison articles), any genuinely local GJ bundle pricing, and confirmation of HTJ Media's location.
