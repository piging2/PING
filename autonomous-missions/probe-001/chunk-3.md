# Chunk 3: Which mechanisms matter most for worker-replacement reconstruction

Of the mechanisms harvested in mechanisms.jsonl, the ones that matter most for the worker-replacement reconstruction invariant are those that make an in-flight attempt self-describing and resumable without conversational memory, and this very probe is the test case. oa-runstate-durable-snapshot matters because it serializes inputs, model responses, turn counters, usage, and approvals into durable storage, which is exactly what a cold replacement worker must recover after a kill; DBOS-STEP-RECORDING matters because recording each completed step as it finishes bounds the redo surface, which is why chunk-1 and chunk-2 were verifiably complete and did not need blind retry; DBOS-ATOMIC-RECOVERY matters because re-enqueuing recovery through an atomic queue transition guarantees exactly-once revival even when multiple executors race to claim the same dead attempt, which is the replacement worker's central hazard; O-1 matters because a canonical SQLite run row with typed status and a single writer gives the replacement worker one authoritative, queryable home for the attempt's existence and lifecycle instead of scattered claims; and LANGGRAPH-CHECKPOINT matters because versioned, monotonically increasing checkpoints with pending writes let the replacement restore exact state and distinguish verified-completed work from merely attempted work. Together these five establish the invariant this probe demonstrates: durable, monotonic, atomically transitioned records turn a mid-attempt worker death from an ambiguity into a bounded, replay-free continuation.
