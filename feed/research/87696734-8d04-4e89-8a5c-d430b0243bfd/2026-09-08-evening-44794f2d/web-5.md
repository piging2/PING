## Finding 1
Xcel Energy's Colorado rebate for cold-climate heat pumps drops on October 1, 2026 from $2,250 per heating ton to $1,500 per heating ton, a one-third cut worth roughly $2,000 on a typical three-ton system. Only equipment installed and invoiced before October 1 qualifies for the higher amount, with the rebate application due by October 15; a signed contract or deposit before October 1 does not hold it. A typical three-ton cold-climate system installed before October 1 can qualify for roughly $7,500 in combined rebates (Xcel plus Power Ahead Colorado's $1,500 for Denver-metro installs plus the Colorado state tax credit), versus roughly $5,500 if installed October 2 or later. Goodfellas Heating & Cooling of Northglenn, which published this breakdown, says it is installing next day, every day, through the deadline and warns the only way to miss the higher amount is to wait until the last days of September when everyone else is calling. The stackable-incentive rush lands right as Denver heads into heating season, meaning Denver-area HVAC shops are likely in a booking crunch this month rather than the usual fall lull. The federal 25C tax credit is gone for 2026, so Xcel and state programs are doing the pulling now.

## Finding 2
Veteran Heating, Cooling, Plumbing & Electric, a Denver-based home services company, marked its first anniversary on August 26, 2026 with 2,132 completed jobs, roughly 18,000 field hours, about 4,000 customers served, and approximately 350 five-star reviews. The company says it grew from its first job to 69 employees operating 17 wrapped and upfitted trucks across the Denver metro in twelve months, with no outbound sales calls and no email or text blasts, and avoided the shoulder-season slowdown that typically defines Colorado HVAC. It claims HVAC and electrical work is now booked weeks or months in advance, and it secured more than $1.4 million in approved HEAR (Heat Electrification and Affordability Rebate) projects, helping Denver-area homeowners access Xcel Energy rebate programs for high-efficiency HVAC and electrification upgrades. The company is pursuing licensing and permitting for expansion into Dallas/Fort Worth, with Wisconsin under consideration, and is in the early stages of a capital raise. This is a competitor growing fast on rebate navigation and word-of-mouth, not on outbound marketing.

## Finding 3
As reported in February 2026, the Denver-Aurora-Lakewood metro area lost about 4,700 construction payroll jobs year over year, one of the largest declines among U.S. metro areas in the prior year, even as a 2025 construction industry survey found 92 percent of construction firms were struggling to hire workers, with nearly half reporting that staffing shortages had delayed projects. Colorado-based Laborjack, a provider of on-demand labor support for construction and other industries, expanded its construction labor services in Colorado to meet demand from contractors needing faster, more reliable crew fills. The company says contractors are getting calls to protect schedules before jobs fall behind and are moving toward flexible workforce models with faster response times, plus mobile-first scheduling and communication tools to fill gaps when workers are unexpectedly unavailable. This is an older report, from February 2026, but it describes a structural labor squeeze that directly hurts home services shops heading into their busiest booking window of the year.
