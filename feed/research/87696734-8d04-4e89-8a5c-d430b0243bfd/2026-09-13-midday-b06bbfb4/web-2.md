## Finding 1
The Colorado Attorney General's office will hold a public hearing on October 26, 2026 at 10 a.m. at the Department of Law in Denver (with remote participation) on proposed rules implementing two new state AI laws, Senate Bill 26-189 (the Automated Decision-Making Technology Act) and House Bill 26-1263 (the Chatbot Safety Act), both taking effect January 1, 2027. The proposed rules, filed August 11, 2026 at 4 CCR 904-6, would clarify requirements for developers and deployers of automated decision-making technology, including disclosure and reporting obligations and consumer rights, as well as requirements for operators of conversational AI services to protect users and submit annual reports to the Attorney General. Written comments may be submitted through October 26, 2026; the AG asked for comments by September 4 for consideration in interim revisions expected to be posted by September 23, 2026. Among the open questions the department is seeking input on are responsibilities when businesses rely on AI operated by third-party vendors, which is exactly how a contractor using a vendor's AI receptionist would deploy the technology. For PING Social: the Colorado rule that will govern conversational-AI disclosure mechanics is now in formal rulemaking with a hearing October 26, and the vendor/third-party responsibility question is still open in the comment record.

## Finding 2
The Colorado AG's proposed rules filed August 11, 2026 would put operational detail on the state's two AI statutes before they take effect January 1, 2027. For operators of consumer-facing conversational AI, the proposed rules spell out disclosure mechanics: how and when the 'you are talking to an AI' disclosure appears, including refreshes during long sessions; minor protections and crisis protocols; professional-impersonation controls implementing the ban on presenting chatbot output as provided by, endorsed by, or equivalent to a licensed health-care, legal, or mental-health professional; and annual reports to the AG with user tiers, with the reporting duty starting July 1, 2027. Critically, neither the statutes nor the proposed rules contain a small-business exemption; business size shows up only in the 'commercially reasonable' qualifier on the meaningful-human-review duty for ADMT deployers. The formal comment window runs through October 26, 2026 at 11:59 PM via comments.coag.gov. For PING Social: a Colorado contractor running an AI phone receptionist is the kind of small-business deployer these rules reach with no exemption, so the 'you are talking to an AI' disclosure mechanics are the pitch-relevant compliance fact, and the rules are still a preview rather than an obligation until January 1, 2027.
- https://availablelaw.com/blog/colorado-admt-chatbot-rules-proposed-2026 (published 2026-08-12)

## Finding 3
The FCC is moving to tighten its robocall regime on the providers that carry calls. The FCC's proposals (backed by a July 1, 2026 fact sheet) would strengthen 'know-your-upstream-provider' requirements, raise STIR/SHAKEN caller-ID attestation standards, and close STIR/SHAKEN implementation loopholes, with the aim of cutting providers that enable illegal robocalls out of the voice ecosystem. A September 2026 industry analysis of the proposed Robocall Mitigation Database overhaul reports filers would certify four things: no false filings, compliance with robocall/caller-ID/numbering rules, meeting STIR/SHAKEN attestation requirements, and responding to traceback requests within 24 hours; the proposed filing would also carry principals' contact details and citizenship, every affiliate and subsidiary, a US registered agent, and prior regulatory actions. The July 2026 NPRM proposals also seek enhanced monetary penalties for know-your-upstream violations, improper attestations, and STIR/SHAKEN failures. For PING Social: this is provider-side compliance that affects any AI calling platform a contractor uses; contractors' outbound AI calls (reminders, callbacks) inherit the diligence posture of their voice provider, so a vendor that cannot answer a traceback with originating tenant, source IP, caller ID presented, and consent record is a liability for the contractor.

## Finding 4
No new federal AI-call disclosure rule moved in the last 14 days. The standing federal position remains the FCC's 2024 posture: its unanimous 2024 declaratory ruling treats AI voice-cloning robocalls as 'artificial' voices under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, making AI-generated robocalls illegal without prior express consent, and a 2024 NPRM proposed requiring callers making AI-generated artificial or prerecorded voice messages to clearly disclose at the beginning of the call that the call uses AI-generated technology, plus consent-form disclosure that consent extends to AI-generated calls. Neither has advanced to a final rule in the recent window. Separately, the EU transparency code angle was already covered in a prior edition and is excluded from this sweep.
