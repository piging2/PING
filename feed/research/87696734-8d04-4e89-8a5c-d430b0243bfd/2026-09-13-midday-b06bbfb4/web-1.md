## Finding 1
Take 5 Oil Change is building a new location at 2260 North Ave. in Grand Junction, at the northwest corner of the 23rd Street and North Avenue intersection. The former auto-repair building on the site has been demolished, and a Mesa County building permit issued May 11, 2026 describes the work as a new build for a one-story building. The Business Times reported it in its August 18, 2026 business briefs. A new service location means a business setting up phones, scheduling, customer intake and follow-up from scratch, which is the moment PING Social pitches. Note the trade is automotive service rather than home service, but the customer-service shape is the same.
- https://thebusinesstimes.com/category/business-briefs/page/3/ (undated)

## Finding 2
Rescue 1 Plumbing, a Grand Junction plumbing company, announced on September 9, 2026 that it has expanded into water-efficiency services for residential and commercial properties, including smart fixture installations, water auditing, precision leak detection and advanced irrigation control systems. Company founder Jack Thompson said the expansion reflects a commitment to local resource management as drought conditions and rising municipal utility costs drive demand. Not a new business, but an established local plumber adding a service line this month, which makes it a warm prospect for follow-up and intake work.
- https://lifestyle.loopbiz.com/story/329139/rescue-1-plumbing-expands-services-to-improve-water-efficiency/ (published 2026-09-09)

## Finding 3
Comfort Air of Grand Junction was awarded a $2,223,684 Mesa County contract to replace rooftop heating and cooling units on the Mesa County Justice Center, approved by the Mesa County Commission at an August 11 public hearing and reported August 18, 2026. An established local HVAC contractor landing a county-scale job is a warm signal: bigger commercial workload typically strains scheduling, dispatch and customer follow-up, the exact pain PING Social targets.
