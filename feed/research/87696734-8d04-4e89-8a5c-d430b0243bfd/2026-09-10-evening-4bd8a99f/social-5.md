## Finding 1
Jia, a cofounder of the tool Mochi, posted September 8, 2026 that creators and businesses are answering Instagram DMs in the wrong order because Instagram surfaces the newest messages first, which buries qualified leads underneath hours or days of unread threads. His video illustrates inboxes overflowing with hundreds of unread messages and a pricing-request message from 'Richard Sean' sitting unanswered. Mochi's 'Priority Inbox' automatically categorizes conversations into 'New lead,' 'In contact,' 'Qualified,' and 'Call booked' so high-intent messages get answered faster, and graphics show booked calls climbing to 88+. His repeated core claim, filed here as the platform's own summary of the post rather than quoted words, is that the problem is not needing more leads or messages but converting the ones already arriving. The post promotes a 14-day free trial via DM. The account @jiia has 46K followers, per the search result metadata, making this one of the larger accounts in the sweep.

## Finding 2
The account @fixyourinbox, which markets auto-replies for local businesses, posted September 8, 2026 a pitch aimed directly at the DM-handling problem: it lists the four repetitive questions local businesses receive in DMs, namely how much the service costs, whether they open on Saturdays, where they are located, and whether they take cards, and argues that manually answering them all day keeps owners away from ready-to-pay clients, wasting time and money. The post promotes an AI-powered inbox product called 'THE INBOX' that works with Instagram and WhatsApp to automatically reply to and qualify leads without touching the phone. It cites an Instagram profile for 'Armando Reza' with 94.7K followers and claims 500+ messages a day are handled with personalized chatbot replies, and closes with the line 'Nobody buys from the best. They buy from whoever answered.' The account is small (about 1K followers per the result metadata) and the performance claim is the marketer's own, so a writer should present it as the vendor's claim, not as verified fact.

## Finding 3
Brandon Harrison (@mr_brandon_harrison), posting September 10, 2026 and targeting remodeling contractors, argues in a six-slide carousel that owning a CRM is insufficient because homeowners often initiate contact outside formal forms, via social comments, DMs, missed calls, or website visits, and those touchpoints need automated capture before they ever reach the CRM. His slides describe the gap where inquiries stall without capture paths and lay out a 'connected approach' workflow: social interaction to capture to CRM record to follow-up to appointment, with a note that unanswered comments can remain completely unknown to the CRM. Slide 5 illustrates the flow with an example sequence, and the post closes inviting viewers to comment 'DEMO' for a workflow example. The account is small (32 followers per the result metadata), so this reads as a practitioner's take, but the underlying observation, that social touchpoints bypass the CRM, is directly the consultative-pitch material the assignment asked for.
