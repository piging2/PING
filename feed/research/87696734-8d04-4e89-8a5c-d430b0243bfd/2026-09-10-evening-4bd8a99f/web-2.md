## Finding 1
Mesa County Building Department warns that following recent hailstorms, many property owners across the Grand Valley are moving quickly to repair roof damage, and the county is seeing a growing number of out-of-town contractors starting work without proper permits, inspections, or, inside Grand Junction city limits, a valid City contractor's license. The county states permits are required before any roofing work starts, mid-roof inspections must take place when 10 percent to 40 percent of the roof is complete, and in the City of Grand Junction roofing contractors are required to be licensed with the City. It adds that a roof completed without a mid-roof inspection may need partial removal for inspection, causing extra cost and delay, and advises homeowners to verify licensing, confirm the contractor obtains the proper permit, and have the contractor coordinate inspections. ## Finding 2
Mesa County commissioners approved an updated agreement with Housing Resources of Western Colorado for a regional stormwater detention facility near 32 1/2 Road and the Grand Valley Canal, discussed at the Sept. 8 public hearing. The approximately 2.3-acre facility will temporarily hold and manage stormwater runoff and help meet water quality requirements for the 32 1/2 Road Phase II Roadway Improvement Project and future development on the property. The county plans to build it as part of the larger roadway project; Housing Resources will continue to own the property and will hold title to, operate and maintain the facility at its expense after construction. If Housing Resources needs it sooner, it may build with county approval and the county could reimburse eligible documented costs up to $200,000. Design is complete but construction has not begun, and a separate construction contract for the roadway improvements and stormwater facility will come before commissioners. Housing Resources plans Crawford Commons Apartments on the remaining property, with a proposed first phase of 60 affordable housing units and space reserved for a possible second phase, expecting to complete tax credit and debt financing in spring 2027 and begin construction afterward. ## Finding 3
Mesa County is pursuing the Orchard Mesa Check Piping - 38 Road Bike and Pedestrian Project in Palisade and East Orchard Mesa, with a total estimated cost of $5,968,600. The county signed a letter of support at its June 24 public hearing to apply for $1,786,800 through the Colorado Water Plan Grant, with a local match of $4,181,800, including a $1,882,355 Federal Transportation Alternatives Program grant already awarded. The Orchard Mesa Irrigation District is a partner and will provide right-of-way and easements. The project would install two 96-inch pipes to replace the aging open check channel, increasing efficiency and conserving water, and build a bike and pedestrian shared-use path over the pipes along 38 Road between Palisade and East Orchard Mesa, part of the Fruit and Wine Byway. The county states that without piping the channel, alternative path installation methods are cost prohibitive and the path project would be cancelled. The page states no publication date.
