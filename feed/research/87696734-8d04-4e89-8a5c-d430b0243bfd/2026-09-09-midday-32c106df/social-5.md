Beat: social
Angle: What Facebook content and engagement tactics are working for home-service contractors in 2026: post formats earning real comments and profile views, groups driving inbound inquiries, contractor accounts owners point to as doing it well. Nolan runs Facebook-first outreach (likes, comments, DMs), so concrete examples beat general advice.

## Finding 1
Local Facebook groups are the most-cited organic inbound channel for home-service contractors in 2026, and the tactic is specific. Contractor and service-business owner Shellsea Moala's playbook (posted July 2, 2026) tells new contractors to skip paid ads at first and instead embed in local Facebook real-estate, community, and contractor groups, posting from their business page with a phone number visible for immediate contact; she pairs it with local-SEO websites, yard signs on every job, and door hangers. Nick Morales (1.2K followers, posted August 11, 2026) runs a 'Local Service Lead Engine' built on the same insight: homeowners post urgent requests daily in local groups ('AC stopped working', 'need a roofer'), and his system scans new posts and comments twice daily at 9 AM and 7 PM, flags service intent plus location, and drafts a helpful non-salesy public reply with AI, with the first 50 replies human-approved to train tone; the payoff is being the first helpful contractor in the thread instead of buying shared leads. On the paid side, Joshua Crouch (September 8, 2026) demonstrates mining public local homeowner groups (example: 'Las Vegas Homeowners') with the Lead Enforce platform to build Meta ad audiences of actual local homeowners. Caveat for the writer: two of these three sources sell marketing services, so the group-posting advice is practitioner consensus among contractor-marketers rather than a neutral study, but the mechanics are concrete and directly usable for Nolan's engagement passes.

## Finding 2
The single most repeated creative rule across 2026 contractor-marketing posts: real job-site photos beat polished graphics and stock. Kara Lee, a social media consultant for home-service businesses with 88K followers, posted August 25, 2026 advising contractors to use authentic, imperfect photos of themselves and crews, work in progress, before-and-after results, and real faces rather than polished graphics; her September 2, 2026 follow-up adds that owners should show up personally, document a project start to finish, and educate homeowners, valuing content quality over posting frequency. Contractor Zech Osburn (55K followers, July 21, 2026) tells home-service owners to 'smother' Facebook group posts and websites with authentic working photos of themselves and their teams working and smiling, calling it 'money.' A September 8, 2026 graphic from Authornate Digital visualizes the rule: one highlighted real photo of a technician beside an HVAC unit versus five generic stock photos, headlined 'ONE REAL JOB PHOTO CAN BUILD MORE TRUST THAN FIVE STOCK IMAGES,' arguing stock conveys nothing about the actual business. Contractor Click's ad-creative tier list (posted September 9, 2026, today) ranks before-and-after transformation ads S-tier ('they sell the dream'), customer testimonial ads A-tier, and stock footage F-tier, calling it 'impersonal and ineffective for 2026.' ## Finding 3
The highest-leverage organic post format being taught to contractors right now is the one-question answer video. Jordan Armstrong of Mira Lane Marketing (Acworth, Georgia, seven-plus years in the trade niche) posted a run of videos September 4, 2026 arguing that Facebook and Instagram work for home-service businesses not as 'call us' billboards but as pre-emptive trust builders: homeowners with questions are really asking 'can I trust this person with my home and my money,' so each video should answer one real homeowner question (cost, timeline, quoting from pictures, what happens if I wait) conversationally, with no scripts or trends. His system: write down the five most-asked questions from real customers, turn each into one simple video, promote the winners as local ads. The funnel he teaches skips the website and goes relevant message to DM conversation ('send me a picture,' plus ZIP code and availability) to qualify to estimate, and he judges it on revenue not likes: his September 2026 campaign snapshot shows $600 in Facebook/Instagram spend generating $4,175 in tracked revenue, a 6.96x ROAS. A compatible note from Daichi Zaatari (August 28, 2026): the winning 2026-2027 creative makes homeowners 'smile warmly' so the contractor reads as a trustworthy neighbor rather than a salesperson, which he says reduces price haggling and builds pre-click trust. Armstrong is a small account (87 followers) selling services, so treat the ROAS figure as his claimed case study, but the format advice is echoed across at least four independent marketer accounts in the sweep.

## Finding 4
Three dated, numbers-attached case studies contractors are pointing to this month. Dallas Davidson (posted September 1-2, 2026) shows a three-step Facebook ad formula, call out the audience, quick intro of who you are and why you are different, CTA with expiring value, and posts screenshots of three campaigns totaling 153 leads and $81,000 in reported revenue from $7,280 in ad spend for a concrete contractor earlier in 2026, offering the exact ad copy to commenters. Sarah B (posted September 7, 2026) walks through a Miro-board case study for an Oregon remodeling contractor: a Facebook Ads Manager screenshot shows 130 website leads at $8.55 cost per lead ($1,110.93 spent), with the client closing two jobs worth over $35,000 in the first month (a $22,000 kitchen remodel and a $13,000 bathroom), using before-and-after collages, discount offers ($1,000 off, 20-25% off), broad targeting layered with top 5-10% household income, a landing page with qualification questions, and automated SMS/email follow-up. KingPost Media (posted September 8, 2026) interviews Roberto of Modern General Contractors in Westchester, New York, who spent roughly $1,000 on ads in about 30 days, got around 13 booked appointments, and closed five jobs totaling about $55,000, after previously relying on Yelp ads where leads went to the lowest bidder. Supporting mechanics cited across the sweep: pre-qualifying form questions (project type, budget range, timeline) to get ten real leads instead of forty phone numbers, and contacting new leads within five to ten minutes of form submission, which remodeler-marketer Bercan Vlad says dramatically lifts estimate booking versus calling the next day. All figures are self-reported by marketers selling their systems, so they read as directional proof points with screenshots, not audited results.
