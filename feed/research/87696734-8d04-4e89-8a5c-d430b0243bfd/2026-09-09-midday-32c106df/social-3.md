Beat: social
Angle: How are home-service owners publicly talking this week about where their leads actually come from now: organic social, Facebook groups, Nextdoor, referrals, repeat business? Pull specific posts and threads, not vendor surveys; the paper already ran lead-price data and the Local Services Ads migration, so file only owner-reported tactics those stories did not cover.

## Finding 1
In a Wichita contractors Facebook group thread started August 23, 2026, a contractor asked peers how they consistently get leads, listing ads, yard signs, and word of mouth as options. Across 56 comments, contractors overwhelmingly answered that their work comes from referrals, repeat customers, and word of mouth, with several saying they do not advertise at all. Phone calls were repeatedly named as the primary inbound channel, with owners saying they answer the phone themselves while on jobs. Secondary channels named in the thread included Facebook group recommendations, Google presence, BBB listings, door knocking, networking and referral groups, chamber of commerce, email campaigns, truck wraps, and yard signs. The platform's summary of the thread notes the dominant theme was reputation, communication, and contractor-to-contractor referrals as the key drivers. The thread is owner-to-owner discussion, not a vendor pitch.
- https://www.facebook.com/groups/wichitacontractors/permalink/28156927240605208/ (published 2026-08-23)

## Finding 2
Connor Maendel of Maendel Fence & Deck posted August 24, 2026, breaking down exactly where his work comes from. He said he spends about seven to eight thousand dollars per month on advertising: approximately $3,000 on Facebook, $3,000 on Google, $1,500 on a billboard, and about $1,000 in other places. Despite that spend, he said 50 percent of his work comes from current customers, either through referrals or repeat jobs. He said he tracks all of it in Jobber, which shows his average job size, where he is working, and where leads originate. His account has about 19,000 followers and the post is framed as advice to fellow business owners.
- https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcbRvakxXeP/ (published 2026-08-24)

## Finding 3
In a Facebook reel posted August 25, 2026, a small business owner named Noah recommended the Nextdoor app as a free way to get new customers. He said he has used it for about a year and a half and got 13 jobs in the last year alone, describing them as large projects like patios and driveways. He emphasized that neighbors trust recommendations on the platform, especially genuine ones, and that the app is completely free to use. Note on sourcing: this is a small, unverified account with about 120 followers and minimal engagement, so it reads as one owner's anecdote rather than a corroborated trend; file it as texture alongside the stronger findings.
- https://www.facebook.com/reel/1419684613657850/ (published 2026-08-25)

## Finding 4
A current-week tactic circulating among home-service marketers targets local Facebook groups as the lead source itself. In videos posted September 3 and September 8, 2026, the accounts behind Leads for the Trades describe a 'power team' approach: when someone asks for an HVAC recommendation in a local Facebook group, a crew of allies all drops the contractor's name, which the on-screen text calls 'the chorus,' so the contractor wins the job without scrolling the groups himself. A related September 8 video from marketer Josh Crouch pitches Lead Enforce software that copies a public local group's URL, such as 'Las Vegas Homeowners,' pulls audience data from its members, and feeds it into Meta Ads Manager to run 'Local Homeowner Ads.' Note on sourcing: these are vendor and marketer posts, not owner reports, so they describe the Facebook-group tactic as it is being sold this week rather than as owners report using it; the owner thread in the first finding independently confirms Facebook group recommendations are a real channel contractors cite.
