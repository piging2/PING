## Finding 1
Morgan Mining, a contract miner that describes itself as 'mostly a skilled labor service company,' announced in February 2025 that it would build a mining-focused hub at the former Halliburton campus at 365 32 Road near Clifton in Grand Junction, with a goal of 893 net new jobs in the Grand Valley over eight years at an average salary of $92,447, well above the Mesa County median income. President Justin Morgan told The Daily Sentinel the company had brought in roughly 50 people in December and was 'hiring right now,' mostly experienced underground miners at first, with hiring to continue in sporadic waves of 40 to 50 people in a single month; he said all operations will be managed out of Grand Junction, with much of the training happening there too. The company, a subsidiary of Morgan Companies that had acquired Delta-based Phoenix Mining the prior year, chose Grand Junction over Knoxville, Tennessee for the expansion, citing the old Halliburton property and the Western Slope workforce; it received approval for a Job Growth Tax Incentive valued at up to $10.89 million, contingent on job creation and salary requirements. The City of Grand Junction separately purchased part of the old Halliburton property for $5.6 million for a materials recycling facility. The Daily Sentinel page states no publication date; its archive images are filed under the paper's March 12, 2025 edition, and the Mesa County page likewise states no publication date while referencing the February 25 public hearing. Nearly 900 high-wage households arriving in phases is the clearest standing demand signal on the Western Slope for residential HVAC, plumbing, and electrical work.

## Finding 3
Pride Plumbing Heating & Cooling, a Grand Junction plumbing and HVAC company, announced May 8, 2026 that it was expanding its commercial plumbing and HVAC service offerings for business customers across the region. The release lists commercial plumbing services including pipe installation and repair, drain cleaning, sewer line maintenance, leak detection and emergency plumbing response, and commercial HVAC work including furnace installation, rooftop unit servicing, air conditioning repair, ventilation system optimization and full HVAC replacements, plus preventive maintenance programs with scheduled inspections, performance evaluations and tune-ups. It says the company targets office buildings, retail spaces, restaurants, warehouses and industrial facilities, positioning around minimizing downtime and energy-efficient systems. This is a company-issued release distributed through EIN Presswire rather than independent reporting, and it is four months old, but it is a direct expansion signal from a home-service competitor operating in Nolan's primary market.

## Finding 4
The Mesa County Board of Commissioners voted to join the West Slope Colorado Oil and Gas Association (WSCOGA) as a co-plaintiff in a lawsuit challenging the Colorado Air Quality Control Commission's February 14 enactment of new greenhouse gas rules for oil and gas midstream facilities, which the county says would require operators of pipelines, compressor stations and other facilities to convert to electric power at prohibitive cost or shut down. Chairman Cody Davis said, 'Mesa County supports clean air, but these new rules ignore the realities of our region,' and that 'one-size-fits-all mandates don't work for western Colorado and could cost our communities jobs, tax revenues and economic stability.' The county's page states no publication date; its text references a May 20 hearing approving participation and its lead image is filed under May 2025. This is economic backdrop rather than a demand signal: it speaks to how much of the Western Slope economy, and the household income behind home-service spending, is tied to energy jobs.
