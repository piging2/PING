## Finding 1
Verizon is raising its outbound A2P SMS surcharge from $0.0045 to $0.005 per message effective October 1, 2026, across 10DLC, standard-rated short code, and toll-free traffic. The change first appears on November invoices, covering October traffic. Inbound (MO) remains $0.00 and MMS rates are unchanged at $0.007 outbound. Tychron's dated updates feed announced this increase as one of two already-announced carrier fee changes still ahead in 2026. Carriers have changed pass-through fees repeatedly this year: T-Mobile and UScellular on January 19, Verizon on May 1 (outbound SMS/MMS plus a $500 short-code migration charge), eighteen regional operators on July 1, three more networks on September 1, and now Verizon again on October 1. For a contractor running SMS follow-up, this is a per-text cost that sits on top of whatever a provider charges per message, and it applies to every registered-text segment sent to Verizon recipients.

## Finding 2
Effective September 1, 2026, three more networks introduced outbound (MT) pass-through fees on 10DLC SMS: Cellular One (NE, AZ) and Nemont (Sagebrush Cellular) at $0.0045 per message in the United States, and Telecommunications Services of Trinidad and Tobago (TSTT) at $0.06 per message, first reflected on October invoices covering September traffic. Inbound (MO) remains $0.00 on those networks. This follows the July 1, 2026 change in which eighteen regional and rural operators, including C Spire, Cellcom, Viaero, Union Wireless, Carolina West, Alaska DigiTel (GCI), and Dish Wireless, introduced or increased pass-through fees on 10DLC and standard-rated short code traffic: SMS at $0.0045 per message in both directions (outbound and inbound), and 10DLC MMS at $0.01 outbound and $0.005 inbound, with Dish Wireless inbound exempt at $0.00 and Appalachian Wireless charging $0.004 on outbound SMS only. The regional carriers matter for Western Colorado prospects because mountain-region networks like Viaero and Union Wireless now charge in both directions. Current baseline per-carrier surcharges on registered 10DLC traffic, per Message Central's 2026 writeup: T-Mobile about $0.003 per segment, AT&T $0.0025 to $0.005, Verizon about $0.003 (pre-October increase), and US Cellular following T-Mobile policy after the merger.

## Finding 3
10DLC registration has been fully mandatory since February 1, 2025: U.S. carriers block A2P traffic on 10-digit long codes that is not registered to an approved campaign, with no grace period and no unregistered rate tier. In 2026, enforcement has shifted to post-approval audits: carriers and The Campaign Registry increasingly review campaigns after approval, and content that drifts from the registered use case can be suspended even if it was approved initially. Clean submissions typically clear carrier review in 24 to 72 hours. Current TCR fees, last verified July 21, 2026: brand registration $4.50 one-time ($4 for sole proprietors), standard vetting $41.50, enhanced vetting $101.50, standard campaign vetting $15 per attempt (resubmissions count as new attempts), and monthly campaign fees of $10 for Standard/Marketing/Mixed, $1.50 for Low-Volume Mixed, $2 for Sole Proprietor, $3 for Charity, and $30 for Agents and Franchises. Any contractor sending texts without a registered campaign is not being delivered at all, and even registered campaigns can lose delivery if their message content does not match the registered use case.

## Finding 4
FCC opt-out rules in effect since April 11, 2025 require senders to honor opt-out requests made by any reasonable means, not just the STOP keyword but natural-language replies too, within 10 business days, with at most one final confirmation message. Telephone Consumer Protection Act damages of $500 to $1,500 per message still apply to a fully registered campaign that texts without consent, per a 10DLC compliance guide verified August 13, 2026. Prospecting context for Nolan: registration protects deliverability and avoids carrier blocking, but it does not protect against TCPA liability; a contractor's follow-up sequences need documented opt-in and opt-out handling that recognizes conversational revocations, not just keywords.
