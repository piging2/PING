## Finding 4
Review options for a single-location cafe that stay cheap and compliant. Google Business Profile itself is free and ranked as the best genuinely free funnel for 2026: the review link and QR code live inside the profile under Read Reviews then Get more reviews, cost nothing, and send the customer straight to the public review prompt; the QR code can currently only be generated in a computer browser, not on a phone. For a two-step funnel, Tally's free plan includes unlimited forms and unlimited submissions within fair use, where the cafe writes its own questions and pastes the Google review link on the thank-you screen; Google Forms is free with a Google account and feeds straight into Sheets. Low-cost paid tools: BrightLocal from 29 dollars per month, Repmanager from 10 dollars per month with a 7-day free trial, Vercepta Silver at 49 dollars per month for up to four platforms and locations with real-time alerts and AI-drafted responses, and Vercepta Gold at 119 dollars per month for up to twelve locations. Compliance notes the cafe must hear: Google prohibits offering incentives such as free or discounted goods for reviews, prohibits gating such as discouraging negative reviews or selectively soliciting only happy customers, and prohibits pressuring customers to leave a rating while in the shop; the FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465, effective October 21, 2024, bans reviews from non-customers, undisclosed insider reviews, and compensation tied to sentiment, with civil penalties for knowing violations. Operational play: ask soon after the interaction while it is fresh, ask everyone for an honest review with no discount or gift, respond to all reviews within 24 hours and within one hour for negatives, and treat recency as the metric because BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 83 percent of consumers who were asked left a review, 97 percent read reviews for local businesses, and 74 percent give most weight to reviews written in the last three months.
