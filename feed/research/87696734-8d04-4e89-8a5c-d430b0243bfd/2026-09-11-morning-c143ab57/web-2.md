## Finding 2
Mesa County Public Health reports wildfire smoke from nearby fires is impacting air quality in Mesa County. The Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment has issued an Air Quality Alert for parts of the Western Slope. The county urges caution outdoors, especially for people with heart or respiratory conditions, the very young, and the elderly, and advises limiting outdoor activity during moderate to heavy smoke, limiting use of evaporative coolers, and using air filters to reduce indoor particulates. A No Burn Advisory remains in effect for Mesa County, and burning of any kind including agricultural burning is not permitted during an advisory. In a separate update, Mesa County Public Health says it is delaying the start of the fall open burn season due to ongoing fire restrictions and unsafe weather conditions. Under normal circumstances the fall season would be September 1 to September 30 for City of Grand Junction residents and September 1 to October 31 for Mesa County residents outside city limits. The county remains under Stage Two Fire Restrictions issued by the Mesa County Sheriff's Office in June 2026, with the No Burn Advisory in place since June 2026 due to dry and hot conditions. Burn permits will not be issued until the No Burn Advisory is lifted. The agency says it is monitoring week by week and will update via website and social media when it is safe to begin burning or obtain a permit. Questions go to 970-248-6900.

## Finding 3
Fall HVAC and plumbing checklists point to early September as the service window before heating season. A USA Today end-of-summer home checklist advises that if homeowners do one thing, it should be getting the HVAC system serviced: in fall a technician will clean up and winterize the cooling system and get the heating system ready, catching minor repairs before they become costly breakdowns or emergency replacements. Oncourse Home Solutions General HVAC and Plumbing Manager Elizabeth Shavers recommends replacing the HVAC filter after a summer of running the AC, saying dirty filters restrict airflow and make the system work harder. She also recommends a test run of the furnace now, saying not to wait until the first freezing night to find out something is not working, and to watch for unusual noises, weak airflow, or anything else that seems off. For plumbing, the checklist advises winterizing outdoor faucets by removing and draining garden hoses, turning off the water source to the outdoor faucet, and draining any remaining water. Related seasonal items in the same checklist include cleaning gutters as leaves fall, sealing air leaks around windows and doors, and testing smoke and carbon monoxide detectors before heating systems run more frequently. The page states no publication date in its body text.
