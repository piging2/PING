## Finding 1
Heather Cooper posted a screenshot of Cooper Climate Control's Google Reviews page on September 10, 2026, showing a 5.0-star rating across 571 reviews, built around a same-day customer story. The highlighted review is from Kevin Raine, who says another company quoted him $2,100 to fix his HVAC, then he met Cooper's owner by chance; a Cooper technician arrived within three hours, bought the part from a local home improvement store, and finished the repair for less than one-quarter of the original quote, closing with 'Congrats Cooper, you just won my loyalty.' Cooper's caption says she overheard the conversation, confirmed the part was available with her colleague Wesley, and frames the post as pride in her team six years after starting the business. The post drew 150 likes and 12 comments, the warmest reception in this sweep; the platform's comment summary describes the replies as uniformly affectionate approval, typified by 'I love this!', with no debate or technical discussion. The account has about 1,300 followers. This is the clearest job-proof format that converted into conversation: a specific customer story with named people, a price contrast, and a same-day timeline, where the comments that sparked were short emotional endorsements rather than questions.

## Finding 2
HVAC technician Terrell Powell (@creatinet1, about 22,000 followers) posted a time-lapse furnace replacement reel on September 10, 2026, captioned 'Sold it, installed it.' The video opens on corroded ductwork with on-screen text 'Sold the job. Now I gotta install it,' then shows him removing the old furnace, setting a new dark gray Goodman furnace, cutting new round sheet-metal ductwork, and finishing with the completed install. It closes by asking viewers 'Team install or service?' The reel earned 590 likes and 29 comments, the highest comment count found in this sweep. The takeaway on comment styles: the only posts that generated real reply threads ended with a direct question or a customer tag. Powell's 'Team install or service?' prompt invited technicians to pick a side, while a parallel example, Air Giant's September 10 Facebook post thanking customer Mrs. Trish Procell of Many, Louisiana for an Amana condenser install, drew a direct reply from Tony Procell saying 'We love it!!' Posts without a question or a tagged customer, including nearly all the fall tune-up graphics, drew likes but almost no comments.
