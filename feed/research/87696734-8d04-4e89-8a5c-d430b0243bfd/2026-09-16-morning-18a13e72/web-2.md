## Finding 1
Callacy AI announced the launch of its AI front desk platform on September 16, 2026, a business communications and automation software company that now markets an always-on AI front desk aimed at small and growing businesses. The platform combines AI call answering with a natural-sounding voice, lead qualification, appointment and job booking, text messaging, social communication automation, quote and invoice creation, and automated follow-ups in a single workflow. ## Finding 2
Orbits, a Toronto and San Francisco startup, announced September 16, 2026 that it raised nearly $2 million in pre-seed funding to build Bit, an AI household assistant, with backing from Andreessen Horowitz's a16z Speedrun fund, N49P, Garage Capital, and angel investors including a team member at Anthropic. Bit is designed to take actions on the homeowner's behalf: it can browse websites, log into authorized accounts, book reservations, reschedule appointments, and collect quotes from service providers for jobs such as roofing or HVAC repairs. Co-founder and CEO Nomaan Ahmed (with CTO Erik Tillberg, previously an AI researcher at the University of Toronto) said the goal is a household manager every family can afford. The funding goes toward infrastructure, hiring, and expanding beyond its initial group of families; the app is available for iOS and Android. Playbook relevance: consumer-side agentic AI that will start calling and emailing contractors to collect quotes on behalf of homeowners, a new inbound channel home-service businesses will have to handle.

## Finding 3
Crewscope launched its AI-native field intelligence platform on September 15, 2026 in Toronto, and completed a SOC 2 Type II audit with no exceptions. The platform is built for construction, industrial, and mining sites: workers text, photograph, or voice-note a dedicated site AI coordinator with no app and no login, and the AI captures, classifies, routes, and closes the loop on field observations, chasing missing information from the right people. It integrates with Procore, Google Docs, Fieldwire, Trimble, and other project management systems. Crewscope claims 10x ROI across deployments, 100% jobsite adoption in 8 weeks at UHN Toronto Western Hospital with zero permit stoppages, and 6+ hours saved per site leader weekly. It was built and proven with EllisDon site teams over three years; Chris Masse, General Superintendent at EllisDon, said clients are starting to ask for this and it is part of their bid. CEO is Calvin Benchimol, CTO is Ehsan Foroughi; two patents are pending for industrial forecasting and a multi-agent privacy architecture. Available now at crewscope.com. Playbook relevance: a proven model of AI-native field workflows and a benchmark for how trades accept AI that fits how they already work.

## Finding 4
David Kohlani, founder of ApexSmartAI (a 24/7 bilingual English-Spanish answering service for U.S. HVAC, plumbing and home service companies), published a study on Medium about one day ago measuring missed-call complaints in Google reviews. He reviewed 59 currently trading home-service companies in San Antonio, Texas (HVAC, plumbing, related trades, local phone numbers, no national franchises), collecting up to 20 reviews per company sorted lowest rating first, 1,172 reviews total, 1,068 with written text and 501 at three stars or fewer. Twenty-five reviews said plainly that the customer called and nobody answered or called back; 14 of the 59 companies, roughly one in four, had at least one such review on their public Google Business Profile, and for 8 of the 59 that review was written within the last 18 months. Every match was read by hand before being counted. His stated limits: he counted reviews, not missed calls, and anyone quoting a dollar figure for a missed call from this data invented it. Key patterns: timing (after-hours emergency calls, some listings say open 24 hours but do not answer at 2am) and language (English-only voicemail in a heavily bilingual market). He argues the missed-call review is the only bad review written by somebody who never became a customer, which makes it an access problem rather than a service problem, and advises reading your own one-star reviews by type, calling your own number after hours, and checking what happens when both lines are busy. Playbook relevance: an honest, citable data point for review-triggered outreach pitches.
