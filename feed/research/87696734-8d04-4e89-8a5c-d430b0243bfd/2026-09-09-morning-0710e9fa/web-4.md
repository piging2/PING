## Finding 1
The Federal Communications Commission proposed a Robocall Scorecard to rate how well voice providers protect consumers from illegal robocalls. FOX Business reported the proposal as an exclusive after the FCC released a public notice on a Wednesday seeking comment on the scorecard's design principles, including that it be easy to understand and accessible to consumers, with relevant and accurate rating criteria and an iterative improvement process. FCC Chairman Brendan Carr said, "Combatting the scourge of illegal robocalls remains the FCC's top consumer protection priority." Carr said the scorecard would give consumers "more information about the measures providers are taking to fight illegal robocalls" and would "incentivize providers to improve their efforts." The FCC proposes to rate only domestic voice service providers with retail customers. It outlined two metric categories. Conduct-based metrics would assess whether a provider took specific mitigation steps, such as offering consumer tools to label or block calls and responding to traceback requests. Outcome-based metrics would measure whether those efforts actually reduced illegal robocalls reaching consumers, using consumer complaints filed with the FCC and other federal agencies, aggregated data on calls blocked and the false positive rate for legitimate calls mistakenly blocked, the percentage of illegal robocalls received, and trend data over time drawn from third-party analytics where possible. ## Finding 2
On August 31, 2026, the final day of its 2026 session, the California Legislature passed SB 1050, a bill requiring disclosure for advertisements prominently featuring AI-generated synthetic performers, and sent it to Governor Newsom. The bill is strongly supported by SAG-AFTRA. It would add a new article to the Business and Professions Code covering a synthetic performer defined as "a digital figure, voice, or representation created in whole or in part using generative artificial intelligence that creates the realistic impression of the audio, audiovisual, or visual performance of a human performer who is not recognizable as any identifiable natural person." Disclosure is required when the synthetic performer is featured prominently, meaning it appears in the foreground while demonstrating or illustrating a product or service, or provides on- or off-camera narration or the commercial message itself. The article lists acceptable disclosure language such as "this performance features a synthetic performer" or "no human performer is depicted." Failure to disclose would expose advertisers to claims under California's False Advertising Law (Bus. & Prof. Code, section 17500) and Unfair Competition Law (Bus. & Prof. Code, section 17200). The analysis contrasts New York's earlier law, which generally requires a conspicuous disclosure whenever an advertiser with actual knowledge uses a synthetic performer and enforces it through statutory civil penalties, with express exemptions including for audio-only advertisements. California's bill expressly encompasses AI-generated voices, so compliance in one state would not satisfy the other.
