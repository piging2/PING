Beat: social
Angle: Fresh public conversation from the last couple of days among home-service owners and contractors about AI receptionists and automation: new posts, threads, wins, and objections. The paper covered the missed-call-text-back thread last night, so bring only conversations that postdate it, with the sentiment and objections Nolan can use in pitches.

## Finding 1
An articulate objection wave to AI receptionists surfaced September 7-8 from people adjacent to the trade world, and it is the sentiment Nolan will hit in pitches. UK builder Andrew (@andrewbuildsit7, Instagram, September 7) calls the standard pitch backwards: 'AI receptionists are being sold like every small business should replace the phone tomorrow,' arguing a human caller conveys uncertainty, humor, frustration and local accent, so AI belongs only where the alternative is silence, never as a replacement for a good human conversation. Guileo.AI (Instagram, September 7) posted a cautionary rule from a real call: an AI told a caller a spa offered nail services it did not offer, and an angry customer showed up at the counter; the rule is 'if it is not written down, it does not exist.' PM Consulting (Facebook, September 8), a marketing agency that sells to contractors, trashes the current wave as 'garbage' and 'polite robots' that cannot tell a burst pipe from a quote request, saying owners try them, they botch three calls, and get shut off; it also attacks pricing, claiming services advertise $200/month but bills land near $800 after setup fees, minutes and calendar integration. Consumer-side hostility is running in parallel: a Peanuts comic in the Facebook group 'thesnoopyfamily' (September 7, 3.7K likes, 270 comments) with Charlie Brown shouting 'GIVE ME A HUMAN BEING WHO ANSWERS ON THE SECOND RING' drew hundreds of comments venting about AI phone systems and automated answering, the dominant tone exasperation with a longing for live support. The pitch takeaway: lead with 'silence replacement, not staff replacement,' written guardrails, instant human handoff, and transparent pricing.

## Finding 2
Two builder-side posts this week give Nolan counter-ammunition to the objections. Devvrath Shishodia (@devbuildsai, 34K followers, Instagram reel September 7, 110 comments) built a $1,500 AI agent for Phoenix HVAC companies after cold-calling them and finding nobody answered after 6 p.m.; he states it is built on Twilio, Claude and Make.com, has run in production for seven months serving two HVAC companies, answers 24/7, transfers emergencies to humans, logs transcripts and books appointments, and he frames the economics against client feedback that one emergency call in Phoenix costs $400 to $1,200 while the agent costs a fraction. Meanwhile @cyrifusion (Cyri Talks Tech, Instagram carousel posted September 6) ran a buyer-protection argument aimed directly at the 'customers will hate talking to a robot' objection: callers dislike silence more than a bot, blind tests show most callers cannot spot a well-built AI, and outcomes depend on setup quality, not the technology; he cites survey data that 64% of consumers prefer no AI in customer service and 53% have considered switching, using it to argue proper implementation is a competitive moat, and lays out three trust rules: disclose the assistant, offer an instant human path, and escalate emotional calls. Both posts show owners debating, not just vendors broadcasting.

## Finding 3
Three fresh owner-side win stories landed in the last 48 hours, all built on missed calls on the job. An arborist posting via @nomissedcall.app (Instagram reel, September 8) recounts being 'forty feet up an oak' the Tuesday after a big storm, coming down to a voicemail box that was full and could not take messages, with nineteen missed calls, half of whom had already hired someone else; he now uses the No Missed Call service ($39/month after trial) so calls do not go to voicemail. A plumber testimonial for Plumber Secretary (Facebook reel, September 8) says he counted the calls that rang out in a week, calculated the lost revenue from missed jobs like water heater swaps, and 'ran the numbers twice because the first result seemed like a typo' before having 'Bella' answer every call. RetargetLEAD (Instagram reel, September 7) includes a testimonial from Mike R., owner of RidgeLine Roofing, citing $27,600 in additional revenue, 18-plus hours saved weekly, and a 5.0 out of 5 rating after one month, with the service at $45 per week; the video opens by addressing roofer skepticism directly ('AI? No way.'). These are usable pitch proof points because the owners name the concrete before-state, not just the after.

## Finding 4
The sharpest ROI funnel numbers of the week came from @phrip_lea (Enoma Efosa David, Instagram, September 9), who posted a call-funnel dashboard for one HVAC company over 30 days: 387 total inbound calls, 241 answered during business hours, 146 after-hours calls that would previously have been lost, all 146 answered by AI, producing 89 qualified leads, 54 bookings, 7 cancellations and 47 completed jobs at an average value of $1,180, for $55,460 in revenue against $297 to run the AI. The post's headline claim: for every $1 spent on voice AI, $186 came back in revenue. The visual shows 146 total calls, 93 qualified, 54 booked, 47 completed on the dashboard. It is a vendor-style post, but the funnel is itemized per stage, which makes it quotable in a pitch against the 'polite robot' objection.
