## Finding 1
Lead-generation costs for home services keep climbing, per two fresh datasets published September 4 and September 6, 2026. 99 Calls' year-over-year comparison of Google Local Services Ads costs across its managed campaigns shows average cost per charged lead up 11 to 69 percent by trade: appliance repair rose from $20.33 to $34.35 (up 69 percent), junk removal from $19.85 to $33.14, landscaping from $22.67 to $37.29, electrical from $28.58 to $43.15, roofing from $73.62 to $106.87, and water damage from $171.64 to $215.31. Painting was effectively flat at about $33 and plumbing fell 5.6 percent to $66.17. Separately, pay-per-call network Lead Smart Inc published its September 2026 payout file covering 296,513 live ZIP-by-service records across 41,259 U.S. ZIP codes, 28 verticals and 53 state codes, with data as of September 5, 2026: the median qualified phone lead pays $42.00 to the publisher, up from $32.50 in the network's August file, the mean is $71.71, the top decile pays above $189.50, and the ceiling set a new high at $507.00. Of the published records, 66,053 pay $100 or more per call and 22,870 pay $200 or more. Notable moves: solar cost-per-lead went live in 496 ZIP codes at a $73.50 median (the first vertical added to the public file this year), personal injury attorney coverage expanded from 344 to 963 ZIP codes with the median rising from $50.33 to $99.00, water damage set a new $507.00 ceiling with the median rising from $201.00 to $213.00, and appliance repair repriced hardest with its median more than doubling from $6.33 to $14.00 while coverage nearly halved from 25,573 to 13,568 ZIP codes. The release also cites Invoca's 2026 Home Services Lead Conversion Benchmarks Report, built on more than 70 million analyzed calls: 38 percent of home-service calls answered by a person are genuine leads and 45 percent of those convert on the call, and calls referred by ChatGPT carry the highest phone-lead rate of any channel at 45 percent, about five points above paid search. The figures are stated as minimum expected payouts, exclude buyer-side reconciliations, and the press release is marked as third-party content on the carrying site.

## Finding 2
Google is folding Local Services Ads into Performance Max, its automated Google Ads campaign type, and advertisers get an audit checklist to prepare. The first U.S. advertisers moved in August 2026, and the rest follow on migration dates Google assigns through 2027. The ads themselves stay put: the same listings, the same Google Guaranteed badge, and the same pay-per-lead model. What changes is how ad spend is counted, how bids are set (Google bids automatically instead of the advertiser setting their own bid), where historical reports live, and where the campaign is managed day to day. One part of account history does not transfer at all, so the only window to save it is before the account moves; the checklist covers pre-migration exports of reports and settings, cost-per-lead checks for where CPL could move once Google sets the bid, and an agency playbook for keeping client accounts profitable when migration dates land months apart. Search Engine Journal ran a free webinar on September 8, 2026 with Meagan McLoughlin, Director of Product Marketing at CallRail, walking through the audit in order, including how to read where an account sits in the migration queue. The page states no publication date; search metadata placed it about 12 days ago.

## Finding 3
Google Business Profile has a new 'What's Happening' section that lets businesses post offers, events, and updates directly onto the profile as it appears on Google Search and Maps, a free and prominent advertising slot reported by PageLaunch on September 6, 2026 (spotted via a video from GBP Wizards). Examples the piece gives: '10% off boiler services in October,' 'Last-minute garden tidy-up slots available this week,' and bank-holiday closure notices. The post takes about five minutes in the Business Profile dashboard; 'Updates' suit general news and 'Offers' suit dated deals, and the piece recommends a specific claim, a time limit, and a call to action. Beyond the noticeboard angle, the piece notes posted content feeds Google's AI answer systems: Google has been replacing the manual 'ask a question' button on profiles with an AI called 'Ask Maps' that scans the profile, website, and reviews to answer queries directly, so a 'What's Happening' post is prime material when someone asks Google which nearby plumber has a special offer. The same piece folds in two other 2026 GBP additions worth knowing: native post scheduling (previously needed a third-party tool) and a verified badge, plus a WhatsApp contact button replacing the removed native chat, AI-drafted review reply suggestions in testing, and email alerts when a review restriction is lifted.
- https://pagelaunch.co.uk/blog/google-business-profile-offers-updates-feature-uk-trades/ (published 2026-09-06)

## Finding 4
Podium formally announced its AI Operating System for Home Services, a single platform combining AI agents with field service management, communications, and membership tools, available for HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and garage door contractors in the U.S. and Canada. The system has been live since early 2026 and the release says hundreds of contractors have already migrated from legacy field service management software, going live in weeks rather than months with a dedicated onboarding team and migration partner. Three AI agents are named: Larry, which answers every call, qualifies the lead, checks availability, and books the confirmed appointment on the dispatch board; an AI Membership Coordinator that works through the membership list and books seasonal visits; and Operator, an AI business manager that works across jobs, invoices, and schedules. Contractor results cited: Air Design Heating and Cooling worked through 600 maintenance members with a 40 percent booking rate and collected $35K in revenue without a single outreach call; Magnolia Plumbing cut invoice-to-payment time in half and freed up 60 hours of admin time per month; Mountain Valley Home Comfort migrated 20,000 customers and 5,000 maintenance agreements and now runs daily reporting through Operator; Carlsbad Heating and Cooling consolidated four platforms into one with every call covered after hours without adding staff. CEO Eric Rea is quoted: 'When you build the system around agents instead of workflows, the business runs differently. The agents go do the work.' The carrying page is a PRNewswire press release that states no publication date; search metadata placed it about 12 to 13 days ago.
