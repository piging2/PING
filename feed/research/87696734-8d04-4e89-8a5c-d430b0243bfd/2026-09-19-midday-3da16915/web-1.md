## Finding 1
Montrose Forest Products, the sawmill at 11925 6530 Rd in Montrose owned by Neiman Enterprises, is up for sale and jobs are on the line. The report, published two days ago, says Neiman issued WARN notices affecting around 90 employees on September 8, with November 6 as the last day on the notices. The mill is still operating. A report from the Montrose Business Times, cited in the article, says Neiman works with eight independent logging contractors representing about 50 people, and only two contractors are currently working with the mill. Neiman bought the mill out of receivership in 2012 and has invested more than $60 million in upgrades over 14 years. A 2020 Montrose EDC report indicated contracted labor supported more than 250 jobs at the mill and nearly $30 million in annual payroll. If the mill closes, timber removal through local forest-management projects in Montrose County gets harder and more expensive.

## Finding 2
Mesa County commissioners approved a $4.6 million contract for phase two of roadway and safety improvements along 32 1/2 Road in Clifton, published September 16, 2026. The contract was approved at a public hearing on Tuesday, September 15, and awarded to Mountain Valley Contracting, Inc. after a competitive bidding process that drew responses from seven contractors. Construction is slated to begin in late September or early October and is expected to be completed by the end of 2027. Phase II covers 32 1/2 Road from the north side of the Grand Valley Canal to Front Street and includes a full roadway rebuild and widening, new sidewalks with accessible curb ramps, enhanced drainage infrastructure, and a new roundabout at 32 1/2 Road and E 1/2 Road. The roundabout decision stems from a high number of broadside crashes at the current two-way stop; county officials say roundabouts have been shown to reduce overall crash rates by 70% and fatal and injury crashes by more than 80%.

## Finding 3
Grand Junction's new Community Recreation Center at 2836 Recreation Way opens with a free weekend later this month, per a report published two days ago. The ribbon-cutting is followed by free public use from 5 to 8 p.m. Friday, September 25, and again from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Saturday, September 26, with no pass needed. Saturday pool access is first-come, first-served across three two-hour swim sessions: 9 to 11 a.m., 11:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m., and 2 to 4 p.m. Regular operations begin Monday, September 28, at 5:30 a.m. with normal admission and membership rules. Pricing after opening weekend: a city-resident adult pass is $42 per month, a 20-punch pass costs $162, and a single-day pass is $9; the city will also accept SilverSneakers and Renew Active. The facility includes the five St. Mary's Pools with water features, a three-court gymnasium, and climbing walls. The page states no publication date; the dates above are as the article states them.

## Finding 4
Graff Dairy on 29 Road in Grand Junction has new owners: Jordan and Maddison Burge officially took ownership on September 1, 2026, after previous owner Darin Carei put the business up for sale in March. The report, which surfaced four days ago, says the Burges plan earlier 7 a.m. openings with a new coffee program including coffee, refreshers, and Red Bull-infused drinks, plus self-ordering kiosks and new screens (customers can still order from employees). They bought the business, not the building: Darin Carei still owns the real estate. The plan keeps the vintage look and nostalgic experience, including the vintage cars, while the menu gains new items. The store's Facebook page is soliciting menu feedback.
