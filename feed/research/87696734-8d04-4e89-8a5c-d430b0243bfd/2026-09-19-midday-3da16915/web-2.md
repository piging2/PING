## Finding 1
Google's AI is now calling home service businesses on behalf of homeowners, and for a contractor this changes what the front desk has to win. Google announced the feature at I/O in May 2026 and rolled it out across the United States over the summer for home repair, beauty, and pet care. A homeowner searches, taps an option like 'have AI check pricing,' and Google's system places real phone calls to the businesses in that search using the number on their Google Business Profile. The AI identifies itself as an automated system calling for a customer, asks the standard questions a homeowner would ask (can you do the job, when are you available, roughly what does it cost), then sends the homeowner a text with a side-by-side comparison of the businesses it reached plus a link to book. The part most owners miss: businesses are opted in automatically, with no signup and no notification before the first call. A missed call from Google's AI looks exactly like any other missed call in the call log, so phone-coverage gaps become invisible losses. Turning it off is done in Google Business Profile under Business Profile settings, then Advanced settings, in the section labeled Google automated calls and text messages. Digital Footprint Solutions frames the bottom line as: answering fast with a real price and real availability is now what wins the comparison, and an AI receptionist that picks up in one or two rings with current pricing on hand is positioned as the way to never lose one of these calls. The article itself appears to be from about five days before the reading day, but the page states no explicit publication date.

## Finding 2
A September 2026 comparison of Smith.ai and its alternatives lays out current contractor-focused pricing in one table. Smith.ai AI Pro is $150 a month for 75 bot-first calls, with a North America human available for transfer; about a quarter of AI-first calls still involve a person, and live-agent plans start at $300 a month for 30 calls with $11.50 per extra call. The alternatives priced for shops: Rosie at $49 a month for solo 24/7 pickup (the $49 plan does not book, Scale at $149 adds calendar booking), Frontdesk at $99 a month aimed at Housecall Pro plumbers and HVAC for AI-only after-hours coverage, OnCrew at $49 a month for 1 to 15 truck HVAC and plumbing shops with urgent on-call text to the tech, Dialzara at $29 Lite or $99 Pro (Pro is the first plan that books), and Voksha from $14 Starter to $99 Premium (booking waits for Premium). The piece's guidance: stay on Smith.ai when a missed call is a missed emergency job and you will cap transfers; switch to Ruby at $250 a month for 50 minutes of a live US person when a human must greet; use Rosie, Frontdesk, or OnCrew for cheap home-service night coverage; and treat missed-call text-back as a different aisle from receptionist answering, worth choosing on its own when the job is recovering voicemail rather than answering the ring.

## Finding 3
A 2026 pricing comparison of five AI receptionist services, last verified in September 2026, shows how much entry pricing moved this year. Smith.ai now has a free AI tier at $0 a month for 25 calls with $3.00 per extra call; its AI Pro runs from $150 a month for 75 to 300 calls. Voksha dropped its entry plan to $14 a month for 15 calls with $1 per call after that, and its Premium is $99 a month for 150 calls; Voksha also reported more than one million calls handled since December 2025, one in three not in English. Goodcall charges per unique caller rather than per minute, $79 a month ($66 billed annually) for 100 unique customers, which favors shops with lots of repeat callers. My AI Front Desk is $99 a month ($79 annual) and is scheduling-first with a BAA option for healthcare accounts. Rosie AI is $49 a month for 250 minutes, with Scale at $149 adding warm and live call transfers plus calendar integration, and Growth at $299 for 2,000 minutes with waterfall transfers; Rosie does not publish its overage rate. The piece warns that overage rules are the silent budget killer in the category, and it advises measuring your own baseline of missed new-customer calls and average first-order value rather than budgeting on vendor marketing claims.

## Finding 4
A newsletter roundup from about four days before the reading day pulls together the cost math that is making AI receptionists mainstream for shops. Citing Vendasta's 2026 guide on AI receptionists, it states small businesses miss 60 to 80 percent of incoming calls, and for contractors specifically that costs $16,000 to $250,000-plus a year in lost business. Vendasta reports AI receptionists now resolve 90 to 95 percent of calls without a human. NextPhone's 2026 guide lists AI receptionist apps starting around $199 a month, against $2,800-plus a month for a human hire, roughly 7 percent of the cost of a person. A September 2026 thread on Reddit's r/Entrepreneur asking 'Has anyone used an AI receptionist for a small business?' pulled 172 comments, which the piece reads as adoption moving past the curiosity phase into 'does this actually work' territory. The piece also notes real pushback from inside the automation community about whether small businesses are operationally ready for agent-style workflows, which matters for a shop deciding whether to pilot or go all in.
