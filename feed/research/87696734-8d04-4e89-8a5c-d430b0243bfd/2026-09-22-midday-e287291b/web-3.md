## Finding 1
The Mesa County Board of Commissioners unanimously approved ballot measure 1A, which goes to voters this November to fund the long-planned 29 Road interchange connecting 29 Road directly to Interstate 70. The measure allows the county and the City of Grand Junction to bond for up to $80 million without raising taxes; funding comes from the county's existing sales tax revenues. The project expands 29 Road between Patterson and I-70 from two to four lanes and adds pedestrian and bike paths. About $12 million covers extending and modifying 29 Road north of Patterson Road and $68 million builds the interchange itself, with a stated repayment cost of $173,438,202. If voters approve, final design begins. Commissioner Cody Davis said data shows the interchange will reduce greenhouse gases, increase miles traveled, and reduce hours on the road; Commissioners Bobbie Daniel and Janet Rowland emphasized it completes a transportation loop planned since the 1950s to 1980s without taking agricultural land or water. The county's news page carried this within the last day.

## Finding 3
Graff Dairy, the ice cream and burger drive-in on 29 Road in Grand Junction, changed hands on Sept. 1, 2026: Jordan and Maddison Burge bought the business from Darin Carei, who had listed it for sale in March and retains ownership of the real estate. The new owners plan to keep the nostalgic look and vintage-car atmosphere while adding coffee, refreshers, and Red Bull-infused drinks, earlier 7 a.m. openings, and self-ordering kiosks with new screens, with customers still able to order from employees. ## Finding 4
Mesa County commissioners unanimously approved renewing the county landfill's mattress recycling contract with Spring Back Colorado, the only responsive bidder after a public request for proposals. The program, running just over four years, has recycled about 50 to 60 percent of mattresses received, preserving an estimated 34,000 cubic yards of landfill airspace and saving roughly $300,000 net compared with landfilling. Spring Back Colorado has served Colorado landfills since 2011. Residents pay a $20 per-item landfill disposal fee ($40 for a mattress and box spring together); soiled or bedbug-contaminated mattresses still go to landfill. The county's news page carried this within the last day.
