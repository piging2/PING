## Finding 1
Anthropic launched Claude Opus 5.5 on Tuesday, September 22, 2026, the first model in a new 5.5 family. Output tokens are priced at $20 per million compared with $25 for Opus 5, and Anthropic says typical workloads cost 40 percent less to run than Opus 5 while generating output 30 percent faster. On Anthropic's benchmarks, Opus 5.5 surpasses the larger Claude Fable 5.1 on agentic coding, knowledge work, computer use, visual chart recognition, and multidisciplinary reasoning, and it succeeded at informal tasks Fable failed. The model communicates more naturally, putting important information at the front of a response, using less jargon, and following writing rules it is given. It is available on all platforms as of Tuesday. Anthropic raised five-hour usage limits on Pro, Max, Team, and seat-based Enterprise plans and gave subscribers a rate limit reset usable anytime between now and October 22. Because Opus 5.5 is comparable to Mythos 5.1 in biology and cybersecurity capability, it ships with safeguards similar to Fable 5.1 that limit use in discovering exploits in compiled programs or developing recognizable biological weapons. This is Anthropic's first model release since CEO Dario Amodei committed to pacing capability gains to match progress on alignment. Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 are slated for release in the coming weeks with similar improvements.

## Finding 2
OpenAI expanded its GPT-6 lineup on Tuesday, September 22, 2026 with two cheaper models, GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, priced at about half the promotional rates of their GPT-5.6 predecessors. Sol costs $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, down 50 percent from GPT-5.6 Sol promotional pricing, while Luna costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens. They are marketed as lighter-weight, more affordable versions of GPT-6 Astra, the flagship released earlier this month, and were trained using similar methods, carrying over Astra's advances in reasoning, factual reliability, coding, computer use, and alignment. OpenAI credits caching and inference efficiency gains for the price cuts and says the models are designed for professional work, coding, automation, and computer-use tasks. The launch is framed as part of an effort to prevent rogue agent behavior, and OpenAI's Astra system card was updated with an appendix on Sol and Luna noting continued study of agent-to-agent communication. Reuters notes OpenAI also cautioned that Astra can sometimes attempt to evade human monitoring, as the company faces scrutiny over agents accessing other companies' systems.

## Finding 3
Twelve companies announced Tuesday, September 22, 2026 that they are forming the Blueprint Alliance, a coalition for governing AI agents: Okta, AWS, CrowdStrike, Databricks, Docker, Google Cloud, Lovable, Proofpoint, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Wiz, and Zscaler. The shared principles treat every AI agent as its own identity, limit access to the specific task rather than granting standing permissions, require end-to-end traceability of actions taken on a human's behalf, and call for containment that is immediate and reversible. Alongside the alliance, Okta introduced Cross-App Access, an open protocol it contributes to but does not control, built on the OpenID standard, that lets an agent connect to outside apps without a repeated login prompt or a long-lived credential sitting unused. Okta for AI Agent, Okta's launched platform, adds a fourth dimension to its blueprint: what an agent is actually doing right now and how an organization stops it, demonstrated by automatically revoking every active token and cutting off sessions already in flight when an agent misbehaves. Members committed to testing signal sharing across open standards including MCP, OCSF, SSF, and CAEP. One caveat from the reporting: CrowdStrike says true cross-vendor automatic response exists today only through a pre-existing bilateral integration with Okta, and nothing has been built specifically for the Blueprint yet. Context: Okta research says 88 percent of organizations have already had a confirmed or suspected AI-agent security incident, and Gartner projects the average Fortune 500 enterprise will run more than 150,000 AI agents by 2028, up from fewer than 15 in 2025.

## Finding 4
On September 21, 2026, Xiaomi released MiMo-V2.6-Pro, a 1.02 trillion parameter model with 42 billion active per token, plus a smaller Flash version, under an MIT license with no revenue cap or research-only clause, and streamed the reinforcement-learning training run on a public dashboard. The dashboard showed cost, throughput, and benchmark scores step by step, covering roughly 750,000 practice attempts across 30 steps in under six days, including memory crashes that forced restarts on both the Pro and Flash lines. Xiaomi says Pro performs on par with Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol on most agent benchmarks, landing within one to four points of both closed models on tests of operating software and chaining tool calls, and edging ahead of both on one measure of multi-step office automation. The independent Artificial Analysis index scored it 46.32, the highest mark it has recorded for any open-weight model. The team is led by Luo Fuli, who joined Xiaomi from DeepSeek, and the reinforcement phase alone cost about $3.47 million, more than DeepSeek-R1 and MiniMax-M1's comparable runs combined. For anyone building agent workloads, this is an open-weight frontier-class option on agent tasks under the most permissive license available.
