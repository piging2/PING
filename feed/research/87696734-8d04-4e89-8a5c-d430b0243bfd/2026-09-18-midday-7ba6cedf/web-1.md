## Finding 2
The rules at issue implement two laws, both effective January 1, 2027: Senate Bill 26-189 (the Automated Decision-Making Technology Act) and House Bill 26-1263 (the Chatbot Safety Act). The Chatbot Safety Act portion directly touches Nolan's business: the proposed rules would require operators of conversational AI services to protect users and submit annual reports to the Colorado Attorney General. The AG is still seeking input on the open definitional fights: how to determine when an AI output 'materially influences' a consequential decision (two potential standards are on the table), how responsibilities split when deployers rely on AI operated by third-party vendors, and how much AI developers must disclose about training data, known limitations, risks, and testing.

## Finding 3
The small-business exemption did not survive into the final 2026 law, and this piece, crawled within the last day, is the freshest confirmation. Under the original 2024 AI Act, deployers with fewer than 50 full-time employees who did not train the AI system on their own data were exempt from most obligations. The enrolled text of S.B. 26-189 contains no employee-count exemption at all; the only carve-outs are for HIPAA-covered entities, FDA-regulated medical devices, creditors already complying with the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, FERPA-covered education deployers, and certain separately regulated insurers. That means a three-person shop running an AI-driven customer tool sits inside the law's scope the same as a 3,000-person company. Enforcement runs through the Attorney General alone under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act, there is no private right of action, most first violations get a 60-day cure period, and the AG has said enforcement will not begin in earnest until rulemaking is complete, which is due January 1, 2027. ## Finding 4
SB 26-189 closes the human-review escape hatch other states left open. The law applies whenever a tool's output is a non-de-minimis factor in a consequential decision, even when a human makes the final call; tools used solely to summarize, organize, translate, draft, route, or present information for human review are generally excluded, and ordinary marketing, advertising, scheduling, customer-service triage, and fraud-prevention tools are excluded outright. Deployer duties under the proposed rules: clear and conspicuous pre-use notice with instructions for getting more information; within 30 days of an adverse outcome (rejection, denial of interview, materially less favorable offer), a plain-language description of the decision and the tool's role in it, instructions for obtaining more detail, and an explanation of the consumer's rights; meaningful human review on request, meaning a specifically designated, trained reviewer with real authority to approve, modify, or override, who does not simply defer to the system's output; and records demonstrating compliance kept for at least three years.
