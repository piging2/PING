## Finding 1
Grand Junction's new Community Recreation Center holds its grand opening Friday, September 25, 2026, following an official ribbon-cutting. The 107,000-square-foot facility near Matchette Park at Patterson Road and 28 1/4 Road includes five St. Mary's pools with water features, a three-court gymnasium, and climbing walls; the project grew from a planned 83,000 square feet with funding from St. Mary's Intermountain Health and the Colorado Department of Local Affairs. Admission is free for Grand Junction residents from 5 to 8 p.m. Friday, September 25, and from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Saturday, September 26, with regular operating hours beginning Monday, September 28, at 5:30 a.m. Adult day passes are $9, youth $6, seniors $7, with Mesa County residents and visitors paying higher rates. The City of Grand Junction also hired approximately 150 part-time employees across roles including lifeguards, swim instructors, fitness instructors, guest-service representatives, and custodians, according to a Business Times report; the render of that report states no publication date and its referenced hiring events (April 1 at CMU and May 9 at Lincoln Park Barn) are past. ## Finding 2
The Bureau of Land Management and the Southern Ute Indian Tribe are formally initiating a planning process to guide future development of tribal trust oil and gas resources within a planning area of approximately 108,000 acres inside the Southern Ute Indian Reservation, including 64,000 acres of tribal trust minerals; anticipated development would target the Mancos Shale Formation roughly 7,000 feet beneath the surface in the San Juan Basin. An in-person public scoping meeting runs 6 to 8:30 p.m. Mountain Time on September 24, 2026, at the Sky Ute Casino Resort Event Center, 14324 US Highway 172 North, Ignacio, CO 81301. Comments may be submitted through the BLM National NEPA Register's Participate Now option or mailed to the BLM Tres Rios Field Office, Attn: Ryan Joyner, 29211 Highway 184, Dolores, CO 81323, and must be received by October 9, 2026, which is 15 days after the public meeting. BLM says the potential development is expected to boost the local economy, provide jobs, generate income, and increase demand for services, and notes oil and gas operations on BLM-managed public lands boost Colorado's economy by $4.2 billion statewide. The page states no publication date.
