## Finding 1
DeskPanther, operations software built specifically for independent food and beverage shops, launched publicly on September 8, 2026. It was built by Jade Valore, co-founder of Rad Coffee in Southern California, who ran seven disconnected systems (Google Sheets for par levels, Google Forms for repairs via QR codes, an SMS marketing platform repurposed for out-of-stock alerts) before building the tool herself. The software consolidates inventory counts, ordering, repairs, cash handling, and shift communication. It ran every Rad Coffee store, every shift, from July 6, 2026, two months before public launch; at Rad Coffee a weekly inventory-ordering workflow that took 60 to 90 minutes now typically takes under 20. More than 200 businesses joined the pre-launch waitlist, including over 100 ice cream shops. Pricing is per location, not per employee: $39 per location per month for a single shop, $34 at two or three locations, $29 at four or more, with a 30-day trial. The founder is explicit that it is not a POS, does not connect to one, and is not a scheduler; she runs Sling for scheduling alongside it. The company is bootstrapped with no outside investors. This is the tool Nolan should lead with in the Monument View conversation because it replaces the group texts, paper logs, and sticky notes that sit between the POS and the schedule.

## Finding 2
For staff scheduling at a small coffee shop, 2026 pricing runs as follows. 7shifts, the restaurant-specific scheduler with sales forecasting, labor targets, break-compliance alerts, and POS integrations, has a free plan and paid plans starting around $34.99 to $39.99 per location per month, per The Restaurant HQ and a September 2026 scheduling comparison. When I Work, a budget option with drag-and-drop scheduling, availability tracking, open shifts, shift swaps, and automatic notifications, starts at $2.50 per user per month for a single location or $5 per user per month for multiple locations. Sling, the scheduler Rad Coffee and DeskPanther's founder actually use, has a free tier and paid plans from $1.70 per user per month, with a free roster builder and newsfeed-style chat. Homebase has a genuinely free Basic plan for one location with up to 10 employees, and paid tiers from $24 to $30 per location per month with unlimited employees, making it structurally cheapest for a single busy shop with a lot of part-time staff; note its payroll add-on is separate ($39/month base plus $6/employee). Deputy charges per user: Lite at $5 per user per month, Core at $6.50, Pro at $9, with a $25 minimum monthly spend, which suits growing chains but not a lean single shop. For a family-run shop like Monument View, the realistic shortlist is Sling or 7shifts for restaurant-native scheduling, with Homebase's free plan as the zero-cost entry.

## Finding 3
Connecteam is the strongest all-in-one for a small cafe's team communication, because one app covers scheduling, a GPS time clock, in-app chat and announcements, and digital checklists. The page states no publication date; pricing on its current pricing page is a Small Business Plan that is free for life for teams under 10 employees and includes scheduling, time clock, and team messaging, with paid plans from $29 per month for up to 30 users (about $0.60 to $1.00 per month per additional user on monthly billing). If staff are over 10 but under 30, a free-for-life Limited plan covers essentials. It is the tool the Capterra 2026 roundup (4.64/5 across 3,666 small business reviews) recommends first for deskless hourly teams, describing it as a scheduling and communication platform that runs from a phone and replaces separate messaging apps. For Monument View, this is the cheapest path to killing the group-text chaos: free if they have 10 or fewer staff, one app for schedule, shift claims, and chat instead of three separate tools.

## Finding 4
For cafe inventory, TrackItWeekly is a weekly inventory counting and ordering app built by a 20-year multi-unit franchise operator, priced at $19 per month against $49-plus competitors, and it names cafes explicitly in its best-fit list. It is built around a weekly counting ritual: a manager counts stock on a phone in 30 to 90 minutes, the app calculates usage averages, sets PAR levels, and shows green/yellow/red stock indicators per item, then one button sends a vendor-ready order summary by email. It deliberately has no POS integration and no desktop software, with setup in under 5 minutes. By contrast, the full restaurant inventory suites are priced for chains: MarketMan at $249 per month and Yellow Dog at $295 per month, per a September 2026 restaurant inventory roundup. The practical recommendation for a family-run coffee shop is either DeskPanther (finding 1) for an all-in-one ops platform, or TrackItWeekly at $19 per month if they only need counting-to-ordering without adding another suite. Both pages state no exact publication date beyond the SaaSworthy listing showing a September 2026 update.
