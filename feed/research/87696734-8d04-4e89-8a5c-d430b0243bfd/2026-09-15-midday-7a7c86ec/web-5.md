Beat: web
Angle: Gather standout independent coffee shop websites or recent redesigns winning praise, with what makes each work: ordering flow, menu, story, speed, local search. The paper already covered cafe ordering stacks and Monument View's listings, so file only fresh website examples, as groundwork for Nolan's upcoming website conversation with the Monument View Coffee Shop owners.

## Finding 1
Onyx Coffee Lab of Rogers, Arkansas runs the most decorated independent coffee website in the country, and it is the exemplar to study before any website conversation. The homepage (which states no publication date) leads with a Trending Coffees rail, naming Power Nap, Geometry, Southern Weather, Tropical Weather, and Cold Brew, and funnels visitors into an Explore Our Coffee shop plus coffee subscriptions. Storytelling is built into the commerce: each offering carries a producer spotlight, currently Fredy Perez, who farms with his wife Glenis near Caona, San Andres at elevations reaching around 1,800 meters, and a Kenya lot from the Kamunyaka washing station within the Iria-Ini Farmers Cooperative Society at about 1,700 meters, and the company has long published per-coffee transparency sections on how it scored, bought, and priced each lot. The same site books barista classes at its 2nd Street Studio in Rogers, hosts an Our Locations hub with cafe hours and menus across Northwest Arkansas plus its restaurant in Johnson, and runs wholesale, merch, and specialty matcha sections. Its own awards ledger lists 2025, The Webby Awards, Best Ecommerce Website; the 2024 Sprudge Design Award for Outstanding In-House Design Team; a 2026 James Beard finalist nod for Outstanding Bar; and the 2026 100 Best Coffee Shops titles of #1 Coffee Shop In The World and #1 Coffee Shop In North America. A press-quote wall carries Gear Patrol, Thrillist, Bon Appetit, Food and Wine, USA Today, and Tasting Table, with Thrillist noting how the site lays out the whole roasting process, from roaster model to cupping schedule. Takeaway for Monument View: one site can sell beans, subscriptions, classes, and visits at once if the story and the store share the same pages.

## Finding 2
Common Grounds of Waco, Texas shows how a neighborhood cafe keeps online ordering one tap away without breaking the site's look. Its freshly rebuilt Squarespace homepage (which states no publication date) presents four location cards, 8th Street, Franklin, Woodway, and Baylor, each linking straight into that store's ordering flow at commongrounds.craverapp.com, with a persistent Order Online link, a Download the App call to action pointing at craverapp.com, and a cart icon. A French design agency's 2025 roundup of the 30 most beautiful coffee shop websites called it an incredibly reassuring site, with a readable menu, testimonials, and a warm atmosphere, adding that the e-commerce aspect is well integrated, without disrupting the visual consistency. The agency page states no publication date. Takeaway for Monument View: per-location ordering links powered by a dedicated ordering app, rather than one generic order button, is how multi-spot independents keep the flow clean.

## Finding 3
Foxy Loxy Cafe in Savannah, Georgia is the closest working model for a family-run gathering spot. Its Squarespace homepage (which states no publication date) opens with the line Foxy Loxy Cafe is equal parts coffee shop, bakery and Tex-Mex cantina, then sells the community calendar as hard as the coffee: artist receptions every first Friday of the month, and fire pits in the courtyard every Saturday with live music by local and regional musicians. A SHOP section moves merch and coffee, a FOXY IS section walks through Tex-Mex, bakery, coffee, and events, and a Recognition row carries third-party badges, Best of Savannah from Connect Savannah, a 21 Best of Savannah winner logo, Eat It and Like It, and TripAdvisor, alongside SCA member, chamber, and barista championship marks. Hours sit on the page plainly: Monday through Saturday 07:00 to 23:00, Sunday 08:00 to 18:00. The 2025 design roundup said the site tells a story, that of a vibrant gathering spot, with homemade cookies and live music, and that the branding is simple, yet consistent. Takeaway for Monument View: for a community hub, events and proof badges on the homepage convert better than a longer menu.

## Finding 4
Greater Goods Roasting of Austin, Texas is the premium roaster example. Its homepage (which states no publication date) structures the brand story as four pillars, Responsibly sourced, Meticulously roasted, Giving back, with a stated donation of a portion of proceeds from every bag to non-profit partners, and Goodness from seed to cup, followed by Born in the Hill Country, the 2015 founding story of Khanh Trang and Trey Cobb. Product cards lead with transparent from-pricing, From $10.00 on the Kickstart medium-dark espresso blend, and a View Details call to action. The 2025 design roundup praised its premium design, with particular attention paid to product photos, and noted that the site also highlights local commitment and roasting transparency. Takeaway for Monument View: founder story plus visible from-pricing on products makes a small roaster feel established without feeling corporate.
