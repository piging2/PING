Angle: Colorado's AG held the public hearing on AI chatbot disclosure rules today, Sept 15; the paper already covered the proposal and hearing notice ('The mandated introduction'). Report only what moved: what was proposed or decided, key requirements and dates, and what it means for Nolan's PING Social, which sells AI call answering to Colorado home service businesses.

## Finding 1
No public hearing was held on September 15. The Colorado Attorney General's Office has scheduled the public hearing on the proposed AI and chatbot rules for October 26, 2026, at 10 a.m., held hybrid at the Department of Law in Denver with remote participation available. The proposed rules were filed with the Secretary of State on August 11, 2026 and would implement Senate Bill 26-189 (Automated Decision-Making Technology Act) and House Bill 26-1263 (Chatbot Safety Act), both taking effect January 1, 2027. Written comments are accepted through October 26, but the department asked stakeholders to submit by September 4 to be considered for pre-hearing revisions, with any interim revisions expected to be posted by September 23. Nothing was proposed or decided on September 15; the rulemaking is still in its comment period.

## Finding 2
Under House Bill 26-1263, the Chatbot Safety Act, operators of conversational AI services must, beginning January 1, 2027: repetitively and responsively disclose to every user that they are interacting with AI and not a human; for users known to be minors, give the disclosure persistently through a visible disclaimer or at the start of each interaction plus at least once every three hours in a continuous interaction, or responsively when asked whether the service is human; not use unpredictable points or rewards to drive minor engagement; take reasonable measures to block sexually explicit content to minors and to prevent simulated emotional dependence or isolation from real-world supports; protect minor privacy and offer privacy and account management tools; run a suicide and self-harm prompt protocol that refers users to a crisis service provider; and submit an annual report to the Department of Law beginning July 1, 2027, which the department publishes. Operators are also barred from knowingly or recklessly presenting the AI as a licensed health care, legal, mental health, or dietary professional. Violations are a deceptive trade practice under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act. The page states a May 4, 2026 fiscal-note date.

## Finding 3
The August 11, 2026 proposed rules are an initial draft, not final, and the Attorney General is actively seeking input on open questions that directly touch a reseller model like PING Social's. Per the hearing notice, the department wants feedback on how responsibilities divide when an employer or other entity relies on AI operated by a third-party vendor, which is the exact PING Social pattern of selling AI call answering that runs on another platform's models and voice stack. It is also seeking input on the standard for when AI materially influences a consequential decision, and on how much detail developers must give about training data, known limitations, risks, and testing. The proposed rules would take effect January 1, 2027 alongside the statutes.

## Finding 4
For PING Social, which sells AI call answering to Colorado home service businesses, the practical takeaway is the disclosure bar: every AI-answered call would need a clear, repeated disclosure that the caller is speaking with an AI assistant and not a person, given responsively if the caller asks, rather than a one-time fine-print notice. The minor-user duties add a second layer for any business line that takes calls from teenagers, including the persistent disclaimer and the ban on engagement tricks. Two dates matter operationally: January 1, 2027, when the duties take effect, and July 1, 2027, when operator annual reporting to the Attorney General begins. The open vendor-responsibility question means PING Social should watch whether the final rules put the disclosure and reporting duty on the platform operator, the contractor deploying it, or both, since that decides what PING Social must build into its product versus what its clients must do. No new obligation landed on September 15.
