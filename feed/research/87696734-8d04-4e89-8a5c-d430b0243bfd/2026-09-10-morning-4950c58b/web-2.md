## Finding 1
On September 4, 2026, BBB Septic and Portable Toilets of Bentonville, Arkansas, announced it added Wisetack consumer financing to its website, with a live pre-qualification link on the company homepage. Eligible projects qualify for $500 to $25,000 in financing, and qualified applicants may receive a three-month 0% APR option. Checking eligibility does not affect the applicant's credit score, and the program carries no origination fees, prepayment penalties, or compounding interest. Homeowners can review their terms in minutes through the 'Get prequalified now' button, then bring an approved amount to the estimate conversation before work is scheduled. Owner Jon Jouvenaux said: 'Most people call us on the worst day of their month, and price is the first thing they ask about. Putting pre-qualification on the website means a homeowner can find out what they can afford before we ever get to the driveway. It takes the guesswork out of the conversation, and it lets people fix the system correctly instead of patching it and hoping.' Financing is available for eligible projects only, and terms are set by Wisetack's lending partners based on creditworthiness. This is a working example of a small trade contractor embedding buy-now-pay-later style financing into its own estimate flow rather than relying on the homeowner to find credit on their own.

## Finding 2
In a July 22, 2026 report on a Global Advertising Lawyers Alliance analysis, PYMNTS summarized how the 2025-2026 legislative cycle targeted credit and debit card surcharges and junk fees across the states. Louisiana went furthest on debit: Gov. Jeff Landry signed SB254, which prohibits retail businesses from imposing surcharges on consumers paying with debit cards, effective August 1, 2026; it also creates a private right of action for consumers harmed by violations. New Jersey lawmakers considered AB4807, which would prohibit surcharging altogether, while Georgia's HB700 and Oklahoma's SB2132 proposed tying permissible surcharge amounts to merchants' actual payment-processing costs. Illinois and Minnesota considered allowing surcharges only when consumers have access to an alternative payment method without the extra charge. On interchange, Colorado's SB134, which would have prohibited card networks from charging interchange fees on the sales-tax portion of transactions, was vetoed June 3, 2026 by Gov. Jared Polis, citing significant legal risks including federal preemption concerns; a similar Illinois law was substantially blocked in federal court, with U.S. District Judge Virginia Kendall ruling June 1 that Illinois is permanently barred from enforcing its interchange restriction against national banks, federal savings associations, and payment card networks. The picture is a fragmented state-by-state landscape where a surcharge practice permissible in one state can trigger disclosure duties or lawsuits in another.

## Finding 3
On July 15, 2026, Housecall Pro announced its first industry-specific software packages for HVAC, plumbing, and electrical businesses, built from insights across more than 100 million jobs and feedback from more than 200,000 home service professionals. Each package comes preconfigured with trade-specific onboarding, templates, and workflows, integrating core Housecall Pro capabilities such as scheduling, estimates, invoicing, payments, online booking, and reporting. The feature list includes credit card surcharging as a built-in capability, alongside a mechanical KPI dashboard, membership plans on jobs and estimates, real-time AI-driven pricing benchmarks for pricebook items, and price book commissions. The announcement positions credit card surcharging as a standard, first-class feature in the field service platform, which makes the state-by-state surcharge restrictions (such as Louisiana's debit surcharge ban) directly relevant to contractors configuring these packages.
