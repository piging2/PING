## Finding 1
The Mesa County Board of Commissioners unanimously approved ballot measure 1A, sending a November 2026 vote to voters on up to $80 million in county bonds for the long-planned 29 Road interchange at Interstate 70 in Grand Junction. The measure would not raise taxes; the county says the bonds would be repaid from existing county sales tax revenues, with the Sept. 5 public hearing slides stating a total repayment cost of $173,438,202. The project budget breaks down as about $12 million to extend and modify 29 Road north of Patterson Road and $68 million to build the interchange itself, connecting 29 Road directly to I-70, widening the road between Patterson and I-70 from two to four lanes, and adding new pedestrian and bike paths. If voters approve in November, the final design process begins. Chair Bobbie Daniel called it 'smart growth' and noted the project takes no agricultural land or water; Commissioner Janet Rowland said residents have discussed it since the early 1980s, adding 'I think it's time we let the voters decide what they want to do'; Commissioner Cody Davis said 'the science and the data' show the interchange will reduce greenhouse gases because drivers will travel more miles with fewer hours on the road. The county's announcement page states no publication date; it was listed as updated about a day before September 10, 2026, and references the Sept. 5 public hearing. No other fresh Western Colorado openings, hirings, or permit approvals surfaced in this sweep; the other new items found were the excluded rec-center and Morgan Mining stories, a Sept. 9 Lesprom report that Neiman Enterprises may close its Montrose sawmill within 60 days, and an 8-days-old Montrose council vote contributing $325,000 toward Colorado Mesa University's Montrose campus expansion.
