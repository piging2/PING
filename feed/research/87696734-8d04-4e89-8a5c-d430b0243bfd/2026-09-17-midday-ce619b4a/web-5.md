## Finding 1
A 24-technician residential HVAC contractor in the Greater Toronto Area paired dispatch automation with a Voice AI phone agent and measured results over four weeks pre- versus four weeks post-go-live during the early summer rush, without migrating off ServiceTitan or retraining the field. Baseline problems were a first-call booking rate of about 46 percent, a missed-call rate of about 18 percent on hot days, and an average speed to answer of about 1 minute 45 seconds during peaks with two full-time CSRs. After the one-week rollout, first-call bookings rose from 46 percent to 59 percent, a 28 percent lift; the peak missed-call rate fell from 18 percent to 3 percent as the phone agent answered within seconds; average speed to answer for overflow calls dropped to about 8 seconds; same-day dispatch rose from 22 percent to 35 percent of service calls; manual rescheduling calls fell 62 percent thanks to 48/24/2-hour reminders and one-tap self-serve reschedule links; and about 12 hours per week of CSR time shifted to installs, follow-up, and maintenance plan upsells. Quality held: wrong-skill assignments dropped to near zero by week two and complaints about missed windows decreased. The rollout ran in stages: days 1-2 mapping job types and escalation rules, days 3-4 piloting in one zone with a dispatcher approving each suggestion, day 5 turning on Voice AI for overflow and after-hours, days 6-7 refining phrasing and expanding to full coverage. The automation booked directly into ServiceTitan, matched skill tags to job types, honored AM/PM customer windows, and handed complex installs, price negotiations, and escalations to humans with transcript summaries. This is a vendor-authored case study published by the agency that built the system, so the numbers are client-reported through that vendor; the page carries no printed publication date, and search indexing showed it last updated roughly a day before September 17, 2026.

## Finding 2
Review Rover, a Google review automation platform built for home service businesses, reports that it fires review requests automatically the moment a job closes or an invoice is paid, connecting to Jobber, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, QuickBooks Online, Stripe, or 8,000-plus integrations via Zapier, with a default cadence of an SMS within minutes of job close, a follow-up email at 24 hours, and a second email at 72 hours. The company claims the system converts 20 to 30 percent of contacted customers into posted Google reviews, and that a five-touch sequence currently in beta delivers conversion rates roughly three times higher than single-send tools. Documented results across accounts, dated June 17, 2026, include 107 new Google reviews in four months across two Google Business Profiles for a residential cleaning company; 72 new reviews in 60 days for a multi-location law firm whose review pipeline had gone cold; 50-plus new reviews every single month for an outdoor lighting installer that previously averaged five sporadic reviews per month; a 600 percent review momentum increase for a roofing company that had collected only one review in the prior 90 days; and nine five-star reviews in six weeks for an electrical contractor with zero Google reviews before, using a QuickBooks-triggered request on every paid invoice. The platform reports a median 312 percent lift in monthly review velocity within 30 days of setup across 100-plus accounts, charges $97 per month for the first Google Business Profile plus $50 per additional location with month-to-month contracts, and cites the Whitespark 2026 study finding review signals now account for 20 percent of Local Pack ranking weight, up from 16 percent in 2023. These are vendor-reported figures in a company-issued announcement, not independently audited case studies.

## Finding 3
Kansas City AI automation firm Apex Resolution, in a GlobeNewswire announcement dated April 1, 2026, reported client results from Voice AI and automated follow-up systems for small home service businesses. Its initial client engagement, with a Kansas City-area plumbing company, lifted the call answer rate from 45 percent to 95 percent after implementing the Voice AI system, recovering approximately $8,000 per month in previously lost revenue. A separate client engagement re-engaged 73 dormant customer leads with zero advertising spend. The announcement states that home service businesses in the Kansas City metro can lose an estimated 40 to 60 percent of inbound calls during after-hours and weekend windows. The firm's offering includes 24/7 AI Voice call handling, CRM automation, automated lead follow-up sequences, and done-for-you marketing infrastructure, delivered through a proprietary methodology called the Consistency System with a fully operational Voice AI system within five business days of engagement and no long-term contracts beyond the initial 90-day engagement. ## Finding 4
Conversion System's guide for HVAC owners, current as of early September 2026, lays out the four tasks an AI agent typically takes first without a dispatcher: after-hours call capture that qualifies requests and books time slots or routes emergencies to the on-call tech; estimate follow-up running a three-touch text and email sequence over 72 hours, since HVAC estimates followed up within 24 hours convert at a fraction of the rate of those left alone after 72 hours; maintenance reminder outreach that reads the service history and books customers whose last tune-up was more than 11 months ago; and review request delivery on the same day the job closes, noting Google review velocity is a direct input to local map-pack ranking. The guide works through illustrative payback math, explicitly labeled as not client results: a 10-technician HVAC company missing 25 calls per week at a $600 average job value and a 40 percent close rate walks past roughly $6,000 in potential weekly revenue, and converting even three missed calls per week covers a $500/month agent run cost. On pricing, home-service SaaS products typically run $150 to $500 per month, general-purpose AI sales agent platforms $400 to $900 per month, and custom builds $10,000 to $25,000 fixed plus $300 to $700 per month in infrastructure and LLM costs. It advises tracking the handoff rate as the calibration metric: a well-calibrated residential HVAC after-hours agent hands off 15 to 25 percent of conversations to humans, below 5 percent suggests the agent is handling situations it should not, and above 40 percent means qualification thresholds are too sensitive.
