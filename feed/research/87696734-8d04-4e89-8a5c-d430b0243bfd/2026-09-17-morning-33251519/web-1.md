## Finding 1
QuoteIQ announced on September 14, 2026 in Savannah, Ga. the launch of QuoteIQ MCP, described as the first native Model Context Protocol connector built directly into a home service CRM. The connector securely links a contractor's QuoteIQ account to ChatGPT, Claude, Grok and other MCP-compatible AI assistants so users can manage the business by conversation instead of clicking through screens. Contractors can find customers, review account histories, prepare estimates and invoices, check unpaid balances, view schedules, book jobs, create tasks and reminders, manage pipeline activity, review business analytics, and monitor inventory. The release says the assistant gets access to 134 tools spanning 21 areas of the subscriber's QuoteIQ account and works from live business information. Changes prepared by the assistant are previewed before being applied and require approval inside QuoteIQ or confirmation in the conversation, with an audit trail, undo for completed changes, and the ability to disconnect at any time. It uses secure OAuth with read and write permissions and enforces each subscriber's existing plan features and account permissions. Setup is described as about two minutes: copy the secure connector URL from QuoteIQ, add it to a compatible assistant, sign in through QuoteIQ and approve permissions, with no API keys or development work. QuoteIQ MCP is included across all QuoteIQ subscription plans, with available tools corresponding to each plan's features. Co-founders are Mike Vidan and Justin Rogers. The announcement says they rejected a $40 million acquisition and private-equity offer to stay bootstrapped and founder-owned. QuoteIQ is described as an all-in-one field service management CRM covering estimating, invoicing, scheduling, payment processing, customer management, team operations, property measurement, job documentation and marketing automation, serving more than 40,000 home and field service professionals across plumbing, HVAC, pressure washing, lawn care, roofing, painting, cleaning, landscaping and construction.

## Finding 2
2Labs published a field service software comparison for teams of roughly 1 to 30 staff, with pricing and plan details rechecked on September 7, 2026. The page warns vendor pricing, promotions, packaging and contract terms can change and tells readers to recheck before signing. Price snapshot on that date: Jobber annual-prepaid post-promotion amounts shown as Core $29 per month, Connect $99 per month, Grow $149 per month, with monthly-reference amounts of $49, $139 and $199 shown alongside. Housecall Pro annual pricing shown as Basic $59 per month, Essentials $149 per month, MAX $299 per month, and monthly pricing as $79, $189 and $329. ServiceTitan pricing is quote-required and described as per technician, with no public dollar amount on its pricing page. Included users on the check date: Jobber displayed 1-user, 5-user, 10-user, 15-user and custom selectors with dynamic pricing; Housecall Pro listed Basic with 1 user, Essentials with 5 included, MAX with 8 included; ServiceTitan said per technician without disclosing the rate or office-user rules. Evaluation paths: Jobber free trial with no credit card required; Housecall Pro 14-day trial with MAX access and no credit card required; ServiceTitan personalized demo and request-pricing path with no public self-serve trial on the cited page. The comparison frames Jobber as a focused entry point for solo operators and small crews, Housecall Pro as packaged operations for growing home-service teams with routing, GPS, checklists and pricebook features, and ServiceTitan as deeper operations for complex dispatch, commercial work or multi-location businesses. This is a pricing and packaging reference for tailoring pitches, not a launch.
- https://2labstech.com/rural-it/jobber-vs-housecall-pro-vs-servicetitan-small-service-business/ (published 2026-09-07)

## Finding 3
Revved Digital launched on August 25, 2026 in Los Angeles, Calif. a 2026 Home Service Software Directory covering more than 100 software tools for contractors. The library is organized into dedicated comparison categories including CRM and field service management, call tracking, reputation management, SEO and AI search, advertising and lead generation, email and SMS automation, analytics and attribution, estimating and payments, and websites, chat and online booking. Each category includes comparison tables, pricing information where publicly available, major features, integrations, AI capabilities, best-fit use cases and links to deeper product information. It covers plumbing, HVAC, electrical, roofing, restoration, pest control, drain and sewer, garage door and other service businesses, and includes both major industry platforms and smaller, newer or budget-oriented alternatives. Founder Andrew Palacios is quoted saying home service owners face more software than ever with overlapping tools and the library is meant to help an owner quickly understand what a platform does, who it is best for and where it fits in the stack. The announcement says the library will be updated as pricing, features, integrations and software capabilities change. For the playbook, the non-voice categories most relevant are CRM and field service management, reputation management, and estimating and payments.
