## Finding 1
In May 2026 monday.com made its biggest strategic shift since its 2021 IPO: it rebranded from a work-management tracker to an "AI Work Platform" where AI agents perform work under human supervision. Alongside the rebrand it abandoned pure per-seat pricing for a hybrid seats-plus-credits model: human users stay on seats while AI agent consumption is metered with credits. The company disclosed that AI agents now contribute 10% of net new ARR, a number it is publicly committed to growing, which means credit-based AI revenue is now a core line of business rather than an experiment. The same May 11 announcement included the planned acquisition of voice-agent startup OneAI (price undisclosed) to add spoken interfaces to its agents, Q1 2026 revenue of $351.3 million (up 24% year over year, beating the $339.3 million consensus), and a $553 million share buyback. The company positions the platform as the orchestration layer where humans assign work to agents and agents to other agents; its stated pitch is that orchestration, not model performance, is the work-management differentiator. For a combative competitive angle against this: Monday has now made its own business model depend on selling more agent consumption, with customers paying per agent-run on top of seats.

## Finding 2
In July 2026 monday.com raised monday CRM prices across every published tier: Basic CRM went from $15 to $18 per user per month (+20%), Standard CRM from $20 to $25 (+25%), and the top tier from $33 to $41 (+24.24%). At the same time "Enterprise CRM" was renamed "Ultimate CRM" with custom pricing, and all tiers now list AI credits and AI Sidekick (lite on the lower tiers, plus on Ultimate) as line items, confirming AI is now metered inside the CRM price stack. Current list pricing as of September 2026: Basic at $18/user/month ($12 annual), Standard at $25 ($17 annual), Pro at $41 ($28 annual), with a 3-seat minimum so the true entry cost is $36 to $54 per month, and contracts do not auto-renew with no mid-term downgrades allowed. Two structural cost traps stand out for combative content: the Basic plan lacks email integration and automations, features competitors bundle at their lowest tier, so small teams are pushed to Standard; and bucket seat pricing in multiples of 5 after the 3-seat minimum inflates costs for teams that do not fit the increments. Separately, starting March 1, 2026, Monday began offering Standard and Pro users five free Sidekick messages per seat per day with paid overages, so AI chat is rationed even on paid plans while Enterprise gets the expanded Sidekick Plus allotment. Page facts verified July 4, 2026 from 5 independent sources; reported here with that date, in the past tense.
- https://costbench.com/software/crm/monday-crm/ (published 2026-07-04)

## Finding 3
On May 6, 2026 monday.com relaunched around native AI agents available to every customer with no setup, able to draft marketing campaigns, qualify sales leads, triage support tickets, generate reports, run project workflows, and handle budget approvals, all "under human supervision and within monday.com's existing security, permissions and governance controls." The same relaunch announced one-click connectors to external AI platforms (Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini), a multi-model AI Platform Gateway so customers can use several large language models through monday.com, new AI modules for Make (its automation product), and expanded agent experiences coming to Slack. Co-founder and co-CEO Roy Mann said, "For over a decade, we helped teams manage work. Now we are in the business of getting it done," adding that the shift required rebuilding core parts of the platform around agents because "AI is not a feature you add. It is a foundation you build on." The company's own framing admits the core tension a competitive piece can press: the platform's value rests on a single structured store of business context across teams, and the agents are only as reliable as that data layer, while the connectors to outside AI platforms concede that the best models live elsewhere. Reported with its May 2026 date, in the past tense.

## Finding 4
A TRG Datacenters study of the 30 top work-related platforms, reported by TechBrew on August 22, 2024, found Monday.com, Gmail, and Microsoft Teams saw the most service disruptions over the prior year, with Monday.com alone seeing the most outages in the study: nearly one outage per month. Slack and Outlook each recorded five major outages in the same period, but TRG found Slack's disruptions had shorter durations and faster response times. Complementary user-side evidence from Trustpilot reviews of monday.com describes it as "extremely buggy" with slow loading times, and enterprise reviewers report "500 errors on APIs" and unannounced maintenance periods presented as downtime, with one reviewer concluding "Monday.com is no longer a reliable solution" for enterprise use. Separately, monday.com engineering has publicly described rebuilding its notifications platform because the old setup, built inside a Ruby on Rails monolith running through Sidekiq background jobs, made adding a notification take multiple days and production deploys could wait up to two days because of the monolith release process; the redesign moved orchestration into a TypeScript microservice with AWS SQS. The combative angle: the company now pitches itself as infrastructure for orchestrating AI agents across a business, while its reliability record shows monthly outages and a monolithic core it is still unwinding. All reported here with their stated dates, in the past tense.
