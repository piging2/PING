## Finding 1
QuoteIQ announced on September 14, 2026 the launch of QuoteIQ MCP, described as the first native Model Context Protocol connector built directly into a home service CRM. The connector links a contractor's QuoteIQ account to ChatGPT, Claude, Grok and other MCP-compatible AI assistants so owners can manage the business by conversation instead of clicking through screens. Through the assistant, contractors can find customers, review account histories, prepare estimates and invoices, check unpaid balances, view schedules, book jobs, create tasks and reminders, manage pipeline activity, review business analytics and monitor inventory. The release says the connector exposes 134 tools spanning 21 areas of a subscriber's QuoteIQ account, works from live business data, and is included across all QuoteIQ subscription plans with tools matching each plan's features. Changes prepared by the AI are previewed before applying and require approval inside QuoteIQ or confirmation in the conversation, with an audit trail, undo for completed changes, OAuth authentication, and setup of about two minutes with no API keys. QuoteIQ is positioned as an all-in-one field service CRM for plumbing, HVAC, pressure washing, lawn care, roofing, painting, cleaning, landscaping and construction, serving more than 40,000 home and field service professionals. This is estimating, invoicing and scheduling AI beyond the front desk, and it was not part of the Sept 16-19 receptionist comparisons.

## Finding 2
A Builts.ai case study describes dispatch coordination automation for a 24-tech residential HVAC contractor in the GTA that raised first-call bookings by 28 percent without replacing ServiceTitan. The system classifies each new service request, queries the ServiceTitan calendar, and books the best-fit slot by job type and priority, technician skill tags, proximity and drive time, schedule protection with a 15-minute travel buffer, and customer AM/PM window preferences. On selection it books the job in ServiceTitan, assigns the technician, and writes a timestamped note to the job activity log. Paired with Voice AI for peak and after-hours calls plus 48-hour, 24-hour and 2-hour SMS reminders with one-tap rescheduling, the four-week comparison showed first-call booking rate rising from 46 percent to 59 percent, missed-call rate falling from 18 percent to 3 percent during peaks, average speed to answer dropping from about 1 minute 45 seconds to about 8 seconds for overflow calls, same-day dispatch rising from 22 percent to 35 percent, manual rescheduling calls down 62 percent, and about 12 CSR hours per week freed for installs and follow-up. The page states the same playbook also connects to Housecall Pro or Jobber without forcing a migration, and that everything reads and writes inside the existing system of record. The page states no publication date.
- https://builts.ai/blog/hvac-dispatch-automation-raised-first-call-bookings-28-percent/ (undated)

## Finding 3
A competitor research document updated about 6 days before the September 20, 2026 reading day lays out current per-tier pricing for estimating-capable field service platforms relevant to blue-collar shops. Jobber is listed at Core $39 per month for 1 user, Connect $119 per month, Grow $199 per month, with team plans $169 to $599 per month, offering quote templates, signature approvals, auto-convert to job and automated follow-ups on the Grow plan. Housecall Pro is listed at Basic $59 per month, Essentials $149 per month, MAX $299 per month plus $35 per user, with basic quoting on all plans and the Sales Proposal Tool only on MAX plus a $149 per month Price Book add-on. ServiceTitan is listed at $250 to $400-plus per tech per month, or $1,225 to $1,990 per month for a 5-tech company, with Pricebook Pro, on-site estimates and financing integration, demo only and no self-serve trial. QuoteIQ is listed at Essentials $29.99 per month for 1 user, Beginner $74.99 for 2, Pro $149.99 for 4, Elite $249.99 for 7, Max $399.99 unlimited, with an AI-powered estimate generator, customer self-quoting, property measurement and photo documentation. The page states no publication date; pricing is filed as stated in the document, not verified against vendor sites.
