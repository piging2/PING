## Finding 1
The strongest Western Slope trade conversation of the last 48 hours is a Montrose homeowner's urgent request for an emergency plumber. On September 9, 2026, Kim Shell posted in a Montrose community group that she had found a leaking water heater causing major damage and, having already contacted three plumbers, needed someone faster. The text-only post drew 48 comments and 6 likes, and the replies functioned as a local referral network: commenters repeatedly named the same three businesses, Guardians Plumbing and Heating (specifically Kyle, with phone 970-200-6087), West Plumbing LLC (Kendra and Anthony West), and Unrivaled Plumbing and Heating (Jon), reinforcing each other with replies like 'I second this!' and citing same-day emergency availability, fair pricing, and courtesy. One commenter also gave immediate damage-control steps: shut off power or gas, turn off the water, and drain the tank with a garden hose. Format: text-only ask, no image. Hook that drove the replies: an urgent, specific problem plus visible frustration after striking out elsewhere; the replies that won each led with a name, a phone number, and a speed claim.

## Finding 2

Hook: a direct, low-effort call to action plus an explicit reciprocity rule; the threads became free advertising space, and the replies that stood out named a trade, a place, and one memorable detail.

## Finding 4
The single biggest engagement magnet in Colorado social this window was an auction story, and its mechanics transfer directly to trade-adjacent audiences. FOX31 Denver posted September 10, 2026 an image of weathered vintage cars lined up in a field with overlaid text reading 'FAMILY OF COLORADO COLLECTOR AUCTIONING OFF OVER 200 CARS, SOME STARTING AT 50 CENTS'; the post drew about 4,900 likes and 900 comments. The story: a family's lifetime collection belonging to a grandfather and father, too many cars to part out, being auctioned in Eaton, Colorado, with DJ Born of Rocky Mountain Relics quoted. The comment section was car-enthusiast energy: users tagging friends and family who collect cars, repeatedly asking for the auction link and the Eaton location, discussing specific models like the 1967 Impala and Dodge Power Wagon, joking about spousal fallout ('Men everywhere. My wife is gonna kill me when I come home with this'), and sharing nostalgia about past family cars. Format: single on-location photo with a big overlaid price hook. Hook mechanics that drove the replies: an absurdly low starting price, a specific place and link for people to ask after, and a tag-a-friend subject (project cars people want their buddies to see).
