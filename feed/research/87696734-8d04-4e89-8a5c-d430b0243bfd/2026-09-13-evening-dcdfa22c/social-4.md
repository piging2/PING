## Finding 1
Nathan Stafford, behind the verified account Nathan's Lawns and Gardens (560K followers), posted a storm-drain gutter cleaning video on September 12, 2026 that pulled 35K likes and 150 comments, the highest engagement in the sweep. The mechanic: one man in work clothes, cap, red ear protection and gloves clears a leaf-and-silt-choked curb gutter with a rake, backpack blower, and his hands, cars passing on a wet road behind him, and finishes by walking to camera with two thumbs up and the company logo (Nathan's Lawns and Gardens, est. 2010). Comments were overwhelmingly appreciative, with viewers calling it satisfying and relaxing, thanking him for unseen maintenance, and swapping their own drain-cleaning anecdotes. Borrowable lesson for PING Social: full-process 'satisfying work' footage plus a small civic/public-service angle earns outsized reach and saves-driven distribution that a standard before-and-after never does; the thumbs-up sign-off brands it without a sales pitch.

## Finding 2
Plumber Pete Gilbert (@beaplumber, 520K followers) posted a selfie-style trade-hack reel on September 13, 2026 that earned 2.9K likes and 86 comments, strong for a one-day-old post from a tradesperson. The mechanic: filmed casually in a bathroom mid-renovation, he heats a screwdriver shaft orange-hot with a propane blowtorch to melt paint sealing hinge screws, backs them out with an impact driver, and carries the door off while repeating his running joke 'What a tip!' The humor is self-deprecating tradesman comedy, and comments split between viewers calling the trick genius and others joking about the brown bathtub water visible in frame, with Gilbert laughing along in his caption. Borrowable lesson: a single 30-second trade trick shot selfie-style, framed with a running catchphrase, turns ordinary job-site footage into comment-bait content; the comment fire over the messy background shows imperfection boosts conversation rather than hurting it.
