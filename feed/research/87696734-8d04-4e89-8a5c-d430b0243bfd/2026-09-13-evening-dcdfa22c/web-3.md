## Finding 1
Zuper, the Seattle field-service software company that bills itself as the AI operating system for services businesses, announced four new AI agents: AR Agent, Sales Coach Agent, Weather Agent, and Reputation Agent. They join the existing Field Agent (a voice-first agent for crews on the job) and CSR Agent (an AI receptionist for overflow and after-hours calls). Sales Coach, Weather, and Reputation are available now; AR Agent ships early in Q4. This is a vendor BusinessWire release; the release page itself states no publication date, and the announcement surfaced in the business press in the days just before Sept 13. What each agent does: AR Agent contacts customers about overdue invoices over voice, SMS, and email and keeps following up until the money is in the bank; Sales Coach Agent records in-person sales conversations with the homeowner's consent, scores the visit against the company's own sales playbook, and emails coaching to the rep and manager shortly after the visit (in beta with selected roofing and exteriors companies); Weather Agent checks the weather for every city, town, or region the company works and posts a crew-ready digest to Zuper Chat, email, or both before the day starts; Reputation Agent brings new online reviews into Zuper Chat matched to the customer and their most recent job, including close date and who was assigned. CSR Agent captures the caller's name, address, damage type, insurance status, and urgency in a single call, books the inspection, and creates the job record before the call ends. Field Agent lets crews speak naturally while working, fills out the job checklist, turns photos and narration into a structured job record, and works in whatever language the crew is comfortable in. The agents are configured in plain language inside Zuper's Agent Studio, can read the company's own documents and playbooks, and are priced on usage-based credits as part of Zuper's AI plan; the company says an owner turns an agent on in minutes with no implementation project. The owner-reported numbers: services businesses running Field Agent through beta report roughly thirty minutes saved per crew member per job and checklists completed fifty percent faster with voice. JT Ulyatt, CEO of Maven Roofing, said: 'Zuper's Sales Coach Agent scores sales visits against our own playbook and the feedback is in the rep's inbox before he's out of the driveway.' Founder and CEO Anand Subbaraj framed it as the outcome era: 'Our customers do not ask us for AI. They ask us for documentation that gets finished on the roof, invoices that get followed up on, and mornings that start with answers.'

## Finding 2
Phony.ai launched a provider-neutral AI phone and voice agent platform built on the ERP.io software suite, and home services is named among its target industries alongside legal, healthcare, real estate, and automotive. This is a MarketersMedia vendor press release; the page states no publication date and the launch surfaced in the business press in the days just before Sept 13. The pitch: businesses build AI phone agents that answer inbound calls, rapidly return web inquiries, capture caller information as structured data, answer questions from the company's knowledge base, route and transfer callers, and perform approved actions such as appointment workflows. The provider-neutral part is the differentiator: customers choose the telephony carrier (Telnyx, Twilio, or their own SIP trunk), the speech-to-text provider (Deepgram, Google, Telnyx), the language model (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google), and the voice (Cartesia, ElevenLabs, Google, OpenAI), and swapping a provider is a configuration change rather than a migration. Pricing is stated outright: AI talk time costs $0.12 per minute covering speech-to-text, language model, and voice; telephony is billed separately at carrier cost rather than hidden inside the AI rate; every call generates an itemized record of its cost components plus transcript and outcome. Phony.ai is offered as a module within the ERP.io suite at $20 per user per month with a 30-day trial, and customers can connect their own supported carrier or SIP infrastructure. Consent controls are enforced in the software: outbound calls require an established basis (returning an inbound call, responding to a form on the company's own website, or an existing customer relationship); the platform has no purchased-list upload and no mass-dialing mode, and it excludes ringless voicemail, political or advocacy calling, debt-collection calling, and unauthorized voice cloning. It also supports do-not-call screening, account-wide opt-outs, quiet-hour controls, call-level consent records, and configurable retention, and voice cloning requires authorization from the person whose voice is used. CEO Nate Nead: 'We didn't want to create another closed voice platform where every technology decision is made for the customer and hidden inside a blended per-minute price.'

## Finding 4
Contractor Magazine ran a practitioner-written piece on where AI actually pays off in a contracting business, from a writer who runs a firm building AI systems for service contractors; the page states no publication date and it surfaced as recent trade-press coverage. The core claim: contractors who get value from AI do not buy 'AI for my business'; they pick one specific, painful, repetitive job and make it disappear. First, start with the phone: AI after-hours call intake answers in seconds, asks what is broken, where the customer is, and whether it is an emergency, then routes the result to whoever is on call. One plumbing contractor found that 40 percent of his new jobs came from calls arriving after 5:00 PM; after automating intake he hired no new office person and stopped missing the phone. Second, automate estimate follow-up: contractors who do this routinely report that 10 to 20 percent of 'dead' estimates come back to life. Third, dispatch and paperwork: an electrical contractor cut permit-chasing time by three-quarters with a system tracking missing signatures and drafting follow-ups. The rule of thumb: if a task takes a competent office person less than two minutes and happens more than ten times a week, it is a candidate for automation; if it takes judgment, context, or a relationship, it stays human. What the winners refuse to automate: the final pricing number on a proposal, customer-facing relationship moments (the walkthrough, the angry customer, the negotiation), and their own attention (the weekly review of numbers and exception reports). Warnings: do not buy an all-in-one AI suite on day one, read the terms before handing over customer data, and bring office staff in on day one so the system is trained on their scripts and not treated as a threat.
