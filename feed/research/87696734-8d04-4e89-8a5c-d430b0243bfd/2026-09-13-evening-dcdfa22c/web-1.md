## Finding 1
The BizWest legal briefing page states no publication date (search-listed as updated about 11 days before the Sept 13 reading, so roughly Sept 2, 2026); it summarizes four Colorado laws from the 2026 legislative session with direct effects on home-service contractors. The most material one for Nolan's market is SB26-155, which creates a program giving grants to Colorado homeowners to retrofit residential property with resilient roofs to reduce insurer losses from hail and windstorms; the program will be run by the new Strengthen Colorado Homes Enterprise, homeowners must choose a contractor licensed in the state that meets statutory and regulatory requirements, and a contractor that is awarded bids and receives grant money is prohibited from waiving homeowner insurance deductibles. Also in the briefing: HB26-1034 repeals the strict water and energy efficiency standards previously applicable to irrigation controllers and spray sprinkler bodies installed by landscape contractors; HB26-1272 requires the Colorado Department of Labor and Employment's Division of Labor Standards and Statistics to begin collecting data about temperature-related injury or illness at worksites by January 15, 2027, and to publish a model temperature-related injury and illness prevention plan by July 1, 2028; SB26-001 clarifies that the sales and use tax exemption on construction materials extends to county workforce-housing projects. The article is by the BHGR law firm's Construction Group and is informational, not legal advice.
