Angle: Coffee-shop tech brief for Nolan's in-person conversation with the Monument View Coffee Shop owners in Grand Junction/Redlands: current practical POS, online ordering, and website options for a small family-run shop, with costs and trade-offs. Keep to business outcomes, no AI hype; this grounds his website and operations prep.

## Finding 1
For a small single-location coffee shop, Square is the lowest-friction POS choice and Toast the closest rival, with Clover a pricier third option. Square offers a free plan at $0/month (2.6% + 15 cents in person, 3.3% + 30 cents online) and Square Plus at $49/month per location (2.5% + 15 cents in person, 2.9% + 30 cents online), with no long-term contracts; hardware starts at $799 for the Square Register, and Square hardware works with tablets the shop may already own. Toast Standard runs $69/month per location (2.49% + 15 cents in person, 3.5% + 15 cents online), requires its own hardware ($1,024 Countertop Starter Kit plus $225 customer-facing display), mandates Toast's payment processing, and locks shops into a 24-month minimum contract. Toast's online ordering is a $75/month add-on ($149/month with website builder), while Square bundles online ordering and its website builder free. A third-party comparison notes Toast beats Clover for small cafes ($69/month versus $135/month for the Clover hospitality equivalent) and says Clover has the lowest transaction fees of the three (roughly 2.3% to 2.6% + 10 cents), but Clover runs on proprietary hardware, its marketplace apps commonly add $9.95 to $29.95 each per month, and monthly fees stack up. Business outcome: a shop doing most sales in person saves roughly $240 a year on software with Square Plus versus Toast Standard before counting Toast's paid add-ons, and avoids a two-year lock-in.

## Finding 2
Delivery-app commissions are the biggest leak in online ordering for a small shop. DoorDash charges 15% (Basic), 25% (Plus), or 30% (Premier) on delivered orders, with 6% on pickup orders; on a $25 order that is $3.75 to $7.50. Uber Eats raised rates effective March 10, 2026: the Lite tier went from 15% to 20%, pickup from 6% to 7%, and Plus-tier orders from Uber One members now cost 30% instead of 25%, so the same $25 order costs $5.00 to $7.50. Marketplace commissions already include card processing, and customer-side fees can add 25% or more to what the customer pays, quietly suppressing order frequency. The practical move for a coffee shop: keep one marketplace for discovery but push regulars to a first-party channel. DoorDash's own commission-free Storefront/Online Ordering product takes 0% commission with only standard processing (about 2.9% + 30 cents) and its Starter package is free with a marketplace account. Flat-rate alternatives cost roughly $120/month (ChowNow) to $499/month (Owner.com), while Toast and Square bundle online ordering with their POS (Square includes it, Toast charges $75/month). A shop doing $6,000 a month through apps at 25% pays $1,500 a month in commissions; moving regulars to a direct channel is the single highest-margin change available.

## Finding 3
The cheapest credible website path is the one attached to the POS they already use. Square Online has a free tier (website builder plus online payments at 2.9% processing), with paid plans at $29/month (Plus) and $79/month (Premium); it syncs menu and inventory with the Square POS automatically. Independent builder pricing, from cost data researched August 7, 2026: Squarespace runs $16 to $49/month on annual billing ($19 to $124 month-to-month) with no free plan (14-day trial); Wix runs $17 to $159/month on annual billing (Light $17, Core $29 with a 4% transaction fee, Business $39 with 2%, Business Elite $159); WordPress software is free with hosting at $3 to $30/month and a domain around $15/year, realistically $5 to $15/month for a basic site but requiring the owner to maintain it. If they hire a developer, a basic professional small-business site runs $500 to $2,000 and a custom site $2,000 to $10,000. A coffee-shop software budgeting guide suggests planning $100 to $400 per month per location for the full stack (POS $60 to $200, operations/checklists $30 to $100, scheduling $30 to $80). Business outcome: start with the free Square Online site if they go with Square POS (zero extra cost, one menu to maintain), upgrade to Squarespace or Wix only if they need design polish they cannot get there, and treat a custom-built site as a later investment, not the first step.
