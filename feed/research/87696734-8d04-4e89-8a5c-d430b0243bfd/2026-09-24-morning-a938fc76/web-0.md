## Finding 1
On September 14, 2026, the Colorado Secretary of State's office announced it had marked 12,693 business entities as fraudulent and removed another 10,214 from the state's business registry in the first month under House Bill 26-1088, which took effect August 12. The law lets the office proactively flag entities that list a known fraudulent entity as their registered agent, and remove filings from public view entirely when a bank reverses the filing payment. Between February 2023 and August 11, 2026, more than 7,700 entities had been marked fraudulent through the old complaint process; the announcement brought the total to nearly 20,500 marked plus more than 10,000 removed entirely. This matters for the new-business radar because fraudulent filings have materially inflated Colorado's new-entity counts: one individual allegedly registered 15,433 LLCs to a single Northglenn address during the $1 filing-fee holiday, about 9 percent of all LLCs filed in that period, and state business-research economists say filing data is currently not a reliable predictor of real business formation. Separately, this sweep could not verify individual trade-business filings from September 22-24, 2026: the Secretary of State's business search and its Socrata open-data mirror at data.colorado.gov refused automated access from this seat's tools, and no chamber, city, or press source published dated new trade-business filings in the Grand Junction to Carbondale corridor for those days.
