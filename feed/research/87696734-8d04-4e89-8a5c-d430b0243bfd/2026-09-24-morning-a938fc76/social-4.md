Beat: social
Angle: Western Slope trade chatter since September 18: what local contractors and home service owners are posting and discussing in Grand Junction-area Facebook groups, Instagram, and Threads (hiring, slow-season work, tools, homeowner requests, complaints about missed calls or no-shows). The paper's last sweep found only tradespeople advertising and no homeowner demand; report what the conversation looks like now and what changed.

## Finding 1
The homeowner side of the missed-call/no-show problem is now surfacing in posts, not just contractor chatter. Sandra Scott Wells, posting to Facebook September 17-18 about her rural property, documented a contractor who did not show up on the agreed date to install base gravel before rain arrived. Her caption, alongside rain-over-mud video, disputes the quoted $5,000 price, declines upsell attempts for dirt and a cattle guard, says her own construction knowledge kept her from overspending, and warns she will be furious if the contractor breaks his word again because she adjusted travel for the work. The video drew 19 likes and 3 comments on Facebook from an account with 1.6K followers, and she cross-posted it to Instagram (@thesandrawells, 500 followers) the same day. Separately, Threads user Lee (@lieshamariee, 370 followers) posted September 22 that a scheduled maintenance worker never arrived after she waited the entire day. This is the demand side the last sweep could not find: homeowners asking for work and being ghosted, not just tradespeople advertising.

## Finding 2
The freshest post in the window, posted September 23 to Instagram by @_whatthefunck (38 followers), is a selfie video venting about a home warranty plumber appointment that never happened. She explains she had an 8am-4pm window for a plumber to fix a malfunctioning toilet; by 4:10 pm with no contact, she called the company and was told the appointment had been moved to tomorrow because of a technician car accident. She disputes that account, showing her app still lists the appointment for today and saying she received no call or update. Her closing point is about the communication failure rather than the delay itself: she works from home and was present, but someone who took time off work would have wasted the entire day. The pattern across this week's posts is consistent: the grievance is the silent reschedule, not the late job.
- https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdpWD9Hucmw/ (published 2026-09-23)

## Finding 3
The advertising side persists. A September 21 Facebook group post from @linc.mooney (190 followers) promotes Lincoln Mooney Construction Project Support, offering temporary, project-based construction support for local builders and contractors in Grand Junction and Mesa County. The two-slide carousel advertises over 5 years of residential construction management experience with custom home expertise and lists services including project coordination, subcontractor communication, bid organization, procurement, scheduling, RFIs, plan review, change orders, punch lists, and meeting minutes. Unlike the usual trade advertising seen in the last sweep, this pitch sells the operational layer contractors struggle with, scheduling and subcontractor coordination, which is the same failure point showing up in the homeowner complaint posts this week.
- https://www.facebook.com/groups/1194796072713533/permalink/1445248561001615/ (published 2026-09-22)

## Finding 4
Contractors themselves are publicly trading no-show stories this week. On Instagram September 21, Josh Martin (@josh_triestosell, 21 followers) filmed himself from his car recounting driving almost an hour to his first gutter appointment, generated by a home show lead, only to find a vacant house with clogged gutters; he says he called both the call center and the customer with no answer and frames it as scheduling incompetence with tradesman-to-tradesman humor. On Threads September 19, JP (@jp.flores29, 260 followers, a back-office account for trade businesses) posed a practical scenario to general contractor owners: when a subcontractor fails to appear on site and the customer asks where they are, what should the owner say, inviting responses about communication strategy and customer management. On Facebook September 21, a man in a Garcia Concrete hoodie posted in Spanish discussing a missed appointment by workers, treating no-shows as an open question of accountability versus personal circumstances (source in Spanish; the post was delivered by the platform as a workplace vlog clip). No-shows are the shared topic of the week on both sides of the fence.
