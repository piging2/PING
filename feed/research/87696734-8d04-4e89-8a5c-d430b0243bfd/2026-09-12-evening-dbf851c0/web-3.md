## Finding 1
ServiceTitan (NASDAQ: TTAN) reported quarterly results on September 8, 2026, beating expectations with earnings of 40 cents a share on revenue of $292.8 million, up nearly 21% from a year ago (analysts expected 35 cents a share and $285.9 million), but the stock fell more than 19% in after-hours trading after third-quarter revenue guidance of $285 million to $287 million came in below the $288 million consensus, according to Barron's. Co-founder and CEO Ara Mahdessian said the company's agentic operating system added $50 million of non-GAAP free cash flow in the second quarter, calling AI 'once in a lifetime opportunities'; gross transaction volume rose 19% to $22.9 billion, and full-year revenue guidance of $1.139 billion to $1.144 billion ran slightly ahead of the $1.138 billion consensus. On September 9 the shares cratered $24.46 (-30%), erasing over $2 billion in market capitalization, and two shareholder-rights firms (Holzer & Holzer on September 9, Hagens Berman on September 10) opened investigations into whether ServiceTitan was transparent about its push to upsell 'Max,' the package of its most advanced functionality and AI features with expert guidance. The pitch-relevant detail: ServiceTitan disclosed that 'because Max requires such substantial change management, we typically do not bill for the first quarter of a contract and then ramp to full contract value for the first year or so,' alongside a $2 million to $3 million subscription revenue timing headwind from the mix shift to Max; it also named Rikus Pretorius as chief revenue officer, replacing Ross Biestman. Why it matters for a Western Colorado contractor pitch: the dominant FSM's flagship AI upsell is stumbling on exactly the change-management and workflow problem PING Social solves consultatively, and ServiceTitan shops are likely frustrated and price-sensitive right now, which is an opening for a workflow-first automation conversation rather than another software sale.

## Finding 2
Podium formally announced its AI Operating System for Home Services on August 27, 2026 (PR Newswire), an AI-native platform covering field service management, communications, and membership tools that has been live since early 2026 and has already drawn hundreds of contractors off legacy FSM software. The platform's named AI agents include 'Larry,' which answers every call, qualifies the lead, checks availability, and puts a confirmed appointment on the dispatch board without staff follow-up, and an AI Membership Coordinator that works through the membership list and books seasonal visits onto the calendar. CEO Eric Rea said, 'I started Podium in 2014 and in 12 years I have never met a contractor who loves their FSM. They use it because they have to. When you build the system around agents instead of workflows, the business runs differently. The agents go do the work. For the first time, the software does the work instead of just holding the data.' Why it matters for the pitch: Podium, already known to contractors for reviews and messaging, is now competing head-on with ServiceTitan and Jobber as an AI-native FSM, so prospects will be hearing this exact 'AI answers calls and books jobs' pitch from a big vendor; the playbook should track its pricing and positioning, and the launch confirms the demand PING Social sells into.

## Finding 3
FieldServicePro, a product of EQUP Inc. founded by Vivek Sharma, announced the launch of its AI-native business suite for service companies on August 20, 2026 (IssueWire, Boca Raton, Florida), covering the full customer and job lifecycle from first inquiry through scheduling, service delivery, invoicing, and payment in one system. The suite combines FSM, CRM, sales pipelines, online booking, estimates, scheduling and dispatch, a mobile app for field staff, e-signature agreements, a client portal, and email, SMS, and WhatsApp communication, aimed at HVAC, plumbing, electrical, cleaning, pest control, landscaping, roofing, appliance repair, and pool service businesses. Its AI-supported capabilities include automated estimates, online booking, appointment scheduling, agreements, marketing content, and chatbots, plus Smart Scheduling software that builds technician schedules from job requirements and operating constraints for dispatcher review. Pricing is flat-rate with unlimited users rather than per-seat: the Starter plan is listed at $199 per month (up to 600 service visits per month and 10,000 marketing emails) and the Growth plan at $299 per month (up to 1,000 service visits and 25,000 marketing emails), with annual billing options and a 15-day free trial. Why it matters for the pitch: it is another AI-native all-in-one FSM priced per business instead of per technician, a useful comparison point when a prospect complains about per-tech ServiceTitan or Jobber pricing, and it is aimed squarely at the small and mid-sized shops PING Social targets.

## Finding 4
A detailed ServiceTitan-alternatives comparison guide on urbld.com, whose search listing showed it refreshed on September 11, 2026, now carries current 2026 price points useful for playbook pricing conversations: Jobber entry plans from around $29 per month with mid-tier near $49 per month (4.6 out of 5 on G2 and Capterra across more than 1,400 combined reviews), Housecall Pro from around $59 per month (4.7 out of 5 on Capterra with nearly 2,800 reviews, strong on booking, CRM, and payments), FieldEdge quote-based at roughly $100 to $300 per technician per month with native QuickBooks integration, Service Fusion on flat-rate unlimited-user pricing, and ServiceTitan itself at real-world costs of $48,000 to $72,000 per year for a 10-tech contractor once per-technician fees, module add-ons, and onboarding are included (Year 1 all-in reported at $55,000 to $85,000), with Marketing Pro, Phones Pro, and Pricebook Pro as extra-cost modules and typical onboarding of 3 to 6 months. Treat the numbers as directional since both guides are authored by FSM vendors selling against ServiceTitan, but they give current anchors for 'what are you paying now' discovery.
