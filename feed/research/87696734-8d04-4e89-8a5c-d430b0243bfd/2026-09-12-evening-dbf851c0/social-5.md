Beat: social
Angle: What earned real engagement for trade businesses on social since Friday: sweep Western Colorado contractor Facebook and Instagram posts and reels from the last 48 hours for standout likes, comments, or shares. Already covered and excluded: the Montrose emergency-plumber thread and the two drop-your-business comment-prompt threads — file only business-authored posts with the tactic that worked, so Nolan can borrow it.

## Finding 1
Deborah Tuttle, founder of WestCO Roofing on Colorado's Western Slope, posted a raw founder-story selfie from her car on September 10, 2026, and it pulled 170 likes and 31 comments on Facebook (she has 3.1K followers), the highest engagement of any Western Colorado trade post in the last 48 hours. The tactic that worked: earnest, specific vulnerability instead of a sales pitch. She wrote about starting over to build WestCO after years of relying on referrals, named real numbers (15 houses door-knocked in three days, 3 claims filed, aiming for 100 doors a day), and described the grind of balancing sales work with motherhood (dinner, practices, baths, bedtime after 4:00 PM). The photo is a single low-effort phone selfie in a pink WestCO Roofing tank top. Comments were uniformly warm and supportive, with multiple people sharing testimonials about how she had helped their families, promising referrals, and asking for business cards. She replied to commenters personally by name. She cross-posted the same photo and caption to Instagram as @queend_tutt (730 followers). The borrowable move for Nolan: a named founder, specific numbers, and an honest hardship story outperforms polished portfolio shots on the Western Slope, where the caption explicitly leaned on word-of-mouth and handshake values.

## Finding 2
Rainmaker Landscapes LLC, a Colorado landscaping company (1.4K followers, based in Highlands Ranch), posted a two-panel before-and-after backyard transformation on September 10, 2026, earning 16 likes and 7 comments. The top panel labeled BEFORE shows an underused yard with dry brown grass, weeds, and gravel beds; the bottom AFTER panel shows the same space as a dense green lawn with new trees, mulched beds, and a curved concrete patio. The caption reads 'From overlooked to OUTSTANDING!' and pitches the company's landscape design and construction services. The tactic that worked: the classic labeled before/after split image with a short punchy caption. Comments were all celebratory ('Truly an amazing transformation', applause emoji), with the business replying affirmatively to keep the thread warm. No debate or cost questions, just validation of the visual payoff. The borrowable move for Nolan: label the panels literally, keep the caption to one punchy line plus the service pitch, and have the business owner reply to every comment.

## Finding 3
H & M Services LLC, a small local contractor in Lewis, Colorado (southwest Colorado, 34 followers), posted a completion reel on September 10, 2026, documenting the sanding and staining of a large two-story log cabin, earning 6 likes and 2 comments. The tactic that worked: a process montage set to energetic instrumental rock music cycling through before-and-after angles (light natural wood to rich dark brown stain), with close-ups of the work itself (a bucket of dark stain being stirred), visible scaffolding and protective sheeting, and on-screen text reading 'Sanding and staining done!!!!'. The caption notes relief for the crew's shoulders and neck after the labor-intensive phase. The tone is celebratory and craft-proud rather than salesy. The borrowable move for Nolan: film the messy middle of the job, not just the finished product, and celebrate the labor ('done!!!!') so viewers feel the effort behind the transformation.
- https://www.facebook.com/reel/1105967381893984/ (published 2026-09-10)

## Finding 4
Brandon Hildebrand of Iron Mountain Construction in Grand Junction, Colorado, posted a carousel on September 12, 2026, showcasing the completed Patterson Plaza commercial build at 2388 Patterson, from empty site to finished professional plaza ready for tenant occupancy. The tactic that worked: a polished multi-angle portfolio showcase framed as a pride post, with images of the beige stucco building, stacked stone veneer, black trim, large glass storefronts, the 'PATTERSON PLAZA' monument sign, and one storefront carrying the Iron Mountain Construction logo and contact info. The caption emphasizes pride in delivering another IMC project and positions the space as available for businesses, doubling as an ad for both the contractor and the property. The account has 190 followers. No engagement counts were stated in the post data, but it is the freshest Grand Junction trade post of the weekend. The borrowable move for Nolan: frame a completed commercial job as a delivery announcement ('another one delivered') with the company branding visible in the shots, so the portfolio doubles as a lead magnet.
