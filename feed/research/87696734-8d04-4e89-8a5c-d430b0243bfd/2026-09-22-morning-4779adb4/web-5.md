## Finding 1
Western Colorado gets one dry day Monday, then a wet first week of fall starting Tuesday September 22. A low-pressure system moving across the Great Basin, plus moisture pulled north from the Gulf by high pressure over Texas, brings showers and thunderstorms that could stick around through the end of the workweek. Atmospheric moisture could climb to 180% to 200% of normal across a large portion of the region, with the highest amounts south of I-70 and the most consistent rain chances in the southern mountains. Coverage will ebb and flow day to day. For home-service owners, that means outdoor work scheduled Tuesday through Friday may get interrupted: line up indoor jobs, tell crews to expect rain delays, and watch for roofing, excavation, and exterior painting disruptions. ## Finding 2
Mesa County Public Health is delaying the start of the fall open burn season because of ongoing fire restrictions and unsafe weather conditions. Under normal circumstances the season would run September 1 through September 30 for City of Grand Junction residents and September 1 through October 31 for county residents outside city limits. The county remains under Stage Two Fire Restrictions issued by the Sheriff's Office in June 2026, and a No Burn Advisory has been in place since June 2026 because of dry, hot conditions. Burn permits will not be issued until the No Burn Advisory is lifted, and MCPH says it is monitoring week by week. This affects landscaping, tree-service, and property-cleanup contractors whose customers expect to burn yard debris this fall: those customers will need chipping, hauling, or deferral instead. ## Finding 3
The Mesa County Board of Commissioners unanimously approved putting measure 1A on the November ballot to fund the 29 Road interchange project. It would let the County and the City of Grand Junction bond for up to $80 million without raising taxes, funded from existing sales tax revenues. The project connects 29 Road directly to Interstate 70, expands 29 Road between Patterson Road and I-70 from two to four lanes, and adds pedestrian and bike paths; about $12 million would go to extending and modifying 29 Road north of Patterson and $68 million to the interchange itself. The county calls it the final link in a transportation loop planned since the 1980s. If voters approve in November, final design begins. Contractors should note the timeline: no work starts until after the vote, and the commissioners presented it at a September 5 public hearing. ## Finding 4
The Mesa County Commissioners sent a letter to the United States Postal Service opposing proposed changes to mail processing at the Grand Junction Distribution Center that would reroute local mail through Denver, adding a 500-mile round trip. The commissioners warn of delays to medication deliveries, mail-in voting (the county has over 100,000 voters), Clerk and Recorder operations including document recording and motor vehicle registrations, and local business operations generally. They cite mountainous terrain and frequent Interstate 70 closures as conditions that would worsen the delays. For home-service owners, the practical risk is slower mailed invoices, payments, permits, and registrations if the change goes through; email invoicing and digital document filing are the buffer.
