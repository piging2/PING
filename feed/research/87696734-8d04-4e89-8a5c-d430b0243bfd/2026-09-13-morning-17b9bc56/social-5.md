Beat: social
Angle: Contractor sentiment on AI call answering in public trade conversation from the last 14 days: search Facebook trade groups, Reddit, and Instagram for home-service owners discussing AI receptionists, missed-call text-back, or voice agents, wins, objections, and pricing complaints; file phrasing Nolan can reuse in his pitch.

## Finding 1
Contractors who actually run AI receptionists are filing wins in public, and the language they use is plain. Vince Mitchell posted in a Facebook trade group on September 12, 2026 that he built an AI receptionist for his uncle's company, Advanced Home Repairs in Marysville, WA, after the uncle nearly missed a 6am customer call; it answers when the owner is unavailable, asks qualifying questions, books appointments, and forwards details to prevent lost leads. When commenters asked what service he used and whether he was happy, he answered GoHighLevel and said it works pretty good surprisingly. The 11-comment thread was constructive, with the family-man angle landing: one commenter noted the uncle is a family man with 2 kids so it should help out quite a bit. Separately, a ServiceTitan promo reel from September 3, 2026 carries a customer testimonial from Richard Lozano, CEO of Air One AC, Heating, and Plumbing: he says the AI Voice Agent can take and book calls, knows customer history, setup took about 20 minutes, and the voices sound realistic enough that agents become staff members; he names one Jenny as a favorite team member. On the same day, Adam from Clearpath Profit Solutions posted a pitch citing that home-service businesses miss up to 62 percent of inbound calls and 78 percent of customers buy from whoever answers first, with the line You don't lose jobs to a robot. You lose them to voicemail.

## Finding 2
The objection catalog is consistent across posts and worth lifting verbatim. The Talking AI posted a carousel on September 4, 2026 listing six recurring objections compiled from dozens of conversations: clients won't like talking to AI, it'll sound robotic, what if it says something wrong, clients need to talk to me, it's too expensive, and I don't have time to learn another tool; rebuttals emphasize script control, natural conversation, call-transfer filtering, cost savings versus a part-time hire, and done-for-you setup, closing with the reframe The real risk isn't trying AI. A September 2, 2026 Housecall Pro reel names the root objection: a contractor recounts calling an internet provider and getting a robot that repeated how can I help you until he had to yell SERVICE, and argues contractors remain stuck in the belief AI hasn't changed in five or ten years, while the goal isn't to replace the human touch but to ensure no call goes unanswered. A mixed-sentiment Facebook group thread from August 31, 2026 shows the harder edge: commenters call AI phone agents annoying, say they've seen SO many posts across various groups stating this exact product, and one asks Is this ai phone answer junk some sort of multilevel marketing scam; even defenders only concede niche uses like after-hours scheduling, and the advice given to sellers is to emphasize the pain point of missed calls rather than AI branding and to offer low-risk pilots with 3 to 5 offices.

## Finding 3
Public pricing anchors are clustering at $297 to $500 per month. HH Studios posted a contractor-targeted reel on September 12, 2026 contrasting a full-time human receptionist at $3,050 per month for 40 hours per week with its AI receptionist at $500 per month, 24/7/365. The Blue Collar Growth reel posted the same day prices its AI Receptionist at $497 per month and its FAQ does the math the way owners think: how many recovered customers would cover $497, framing it as buying more answered calls and captured opportunities, not just AI; it also clarifies the AI doesn't replace employees, works after hours, isn't for every business, and is phone-based rather than a website chatbot. MarginMax Solutions priced its AI phone-answering service at $297 per month in a September 3, 2026 Facebook reel. On the low end, an EasySIM.ai promo from August 31, 2026 advertised a UK number plus AI receptionist at 50 pounds per year with $0.20 per AI minute and plans from 24.99 to 89.99 pounds per month, compared against 2,000 pounds per month for a real receptionist.

## Finding 4
The phrasing that gets engagement avoids the word AI and sells the missed call. An AnswerMate reel posted September 12, 2026 aimed at UK trades businesses leads with AI Receptionist vs traditional voicemail and states the key fact plainly: most people do not leave voicemails, so missing calls means missed jobs; its AI collects name, location, and job details and texts them to the owner's phone. A SoloMatic AI graphic from September 4, 2026 sells instant missed-call text-back with the headline MISSED CALLS ARE MISSED REVENUE, showing a text-back workflow: instant text-back, lead qualification, smart follow-up, booked jobs. Kekoa MacAuley, a verified creator with 980K followers, posted a carousel on September 8, 2026 teaching people how to sell a $3K AI voice agent to local service businesses, and his explicit sales advice is to lead with the missed-call problem, not the AI, and to show before-and-after demos to justify the price. Reusable lines from the last two weeks include: Every missed call is a missed paycheck (Pop's AI Communications, September 2), You're on the job. ReplyON is on the lead (ReplyON, September 4), You do the job. Voqua books the next one (Voqua.ai, September 5), and 5 missed calls a week can cost you $6,000 every month (Vozelo, September 5).
