## Finding 1
DoorDash's merchant arm has refreshed its coffee-shop playbook for 2026 around a three-stage plan a non-technical owner can follow in order. The page itself states no publication date; the search listing showed it last updated about six days before the September 23 reading day. Stage 1 (weeks 1-4), aimed at cafes with a thin or outdated online presence, is four free moves that each take under an hour: claim and complete the Google Business Profile (categories, hours, photos, menu link); put a fast mobile website live with the menu as searchable text plus an ordering link; match name, address, and phone exactly across Google, Apple Maps, Yelp, and the shop's own site; and add fresh photos every week. The page says these together decide whether someone searching 'coffee near me' finds the shop. Stage 2 (months 2-3) is a loyalty program, a basic email list (addresses captured at checkout, over WiFi, or through direct ordering), and asking for a Google review after every good interaction. Stage 3 (month 4+) adds automated email sequences (a welcome series, a win-back for lapsed regulars, birthday rewards), testing social media for what drives orders rather than likes, and measuring orders per channel and repeat rate rather than followers. The page names Google Business Profile, online reviews, and email marketing as the three highest-return channels and says to save TikTok for later. Data points it cites: 97% of consumers read reviews when choosing a local business, and AI tools like ChatGPT now account for 45% of local business discovery, up from 6% one year earlier (BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey); a one-star Yelp increase corresponds to a 5-9% increase in revenue (Harvard Business School study); and Google weighs review velocity, the number of fresh reviews each month, more heavily than the all-time total. On loyalty it argues for simple: a 'buy 9 drinks, get the 10th free' punch card with phone-number signup and no app download, because enrollment climbs when there is no app to download. On ordering it says to use marketplace platforms for discovery and then convert first-timers to direct channels (a card in the delivery bag, a first-order discount on the shop's own site), claiming online ordering can add up to 8% on top of current marketplace sales and that mobile apps see 150% more repeat customers than web ordering. The benchmark it gives is spending 3-6% of gross revenue on marketing, with the highest-return tactics costing little or nothing.

## Finding 2
Olo announced September 23, 2026 (per the release's own dated address) that Scooter's Coffee, the drive-thru chain with more than 900 locations across 32 states, has moved its digital ordering onto Olo's platform. The backend of Scooter's award-winning mobile app is now powered by Olo Ordering, and Olo Rails powers the brand's first marketplace delivery channel across participating stores. Olo says it stood up the fully customizable menu, configured point-of-sale routing, and loaded all 900+ locations to get Rails live in June, then rebuilt the digital ordering infrastructure in under 90 days to complete the app migration in September, converting every existing app user within a week of launch. Scooter's chose Olo for 99.99% uptime and infrastructure built for franchise complexity, and now uses Olo for content and menu management plus POS integration APIs. The release describes Olo's model as one menu reaching every channel with orders and guests from all of them landing in one dashboard; Olo says it powers 800+ brands across 90,000+ locations, runs 3.5 million orders a day, and has 450+ integration partners. Tyler Marpes, Scooter's vice president of technology, said: "I define partnership as what happens when no one is looking, in preparation for when they do." Olo founder and CEO Noah Glass said Scooter's looked at its options carefully before choosing the platform. The signal for a small shop is where the industry is heading: a single ordering platform that routes into the POS and unifies app, web, and marketplace delivery, rather than a stack of separate tools.

## Finding 3
Also announced September 23, 2026 (per the release's own dated address), Bareburger, the New York-born organic grass-fed beef burger brand founded in Astoria, Queens in 2009, launched a new mobile app, first-party digital ordering experience, and loyalty program powered by Thanx, live across its footprint. The app, ordering, marketing, and loyalty are integrated with Bareburger's Toast point-of-sale system, and revenue-based reporting draws a line between the offers the brand sends and the spend they generate. The program rewards guests for how they actually engage rather than transactions alone, so a plant-based orderer can be surfaced a matching reward, and it gives the brand one unified view of guest data previously fragmented across channels. The release says the third purchase is worth 10 to 12 times the first. Co-founder and CEO Euripides "EP" Pelekanos said: "We chose Thanx because it helps us understand our guests, reward them in a way that feels personal, and see the revenue that loyalty actually drives." Thanx CEO and founder Zach Goldstein said marketing teams rarely grow as fast as location counts, so first-party digital ordering and data-driven personalization need to become more robust and easier to execute at the same time. Thanx claims brands on its platform see 38% more digital repeat visits, 223% more revenue tracked to loyalty, and 5x more engaged guests in year one, at a quarter of the promotional cost; its other brands include Nothing Bundt Cakes, A&W Restaurants, Huddle House, Flower Child, and Bubbakoo's Burritos. The signal for a small shop: loyalty is moving toward POS-integrated platforms that tie rewards to actual revenue instead of standalone punch cards.

## Finding 4
Lavazza announced September 23, 2026 (per the release's own transmission date) the launch of My Lavazza, its first loyalty program in the United States, timed so members can start earning just ahead of National Coffee Day on September 29. Members earn points called "Beans," 1 Bean for every $1 spent, across three tiers: Enthusiast (0-299 Beans), Barista (300-799 Beans), and Maestro (800+ Beans). Beans are earned through purchases on lavazzausa.com, in-store receipt uploads from authorized retail partners (up to three receipts per month, up to 75 Beans per receipt), codes inside Lavazza Tabli tab packaging (1 Bean per capsule or tab), and "Missions" such as quizzes and surveys on the member dashboard. My Way subscription members automatically advance to the Barista tier, get a 50-Bean bonus on their first subscription, and earn 2x Beans on every subscription delivery. Beans redeem for Lavazza discounts, digital gift cards from a range of retailers, and other rewards, and members get three no-purchase-necessary sweepstakes entries each month. Signup is at mylavazza.lavazzausa.com. Lavazza North America marketing VP Daniele Foti framed it as engaging customers wherever they find the brand, across foodservice, retail, online, and direct channels. The signal: even a 130-year-old heritage coffee brand is now building digital-first, engagement-based loyalty in the U.S., with subscriptions and receipt uploads as earn channels a local shop can watch.
