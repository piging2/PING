## Finding 1
National Fall Tune-Up Day falls this Friday, September 25, and HomeServe is using it to push homeowners to book furnace checkups before heating season. In its September 21 press release (republished in full here, with the original on businesswire.com), HomeServe's survey of HVAC contractors found 43% say most customers never proactively schedule a tune-up, and 18% reported moderate to extended peak-season wait times, 11% turning customers away entirely. The 2026 HomeServe State of the Home survey adds that 22% of homeowners needed an HVAC repair or replacement in the past 12 months, while more than half have $1,000 or less set aside for unexpected home repairs. Cost math in the release: a tune-up averages $150 to $300 and can cut heating costs 5 to 10% (heating is nearly half of a home's energy use), versus a full system replacement averaging $3,800 to $12,000. The Farmers' Almanac 2026-2027 winter forecast cited in the release calls for an unpredictable mix with the worst conditions in January and February. HomeServe points homeowners to www.NationalTuneUpDay.com. For Western Slope HVAC and plumbing shops, this is the national press drumbeat landing two days before the weekend, the exact moment booking-call volume typically starts climbing.

## Finding 2
A new Direct Weather first-snowfall map, reported by Men's Journal on September 22, 2026, puts the mountainous areas of Montana, Wyoming, and Colorado among the first to see flakes, saying they could all see their first snow by the end of September, with more Colorado Rockies snowfall moving in during the first half of October. For the Western Slope, the timing is direct: high-country snow in Gunnison, Garfield, and upper Mesa County typically precedes the valley cold snaps that drive furnace no-heat calls and burst-pipe work in Grand Junction, Montrose, and Delta by days to a few weeks. This is a demand clock, not a weather curiosity: shops pitching tune-ups can point to a dated forecast showing winter is arriving this week in the mountains above their customers.

## Finding 3
Weather.com reported September 17, 2026 that furnaces are far more likely to fail on the first hard freeze of the season than on any other night, framing the fall tune-up as the cheapest moment to catch a weak flame sensor, ignitor, or blower bearing. A fall tune-up runs $70 to $200, but the same fix as a cold-snap midnight emergency call runs $300 to $1,200. This pairs with the other findings as the homeowner-facing version of the booking story: the seasonal rush is not only about convenience, it is about paying five times more when the first freeze exposes every deferred tune-up at once.
