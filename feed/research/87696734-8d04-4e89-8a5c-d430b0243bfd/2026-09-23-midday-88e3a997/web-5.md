## Finding 1
The FCC released a Report and Order and Further Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (FCC-CIRC 2609-05) on September 9, 2026, revising the TCPA consent-revocation rules that had been scheduled to take effect January 31, 2027. The order was circulated for tentative consideration at the Commission's September 30 open meeting, and the changes would take effect 30 days after Federal Register publication, superseding the delayed January 31, 2027 date. Three changes matter for anyone running calls or texts: (1) revocation of consent may be treated as category-specific for informational robocalls only, so a consumer opting out of debt-collection calls would not also opt out of appointment reminders, while a single revocation of telemarketing consent still kills all future telemarketing from that caller; (2) callers may designate one exclusive opt-out method, an automated voice or key-press opt-out, standardized text keywords (stop, quit, end, revoke, opt-out, cancel, unsubscribe), or a website/phone number, and need not honor revocations made by other means, provided the method is clearly and conspicuously disclosed, but callers that designate nothing must keep honoring revocations made by any reasonable means; (3) financial institutions may send exempt fraud-alert calls and texts to wireless numbers obtained from reliable sources, not only numbers provided directly by the customer. A companion notice seeks comment on reducing the revocation-honoring window from 10 to 7 business days, requiring two-way texting, and mandating a revoke-all method as a condition of category-specific revocation.

## Finding 2
The Colorado Attorney General's formal rulemaking on the proposed Automated Decision-Making Technology and Chatbot Safety rules is in its final stretch. The AG told stakeholders that written comments submitted by September 4, 2026 would be considered for a revised proposed rulemaking draft to be circulated no later than September 23, 2026, which is today. The formal comment period runs August 11 through October 26, 2026, and a hybrid public rulemaking hearing is scheduled for October 26, 2026 at 10 a.m. at the Department of Law in Denver, with remote participation available. The rules implement SB 26-189, the Automated Decision-Making Technology Act, and HB 26-1263, the Chatbot Safety Act, both effective January 1, 2027. The chatbot side directly constrains what PING Social can promise clients: operators of conversational AI services must disclose that users are interacting with AI and not humans, safeguard teen users, implement suicide and self-harm response protocols, not present chatbot outputs as equivalent to licensed professional services, and file an annual report with the AG on the efficacy of their safeguards.

## Finding 3
The FTC issued a proposed enforcement policy statement on personalized pricing (FTC press release dated August 19, 2026), and public comments close September 25, 2026, two days from now. The proposal warns that businesses may violate the FTC Act if they use personal data to personalize prices without clearly telling consumers: presenting a price as generally available when it varies person to person based on personal information can mislead, and simply saying prices may vary may not suffice when consumers would not expect personalization. The FTC says businesses should clearly explain that a price is personalized, why, and what types of consumer data are used, and that collecting, using, or sharing personal information for pricing without proper disclosure or consent may itself violate the Act. The proposal is explicitly relevant to businesses that use data analytics or artificial intelligence to help set prices, so any PING Social pitch involving AI-driven pricing, discounts, or personalized offers should include clear consumer-facing disclosure of the personalization, its reason, and the data behind it.

## Finding 4
On September 8, 2026, Rep. Virginia Foxx (R-NC) introduced H.R. 10310, the Robo Calls Off Phones Act (Robo COP Act), which would direct the FTC, within 180 days of enactment, to revise the Telemarketing Sales Rule's do-not-call provisions to prohibit politically-oriented recorded-message telephone calls to numbers on the National Do Not Call Registry. It covers outbound calls where no live person is available and a recorded message plays, either to promote, advertise, campaign, or solicit donations for or against a candidate or political issue, or naming a candidate; live volunteer calls and calls to numbers not on the registry are untouched. The bill has been referred to the House Energy and Commerce Committee and is not law. For PING Social, the context is asymmetric and worth knowing: commercial senders remain the heavily enforced side, with a record 283 TCPA suits in March 2026 alone (220 of them class actions) per TCPAWorld data cited in the piece, while political callers currently fall outside both the FCC do-not-call rules and the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule.
