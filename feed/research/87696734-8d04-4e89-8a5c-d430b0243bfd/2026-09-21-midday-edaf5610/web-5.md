## Finding 1
An NPR investigation published September 18, 2026 reports that Colorado's chatbot safety law (House Bill 26-1263, signed into law in 2026) excludes 12 categories of chatbots, including products providing commerce or customer service assistance, products primarily designed for developers or researchers, chatbots housed within search engines, and products marketed for business productivity. The piece quotes Tech Oversight Project communications director Marjorie Connolly saying big tech lobbied heavily on the Colorado bill and that the exemptions could let widely used chatbots argue they fall outside regulation. Bill sponsor state Rep. Sean Camacho said the exemptions were deliberately built in to protect commercial uses of AI: the piece quotes him saying 'We had to make sure there are enough exemptions for the commercial use of AI, whether it is insurance or a theme park, or [researching] fixing your car or on Gemini, or a search engine of any kind.' For Nolan's work this is directly material: the commerce and customer-service exemption is the category most PING Social chatbot deployments for small businesses would fall under, so those deployments sit outside the chatbot law's safety mandates as written. The investigation also reports the law was written in collaboration with Healthier Colorado, which worked with Google on the bill's language according to a person with knowledge of the situation.

## Finding 3
A September 19, 2026 analysis of SB 26-189 clarifies deployer obligations that would apply to any PING Social client deploying AI that materially influences consequential decisions (hiring, compensation, eligibility for services, credit and similar decisions). Key obligations: before using covered ADMT, the deployer must provide clear and conspicuous notice to the individual with instructions for obtaining more information; after an adverse outcome (rejection, denial, materially less favorable offer), the deployer must within 30 days provide a plain-language description of the decision and the tool's role, information on the personal data used, and an explanation of the individual's rights; individuals can request correction of inaccurate personal data and meaningful human review of the decision, where the reviewer must be specifically designated, have authority to approve, modify, or override the decision, be trained, and not simply defer to the system's output; and deployers must retain records demonstrating compliance for at least three years from the consequential decision. Critically for client work: human review of the tool's output does not by itself take a system out of scope, because Colorado applies the law whenever the output is a non-de-minimis factor in the decision, unlike California, Connecticut, Illinois, and New York City frameworks that generally exempt tools with meaningful human oversight. Indemnification clauses purporting to shield a party against its own ADMT liability are void as against public policy.
