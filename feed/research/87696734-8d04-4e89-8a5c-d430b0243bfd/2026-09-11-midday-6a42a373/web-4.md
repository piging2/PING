## Finding 1
SeenOnMain, a lead-recovery platform for local service businesses, published an audit on September 8, 2026 of 1,965 home service business websites across 30 U.S. metros and eight trades: plumbing, HVAC, electrical, roofing, landscaping, house cleaning, pest control, and garage door repair. The numbers: 55.1% of businesses advertise emergency or 24/7 availability, but only 18.0% actually offer text messaging and just 2.6% have any live chat option. 76.9% make no explicit promise about how fast they will respond to a new inquiry. Only 1 of 1,823 reachable sites (0.05%) scored 'Excellent' on the study's Digital Lead Readiness scale, and only seven businesses in the entire sample (0.38%) mentioned any form of AI assistant or chatbot. By trade, garage door repair and HVAC businesses scored highest on lead readiness while landscaping scored lowest, a gap the report attributes to differences in perceived urgency between trades. 'That gap between what a website promises and what it can actually do is where real leads get lost,' said David Cavill of SeenOnMain. Grade: STRONG INDICATION. The methodology is stated (sample size, metros, trades), but the study was commissioned by a vendor that sells the fix, so the numbers are directionally solid rather than independently audited. What changes Nolan's pitch: this is a fresh, quotable proof point for the exact gap PING Social sells into (fewer than 1 in 5 contractors can text a lead back; almost none mention AI), and the trade breakdown suggests landscaping as the ripest outreach vertical while HVAC is the most competitive.

## Finding 2
Two comparison guides published this cycle map missed-call text-back pricing for 2026. The Jays Online Reviews guide states the page states no publication date but says it researched current pricing and features in September 2026; it ran eight tools through a five-point 'Missed Call Recovery Test' (auto-text on a missed call, two-way SMS, lead saved as a CRM record, automated follow-up, booking without another phone call) and ranked HighLevel best overall at $97/mo Starter ($297/mo Pro), Podium at $399/mo, and found Jobber ($49/mo) and Housecall Pro ($59/mo) do not offer native missed-call text-back, with two-way SMS and AI receptionist features sitting on higher tiers or as add-ons. The Recoverly 14-tool comparison (prices verified from each vendor's public site as of May 2026; the page states no publication date) segments the market: purpose-built solo-trade tools at $29/mo (Recoverly, Caplexer) to $97-297/mo (Korva) and $299/mo plus a $99 setup fee (BookEveryJob); OpenPhone, now rebranded Quo, at $15-47/user/mo with built-in one-way auto-reply; GoHighLevel at $97-497/mo; reputation suites Podium at roughly $399-599/mo and Birdeye at roughly $299/mo per location. Recoverly also notes U.S. A2P 10DLC carrier registration is effectively mandatory for business texting in 2026. Grade: WEAK INDICATION on the price points, since both guides are affiliate or vendor-authored (Recoverly sells the product it compares), but STRONG INDICATION that Quo, Nolan's own phone platform, ships built-in missed-call auto-reply at roughly $15/user/mo, stated by two independent sources. What changes the pitch: Nolan gets current pricing anchors for the playbook, a clear 'feature vs. system' framing (a cheap auto-reply is a notification, not a recovery system, unless it follows up), and a notable playbook addition: prospects already on Quo/OpenPhone can switch on text-back without buying new software, which reframes that conversation from a sale to a setup.

## Finding 3

NextPhone's guide (which states its pricing was verified June 2026) puts the market at $25-299/mo: budget tiers at $25-65/mo with 30-50 call caps and no emergency routing, and full-featured flat-rate plans at $149-299/mo. Grade: STRONG INDICATION for each vendor's own pricing; WEAK INDICATION for competitors' prices, which are vendor-reported and may lag. ## Finding 4
An Octacs Systems contractor guide dated July 24, 2026 cites GoHighLevel's figure that 62% of calls made to small businesses go unanswered and 85% of those callers never call back: the one-slide stat for why missed-call recovery matters. The same guide prices the build: GoHighLevel's native text-back rides the platform subscription plus about $0.0075 per SMS in Twilio pass-through fees, so 100 text replies a month costs under $1; a self-hosted n8n build runs $10-20/mo in server fees plus the same SMS costs. It frames the two systems as complements rather than alternatives: an AI voice receptionist answers before the call is missed, while text-back catches what slips through after hours or during outages. It also flags the compliance line that matters for Nolan's follow-up designs: a single text back to a number that called first is generally defensible under the TCPA's established-business-relationship exception, but anything beyond the first reply, such as drip sequences or nurture campaigns, requires explicit opt-in, every automated sequence must offer 'Reply STOP to opt out,' and numbers need 10DLC carrier registration to send at scale. Grade: WEAK INDICATION on the 62/85 stat (secondhand via a vendor blog, not verified against GoHighLevel's original publication); the TCPA framing is the author's legal summary, not legal advice. What changes the pitch: the 62/85 stat sharpens the opener, and the compliance note belongs in the playbook: Nolan should sell the first text-back as the safe default and scope multi-step sequences as opt-in builds.
