## Finding 1
Google has begun rolling out a "Collected Info" tab in Google Business Profile dashboards, reachable through Edit profile, that shows eligible business owners the information Google's automated assistant has gathered about their company, with each item listed alongside its source and collection date, and lets owners delete individual entries. The collected information may come from automated interactions when Google contacts a business by phone call, text message, or WhatsApp, as well as publicly reported information, user contributions, and licensed content. Deleting a collected item removes it from Google's records but does not necessarily undo a change already made to the public listing, so the listing itself still has to be corrected through the normal profile editing process. The rollout is phased and currently limited to select regions, languages, and business categories. On the same day, September 24, 2026, Search Engine Roundtable's daily recap reported that Google Business Profiles is also testing setting business hours automatically: owners describe their hours in plain text and Google uses AI to interpret them into a structured format for the local panel in Search and Maps. The dev.to article's page states no explicit publication date in its rendered text; it surfaced in search results as updated about two days before the September 24, 2026 reading day.

## Finding 2
Crawler access now runs through three separate gates, and permission at the first does not clear the other two. On July 1, 2026 Cloudflare launched AI bot controls that sort traffic into Search, Agent, and Training behaviors for all customers including the free tier, and from September 15, 2026 its new-domain defaults keep Search allowed while blocking Training and Agent bots on pages that display ads, with site owners able to configure each category separately. Mixed-purpose crawlers combining Search and Training are judged by all of their behaviors, so Cloudflare states that Googlebot, Applebot, and BingBot get blocked for customers who selected Training blocking, including anyone who used the legacy one-click "Block AI bots" setting. The second gate is the edge itself: firewalls, CDNs, and bot management can return 403, 429, CAPTCHAs, or JavaScript challenges regardless of what robots.txt allows, and OpenAI's own crawler-access guidance lists WAF, CDN, bot mitigation, authentication, and rate limiting alongside robots.txt as things that can block its crawlers. The third gate is identity: ChatGPT's cloud browser signs outbound requests under the HTTP Message Signatures standard, RFC 9421, with public keys at a well-known URL, and Google and Bing both warn that a user-agent string alone can be spoofed. The rule quoted in the piece: "robots.txt expresses intent, server logs reveal reality." A companion LLM SEO guide from TechBullion, which surfaced as updated about three days before the September 24, 2026 reading day, adds two backend facts: most AI crawlers do not execute JavaScript, citing a Vercel and MERJ December 2024 server-log analysis of hundreds of millions of crawler fetches that found no evidence of JS execution, and Google's guide to optimizing for generative AI features, updated July 10, 2026, says site owners can ignore llms.txt and other special AI files, chunking content, rewriting text for AI systems, and chasing inauthentic mentions, while structured data stays worth maintaining for rich results but is not required for generative AI features and has no special schema to add. The TechBullion page states no explicit publication date in its rendered text.

## Finding 3
An 82-million-crawler-request analysis across roughly 1,600 WordPress websites over nearly six months, published September 9, 2026, found AI crawlers requested llms.txt just 31 times in total: OpenAI 17, Anthropic 5, Amazon 4, and GPTBot exactly once. In May 2026 alone, five major AI crawlers generated 177,353 sitemap.xml requests, roughly 5,700 times more sitemap activity in one month than llms.txt saw in six. Pages listed inside an llms.txt file received about 1.13 times more AI crawling than unlisted pages on the same sites, but Googlebot, which Google says does not use llms.txt for Search, showed an even stronger 1.39 times preference for those same pages, meaning the file was measuring which pages were already important rather than causing the difference. Googlebot was the most frequent llms.txt requester in the whole dataset, and the author notes that a crawler requesting a file does not mean the search engine uses it. Their stated position: publish llms.txt, automate it, then stop thinking about it; it is a low-impact optimization that does not even feed their AI-readiness score. Separately, Jeremy Howard published version 2 of the llms.txt proposal on August 10, 2026, adding discoverability: a file now covers the pages under its path with the most specific file applying, a page can announce its file with a `<link rel="describedby" href="/llms.txt">` head tag or an HTTP Link header set at the CDN, and the markdown twin of a page may live at page.md or page.html.md. But a DEV piece comparing five WordPress plugins found none of them emits the describedby link or Link header or .md twins, so the discoverability half of v2 has no implementation among them. Chrome Lighthouse 13.3 audits llms.txt in its Agentic Browsing category but assigns no score, and a missing file is marked Not Applicable rather than penalized. The dev.to piece states no explicit publication date in its rendered text; it surfaced within a day of the September 24, 2026 reading day, and on that day it confirmed developers.openai.com/llms.txt, docs.claude.com/llms.txt, and wordpress.org/llms.txt all answered 200.

## Finding 4
A real engineering team's decision record, ADR-005 dated July 26, 2026 and marked implemented and deployed, lays out a durable backend baseline for AI-discoverability on public pages that skips visual polish entirely. The team runs a client-rendered Vite and React SPA with no server-side rendering, and decided: one, static-first metadata, meaning every public route's baseline title, meta description, canonical link, Open Graph and Twitter Card tags, and JSON-LD structured data must exist in static HTML rather than only being injected by client-side JavaScript, so crawlers that never execute JS still get correct facts; two, a full server-rendering migration stays deferred until real indexing data justifies it; three, all canonical links, sitemap.xml entries, and structured-data url fields use the canonical production domain, never a preview alias; four, robots.txt treats AI crawlers like any other crawler, with a single User-agent: * block and no AI-specific carve-out, disallowing only the authenticated application boundary; five, llms.txt is a maintained first-class artifact that must be kept factually in sync with public copy whenever that copy changes; six, structured data must match visible content exactly, with Organization JSON-LD required and FAQ copy shipping both as visible markup and a matching FAQPage JSON-LD block with identical question and answer pairs, so adding a question to a visible FAQ without updating its JSON-LD counterpart counts as a regression. New public pages must ship the static-metadata baseline in their initial implementation as part of the definition of done, alongside accessibility and bilingual parity. The deployment record notes no database changes were involved: this is a frontend, static-asset, and governance decision.
