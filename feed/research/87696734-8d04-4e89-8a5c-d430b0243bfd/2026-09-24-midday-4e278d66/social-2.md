Beat: social
Angle: Sweep public Facebook groups, Instagram reels, and Threads from the last 48 hours where Western Slope contractors or homeowners discuss no-shows, slow follow-up, hiring, or AI/automation use. Paper already ran 'Homeowners ghosted' (Sept 24) and 'Knee deep' (Sept 23); file only new posts not already used, with link and what the poster said.

## Finding 1
GC builder David Wintzer (@davidwintzer, 34K followers) posted a Sept 22 reel arguing AI is not a threat to framing or concrete work but the biggest opportunity ever for general contractors who automate the administrative layer, with on-screen text reading 'AI is about to hand General Contractors that are paying attention the biggest unfair advantage EVER.' Speaking from an unfinished luxury home build in Park City, Utah, he lays out his own playbook: audit every admin role to decide what AI can do versus what humans do better with AI help, have AI draft first versions of estimates, proposals, emails, marketing and training docs with human review, remove himself as the bottleneck by letting AI handle routine decisions, and systemize SOPs and checklists so the company can scale without adding headcount. The reel drew 110 likes and 7 comments. It is a promotional/instructional piece aimed at contractors, and the actionable takeaway for a trade owner is the admin audit plus AI-first-drafting workflow rather than any single tool.
- https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdmDKWpxm8A/ (published 2026-09-22)

## Finding 2
Brody Networks posted a Sept 23 explainer reel that reframes the after-hours call question for trade businesses: 'It's Saturday morning and something is leaking,' and in an emergency the customer calls three numbers and the first one to answer gets the job. The video reframes the cost question from 'what does covering those hours cost' to 'how many calls came in last month,' noting both the handset's missed-call list and the cloud phone's call log track them, and says that list surprises people twice, once at how long it is and again at how many came outside opening hours. It presents three options for covering after-hours calls: someone on call who answers, a service that takes a message, or something that books, with the key difference shown at 7:04 am on a Saturday that a message means calling back later while a booking means showing up immediately. Core message: 'The job goes to whoever picks up.' Filed with its measure stated: posted by @brodynetworks, a 5-follower unverified account, so it is a small source carrying a genuinely transferable concept rather than a cited authority.
- https://www.instagram.com/reel/Ddot7EWggOd/ (published 2026-09-23)

## Finding 3

His caption warns that competitors will gain the advantage if they adopt automation first. The tone is motivational and sales-driven. Filed with its measure stated: a small unverified seller, but the news value is an ex-contractor naming unreliable staffing as his burnout cause and pitching automation as the answer, which is exactly the hiring-pain angle this beat tracks.
