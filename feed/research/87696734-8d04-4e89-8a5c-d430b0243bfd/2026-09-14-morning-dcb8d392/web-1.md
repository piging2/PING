## Finding 1
Phony.ai, a Seattle-Tacoma company built on the ERP.io platform, announced on September 10, 2026 a provider-neutral AI phone and voice agent platform aimed at businesses where a missed call means a missed customer, with home services named explicitly among its target verticals alongside legal, healthcare, real estate, automotive, staffing, insurance, logistics and education. The pitch is the opposite of a bundled black box: customers choose their own telephony carrier (Telnyx, Twilio, or their own SIP trunk), speech-to-text provider (Deepgram, Google, Telnyx), language model (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) and synthetic voice (Cartesia, ElevenLabs, Google, OpenAI), and swapping any layer is a configuration change rather than a migration. Pricing is stated outright, which is rare in this category: AI talk time costs $0.12 per minute covering the speech, model and voice layers, telephony is billed separately at carrier cost rather than hidden inside the AI rate, and every call generates an itemized record of its cost components. Platform access is sold as a module inside the ERP.io suite at $20 per user per month with a 30-day trial. Agents are configured from ordinary instructions plus the company's own knowledge (website content, documents, FAQs), can answer inbound calls or rapidly return web inquiries, capture structured caller data, route and transfer to humans, and run appointment-related workflows. Compliance is enforced in the software rather than in terms pages: outbound calls require an established basis such as returning an inbound call or responding to the company's own web form, and the platform excludes purchased-list uploads, mass dialing, ringless voicemail, political or advocacy calling, debt-collection calling and unauthorized voice cloning; it also supports do-not-call screening, account-wide opt-outs, quiet-hour controls, call-level consent records and configurable retention. CEO Nate Nead's positioning line, verbatim: "But we didn't want to create another closed voice platform where every technology decision is made for the customer and hidden inside a blended per-minute price."

## Finding 2
Intermedia Intelligent Communications announced on September 10, 2026 its AI Receptionist, a native AI voice agent built directly into the Intermedia platform and available to eligible Intermedia Unite and Contact Center customers in the United States, United Kingdom and Canada. The agent answers inbound calls conversationally from business-specific knowledge, understands caller intent, transfers to configured destinations, and on handoff to a human delivers an AI-generated recap of what was already discussed; it also provides after-hours and overflow coverage. Setup is no-code through Intermedia's AI Builder: pick a voice, communication style and name, add business details and knowledge collections from approved documents and web content, set transfer rules, guardrails and escalation behavior, and assign it a phone number. It sits inside the company's Digital Teammates framing, alongside AI Assistants that support employees and AI Agents that run defined workflows, drawing on the same knowledge repository and Data Hub as the rest of the platform. The release is currently in open beta with general availability expected in October 2026. For the competitive map, the notable positioning: Intermedia, with more than 150,000 business customers and over 7,500 channel partners, is selling this to partners as a way to lead with outcomes and add recurring revenue tied to call-handling performance, with the native build pitched as simpler to standardize than bolted-on AI tools. Chief Product Officer Irina Shamkova, verbatim: "Businesses - especially SMBs - need AI that is simple to configure and works within the communications systems they already rely on, so they can adopt it without adding complexity while keeping customer experiences consistent and teams in control."

## Finding 3
Dispatchly AI, Inc. announced in early September 2026 an AI-native business management platform for home service contractors operated through natural voice commands. The listing page states no publication date; the release is indexed 08092648932 and its press image is dated September 7, 2026. The pitch is consolidation: one voice-operated system replacing the disconnected CRM, scheduling, billing and marketing tools that force contractors into what the founders call a second shift of manual data entry at night. Example workflows given: a spoken request reorganizes a schedule, generates a professional estimate or dispatches a technician; a contractor can photograph a job site and instruct the system to build a Photo Estimate, send it to the customer and set a follow-up reminder; owners can verbally order targeted campaigns such as seasonal maintenance offers to dormant accounts without exporting lists. Co-founder Burak's framing, verbatim: "Contractors end up working a second shift at night just to update systems. We built Dispatchly so a simple spoken request can automatically reorganize a schedule, generate a professional estimate, or dispatch a technician." Note for the paper: this lands in the same talk-to-your-software territory as this morning's QuoteIQ conversational-CRM piece, but from a different vendor and pointed at field operations rather than the CRM record, so it reads as a second entrant in the voice-operated home-services platform race rather than a repeat of that story.

## Finding 4
Madrid-based HeyDiga raised a 5.5 million euro seed round, reported September 10, 2026, led by UK investor 6 Degrees Capital with participation from Italian Founders Fund, Decelera, Kfund and JobandTalent's founders, bringing total disclosed funding to at least 6.5 million euros. HeyDiga builds an AI receptionist that handles customer conversations across phone, WhatsApp and chat: answering questions, making bookings, qualifying prospects, transferring calls, and interacting with existing CRM and scheduling systems. The company reports serving 200 customers, up from roughly 100 in March per earlier reporting, and says its system automates more than 78 percent of incoming business conversations; both figures are company-supplied. Its current base skews toward restaurants, clinics, salons and automotive dealers in Europe, with businesses associated with Audi, Seat, Toyota, Triumph, Marco Aldany and East Crema Coffee cited. For Nolan's map it is a category signal rather than a direct Western Colorado competitor: fresh venture money is flowing into AI receptionists whose value case is captured revenue from unanswered calls, the same thesis PING Social sells to home service shops.
