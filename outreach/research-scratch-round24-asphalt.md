## Segment heat / timing notes

Seasonality: Colorado paving season runs roughly May through October. Sealcoating needs sustained pavement temps above 50F and dry conditions, so September is the tail end of the booking window: property managers and HOAs rush to get lots sealed before winter, and missed calls now are gone until spring. Commercial lot work (striping, patching) also spikes as businesses fix pavement before snow.

Demand triggers for this trade: HOA and commercial property managers scheduling sealcoat and restriping; residential driveways needing replacement after winter freeze-thaw damage; pothole and patching calls after spring melt; municipal and subdivision road projects in summer.

Pitch angles that fit: crews are on pavers, rollers, and sealcoat rigs all day and cannot answer; after-hours and weekend quote requests from homeowners and property managers go to voicemail; estimate follow-up (driveway jobs are won by the fastest quote); seasonal maintenance reminders (sealcoat every 24 to 36 months is a natural recurring touchpoint); multi-branch shops like SealCo need consistent call handling across offices.

Skip patterns: Grand Junction is crowded with paving shops (most of the verified pipeline sits there), so Glenwood/Rifle/Carbondale and Durango/Cortez/Pagosa coverage is thinner and worth another pass; Montrose and Delta yielded only Armor ProSeal verified; Cortez, Pagosa Springs, Craig, and Meeker produced no own-site-verified shops this round. Franchise and lead-broker template sites (Jet-Black style, white-label "CPS" content) appear in this segment and should keep being filtered out. Several strong local shops (Kauffman, High Country, JB's, CM Asphalt) have weak or no websites, which is both a pipeline gap and an inbound angle: thin web presence correlates with phone-only workflows and high missed-call pain.
