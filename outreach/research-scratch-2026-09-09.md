## Ecosystem notes (2026-09-09)

Chimney sweeps: September through November is the annual sweep rush across the Western Slope, with homeowners booking cleanings and Level I/II inspections before heating season and real-estate transactions adding inspection demand. Shops are small and owner-led, often a family crew with the owner on the ladder or roof, so inbound calls go to voicemail mid-job and after-hours callers book with whoever answers first. Pain fit peaks right now: thin office staffing, phone-first intake, CSIA/NFI credentialed techs whose time is the product, and service areas spanning multiple counties so a missed call can mean a lost job 60 miles away. Skip patterns seen: directory-only sweeps with no own website, templated multi-city SEO lead-broker sites with no local address or named owner, Denver-based SEO page farms targeting Western CO towns, phone-only own sites with no email or form, property-address-gated quote wizards with no honest B2B answer, and fireplace retailers that sell appliances but do not sweep.

Handyman: fall is the busiest booking window for Western CO handymen. Honey-do lists pile up with deck and fence repairs, gutter cleaning, siding touch-ups, door and window adjustments, and swamp-cooler winterization before the first hard freeze. Pre-winter work means weatherstripping, heat-cable installs, and pipe protection, while holiday-guest prep drives bathroom fixes, paint touch-ups, drywall patching, and guest-room punch lists through October and November. Second-home owners in Aspen, Snowmass, and Steamboat Springs send photo-based project requests from out of town. Most operators are owner-led or tiny teams, so the core inbound pain is missed calls and slow estimates while the owner is on a ladder or between jobs, plus estimate follow-up that dies when the day runs long. Good fits are project-photo intake, estimate follow-up nudges, and sorting mixed job types into triage buckets. This market is heavy with phone-only operators, directory-only listings, dead or parked sites, and national template lead-gen pages, so only businesses with a live own site and a real email or form route qualify.

Appliance repair: urgency-driven trade where a dead fridge is same-day work and the first shop to answer gets the call. Western CO is dominated by dealer-service hybrids (retail store plus repair bench) and family shops running on a single phone line. Best Appliance Service (Montrose) is literally telling the public it is booking 2 to 3 weeks out and running as fast as it can, which is the clearest missed-revenue signal in this batch. Skip patterns: national SEO page farms (True Appliance Repair, ALS Appliance Repair variants), corporate (Sears Home Services), directory/phone-only shops with no own site (Affordable Appliance LLC, MTN Appliance, Aspen Valley Appliance Repair, The Appliance Doctor, Appliance Service and Parts, Tri-River Appliance, Bob's Place, ABCD Appliance), commercial-only (Deti Repair Services), and Front Range companies with Western CO service pages (D3 Appliance).

Fencing: late-summer/early-fall is still build season before the ground freezes; agricultural fence work (pipe, barbed wire, game fence) is a Western Slope staple and mostly owner-built with crews in the field. Barrett Fence was the one own-site fence shop with a published email and a real form.

Yield note: 11 candidates across four segments is the genuine yield under the strict own-site email/form rule. Both delegated workers reported thin pools: handyman needed ~30 verification rounds to produce 4, and chimney produced 4 after rejecting 14. Western CO home-service businesses are overwhelmingly phone-only or directory-only; this is a real constraint on candidate volume for future runs.
