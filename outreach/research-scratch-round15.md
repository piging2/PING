## Ecosystem notes (segment heat/timing, 2026-09-10)

- Snow removal: mid-September 2026 lands squarely in the Western Colorado snow contract-signing window (September through November), so this segment is in its highest-intent inbound period of the year. Own-site evidence from Grasshoppers documents the operational reality: storm cleanups starting at 2 to 3am, 14 to 16 hour storm days, and multi-day post-storm work, with real-time camera monitoring offered on commercial sites. Storm-morning call chaos, route-capacity pressure, early-hour dispatching, and seasonal contract renewals create exactly the missed-call, follow-up, and admin-drag pain PING targets. Commercial operators (D&D Asphalt, Summit Sweeping, Grasshoppers) with multi-crew routes and site-monitoring offerings are the strongest pitch fits because route coordination and customer updates are their heaviest communication load. The segment is thinner than expected under the own-site email/form rule and heavily phone-primary; pure snow-only specialists with live own sites are scarce, so most qualified candidates are landscaping/asphalt hybrids. If the segment thins, revisit the phone-primary near-misses (Peaceful Valley, J&J Snow Removal) or route through Facebook engagement.
- Roofing: mid-September 2026, late-summer hail and storm season is winding down but roofers are in their busiest insurance-claim and free-inspection quarter. Crews are on roofs all day and phones go unanswered, so first-to-answer wins the claim, and the pitch should lead with capturing the calls they are missing right now. Coverage concentrated where the web footprint is strongest: Montrose yielded six qualified candidates, Grand Junction two, Glenwood Springs two, Durango one, Gunnison one. Smaller markets (Cortez, Rifle, Craig, Meeker, Steamboat, Aspen) produced mostly directory listings, phone-only profiles, or multi-state regional shops rather than local sites with forms or published emails. A recurring pattern: many Western Slope roofers run thin WordPress or Squarespace sites where the contact form is the only digital front door, and several post a Gmail or Yahoo address openly, which suggests they already live in their inboxes and would feel missed-message pain directly. Watch for lookalike regional chains (Good Knight, Horn Brothers) and templated city pages that mimic local shops.
