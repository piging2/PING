## Ecosystem notes

- Painting segment (mid-September 2026): early-fall is interior-repaint and pre-winter exterior season (last exterior window before cold, interior refresh before holidays, cabinet/floor work shifting indoors). Qualified pool spread across Grand Junction/Mesa County (SunShine, Top Choice, St. Bella in Montrose, WBS industrial) and Durango (Prime, San Juan Custom, NHBRHOOD), with no verifiable qualifiers in Gunnison, Glenwood Springs, Rifle, Carbondale, Aspen, Steamboat Springs, Craig, or Meeker this round despite active searching. Common skip patterns: phone-only own sites, no own site at all (directory-only operators like Mortensen), chains/stores, and national lead pages. Pitch angles that land: missed estimate requests during on-ladder hours, slow follow-up on consultation forms, and sorting the estimate pipeline when crews are splitting between interior and exterior work. Email-first outreach is natural here since most qualified shops publish a Gmail directly on their sites.
- Tree care segment (mid-September 2026): entering the fall hazard-tree and pre-winter pruning window, with storm-damage and fire-mitigation work still warm in Durango and the Grand Valley; emergency positioning (24/7 lines at Top Notch, Tree Climbers) makes after-hours missed-call capture the sharpest angle. Qualified pool is thin and partial-form heavy: five of six tree qualifiers rest on a form or email that needs one more page read for full field/CAPTCHA verification, and Durango yielded the two strongest (Southwest Certified Arborists, Rescue). Common skip patterns: phone-only own sites, acquisition by national chains (Alpine and T4 into SavATree), and one Durango form (Lorax) that demands street address plus reCAPTCHA. Market is crowded with one-to-two crew operators, so the consultative pitch should focus on missed after-hours emergency calls, estimate follow-up, and scheduling around weather windows rather than any technology talk.
