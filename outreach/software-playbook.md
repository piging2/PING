## How to use this before a call

1. Find the prospect's stack using the detection section at the bottom of this file. Grade what you find honestly: CONFIRMED, STRONG INDICATION, WEAK INDICATION, UNKNOWN.
2. Look up each tool below. Read its Gaps line first, then its Pitch tailoring line.
3. Lead with one gap, not five. Praise the tool they already pay for, name the leak it leaves open, position PING as working alongside it. Never claim an integration that does not exist.
4. The fastest money is always the same three leaks: unanswered calls, unchased quotes, unpaid invoices. Pick the one that matches their stack and stay there.

## Part 1: Field Service Management (FSM) / CRM Tools

## Intro

Field service management software is the operating system of a contractor's day. It holds the customer database, schedules and dispatches jobs, sends estimates and invoices, processes payments, and texts appointment reminders. When it works, the office stops drowning in paper and double entry, and techs see their day on a phone instead of calling the shop for updates. When it does not, the same leaks show up everywhere: missed calls that never become tickets, quotes that go out and are never followed up, new leads sitting unanswered while the office is busy, and admin staff retyping data between systems that should talk to each other. Every tool below handles the core job reasonably well. The gaps that remain are where PING Social fits, not by replacing the tool, but by covering the human moments the tool leaves open. All pricing verified via public web sources in September 2026.

### ServiceTitan
- What: Enterprise-grade FSM platform with deep CRM, dispatch, reporting, and marketing automation for trade contractors.
- Typical user: HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and garage door shops, 10 to 50+ techs, multi-location and franchise operations. Most value kicks in above 20 techs with dedicated office staff.
- Pricing: Quote-based, not published. User-reported ranges are $245 to $500 per technician per month depending on tier (Starter, Essentials, The Works), plus one-time implementation fees of $5,000 to $50,000+ and a 6 to 12 month onboarding.
- Does well: Deep reporting, pricebooks, marketing campaign tracking, and dispatch intelligence give big shops real control over revenue per truck. Integrations with QuickBooks, payroll, and Google Local Services reduce duplicate entry.
- Gaps: The platform is so complex that owners report using only 30% of what they pay for, and configuring automations takes weeks or consultants. Inbound calls and web leads still depend on staff being available, so after-hours demand and unanswered overflow calls go into a general inbox nobody checks until morning. Marketing modules report on campaigns but do not chase down stalled estimates on their own.
- Pitch tailoring: ServiceTitan is clearly the strongest system in the trade and their reporting is the gold standard; PING works alongside it to catch the calls and form leads your team can't get to in real time and to follow up on open estimates while they are still warm.

### Jobber
- What: Friendly FSM platform covering scheduling, quoting, invoicing, and client communication for home-service businesses.
- Typical user: Owner-operators and small crews of 2 to 10 techs across landscaping, cleaning, handyman, painting, and small plumbing or HVAC shops.
- Pricing: Public. Solo plans start at $29/mo (Core) to $149/mo (Grow) annually; team plans up to $529/mo for 15 users (Plus). Extra users about $29/mo each, card processing 2.9% + $0.30, with add-ons like AI Receptionist and Marketing Suite on Plus only.
- Does well: Fastest onboarding of any major FSM, a clean mobile app techs actually use, and automated quote follow-ups and review requests on higher tiers. Good QuickBooks sync and a big help library that makes self-serve realistic.
- Gaps: Automations are basic and tier-gated: real follow-up sequences and two-way SMS require Grow or Plus, and the AI Receptionist only exists on the top plan. Missed calls still route to a generic voicemail unless someone pays for the highest tier. Lead intake from the website is a form that emails the office, so nobody is responding at 9pm when the homeowner is comparing three quotes. Reporting is thin for owners trying to find which jobs are profitable.
- Pitch tailoring: Jobber is a great fit for a shop your size and the app is easy for techs to adopt; PING works alongside it to answer calls and web leads in seconds at any hour and to run the quote follow-up automatically so none of that revenue sits waiting on a busy office.

### Housecall Pro
- What: SMB-focused FSM with scheduling, dispatch, CRM, online booking, and payments, strong in home-services niches.
- Typical user: Owner-operators and shops with 1 to 10 techs: plumbing, HVAC, electrical, carpet cleaning, pest control, garage doors, appliance repair.
- Pricing: Public. Monthly: Basic $79 (1 user), Essentials $189 (5 users), MAX $329 (8 users); annual discounts drop these to $59/$149/$299. Extra users $30/mo. Some features (Sales Proposal Tool, Recurring Service Plans, Pipeline) are paid add-ons.

No built-in phone system on lower tiers, so missed calls just become voicemails checked whenever someone has time. After-hours website bookings exist but nobody staffs the questions a homeowner asks before booking, and estimate follow-up is manual, meaning one busy week buries a month of quotes.
- Pitch tailoring: Housecall Pro's booking and on-my-way tracking make you look like a much bigger shop; PING works alongside it to make sure every call and web inquiry gets an instant answer, day or night, and to chase open estimates until the customer decides.

### FieldEdge

Two-way texting and marketing features (MarketingEdge) sit on the top Elite tier only, so most users text one way or not at all. Missed calls are logged but not answered, and there is no proactive estimate follow-up outside manual office effort, so quoted work dies silently when the CSRs are swamped.
- Pitch tailoring: FieldEdge's pricebooks and agreement management are genuinely best-in-class for HVAC; PING works alongside it to be the always-on front desk your office can't staff, answering missed calls instantly and keeping estimates alive with follow-up that happens even when the team is slammed.

### Service Fusion

Web lead follow-up is manual, missed calls have no automatic capture, and quote follow-ups depend on someone remembering to call, which is exactly what breaks when the office is busy.
- Pitch tailoring: The unlimited-users pricing is honestly one of the best deals in FSM for a growing shop; PING works alongside it to cover what the office can't, instant answers to every call and web lead, and automatic follow-up on quotes so the techs' good work turns into booked jobs.

## Top 3 gaps across FSM/CRM

- Missed and after-hours calls go nowhere. Almost every tool in this category either lacks a phone system entirely or stops at voicemail and call logs. Nobody answers at 7pm, nobody answers overflow, and voicemail callbacks happen hours later if at all. This is the single biggest revenue leak PING Social can own: live, instant answering that works alongside the FSM and books straight into it.
- Quotes and estimates die without follow-up. Every platform sends estimates; almost none chases them. Follow-up sequences are tier-gated add-ons, manual office tasks, or simply absent. A persistent, automatic follow-up layer that lives on top of the existing tool converts work the shop already quoted, which is the easiest revenue to win.
- Web leads wait for a human who is busy. Contact forms and booking widgets collect inquiries but depend on office staff for the actual conversation. The homeowner comparing three contractors at 9pm books with whoever answers first. An instant-response layer on top of the FSM's lead capture closes the gap between "lead received" and "lead answered."

## Part 2: Estimating, Quoting, Photo Documentation, and Sales Tools

These tools sit between the first customer contact and the signed contract. Measurement tools (Hover, EagleView) replace ladders and tape measures with aerial imagery or phone-based 3D models. Quoting tools (SumoQuote, ResponsiBid, Leap SalesPro) turn those measurements into professional proposals, sometimes with financing and e-signature built in. CompanyCam documents the work with timestamped photos, and SalesRabbit manages door-to-door lead capture. They all make the in-person sales visit faster and sharper. But the spaces between appointments, follow-up, missed calls, and lead nurture, are where revenue still leaks. That is PING Social's territory.

### CompanyCam
- What: Mobile photo documentation app that auto-organizes timestamped, GPS-tagged job photos by project.
- Typical user: Roofing, remodeling, HVAC, and inspection contractors; overwhelmingly small businesses (roughly 80% of reviewers are SMBs).
- Pricing: Core $63/mo (1 user), Crew $119/mo (3 users), Scale $199/mo (3 users), Enterprise custom; 14-day free trial.
- Does well: The fastest way to build a visual job record. Crews snap photos in the field, everything auto-sorts by project with before/after galleries, and it works offline with automatic sync. Strong integrations with CRMs like JobNimbus and AccuLynx keep photos flowing into the job record.
- Gaps: It proves the work happened, but it does nothing to win the next job. Photos do not answer a missed call, chase an unsigned estimate, or re-engage a homeowner who went quiet. Office staff still manually request updates from the field, and per-user pricing gets expensive as crews grow. The photo trail is perfect; the follow-up trail is empty.
- Pitch tailoring: "CompanyCam is the best proof-of-work tool in the business, and your photo game is clearly strong. Where we fit alongside it is the stuff photos can't do: answering the calls that come in while your crew is on a roof, and following up on every estimate until it signs or dies."

### Hover
- What: Turns smartphone photos of a home into accurate 3D models with measurements, then builds visual estimates and signable proposals from them.
- Typical user: Exterior and remodeling contractors (roofing, siding, windows, remodelers) plus insurance restoration pros; works for small shops and larger teams alike.
- Pricing: Starter $29 one-time (first 3 projects free), Pro $999/year; per-project pricing around $50-$100 per job reported by third parties.
- Does well: Eliminates tape measures and manual takeoffs. One photo set produces measurements, material takeoffs, and a polished 3D design homeowners can see themselves in. The visual pitch closes jobs at the kitchen table, and it integrates with estimating and CRM platforms.
- Gaps: It still requires a site visit and photo session, plus processing time before the estimate is ready, so same-day quoting depends on workflow discipline. Once the proposal is sent, Hover goes quiet: no follow-up sequences, no call answering, no re-engagement of homeowners who loved the design but stalled. The measurement is instant; the revenue still waits on a rep remembering to call back.
- Pitch tailoring: "Hover makes your estimates look like the future, which is a real edge at the kitchen table. We work alongside it by making sure the future doesn't stall: every proposal gets persistent, human-feeling follow-up, and every inbound call gets answered even when your estimator is on a roof."

### EagleView
- What: Aerial-imagery measurement reports (roof area, pitch, siding, windows) ordered by address, with no site visit required.
- Typical user: Roofing, siding, and solar contractors of any size, plus insurance adjusters; the industry standard for remote measurements.
- Pricing: Per report, starting around $18/report for basic Bid Perfect roof reports and $31-$100+ for detailed reports; volume subscriptions via EagleView One are quote-based.
- Does well: The gold standard for accuracy without climbing a ladder. Order by address, get precise measurements in 2 to 24 hours, and feed them straight into estimates, material lists, or Xactimate. It saves hours per bid and removes the most dangerous part of quoting.
- Gaps: A measurement report is raw data, not a closed deal. The rep still builds the quote, sends it, and remembers to follow up, and the 2-24 hour turnaround means estimates can't go out same-day for every lead. Per-report costs add up under high-volume quoting, and nothing in the workflow re-engages a homeowner who never replied. EagleView measures the roof perfectly; nobody measures how many quotes die in the inbox.
- Pitch tailoring: "EagleView gives you the most accurate measurements in the business without a ladder, which is exactly why you use it. We sit alongside it handling the human side: instant response when a new lead comes in, and relentless follow-up on every quote your team builds from those reports."

### SumoQuote
- What: Fast quoting and proposal software built for roofing (and solar) sales teams, with price lists, templates, and margin controls.
- Typical user: Residential roofing and solar contractors with dedicated sales reps; Canadian company founded in 2018.
- Pricing: Heavy plan $209/mo annual ($239 month-to-month) for up to 5 users, $42/mo per additional user; Enterprise (50+ seats) quote-based; solar quoting add-on available.
- Does well: Turns a sales rep into a quoting machine. Standardized price lists, professional branded proposals, good-better-best options, and integrations with EagleView, Hover, JobNimbus, and even Leap and SalesRabbit. Reps quote consistently and managers keep margin control.
- Gaps: It makes quoting fast but follow-up is still on the rep, and reps are famously bad at follow-up. No call answering, no automated nurture for quotes that go cold, no reactivation of old lost bids. It also assumes a formal quoting workflow, so shops quoting over the phone or on simple jobs pay for machinery they barely use. The quote goes out in minutes; then it sits.
- Pitch tailoring: "SumoQuote clearly keeps your reps fast and your pricing consistent, which is the hard part done right. Where we fit alongside it is after the send button: every quote gets followed up like it matters, and every inbound call gets answered while your reps are out quoting."

### ResponsiBid
- What: Automated quoting plus customer self-scheduling and follow-up automation for recurring home services.
- Typical user: Window cleaning, pressure washing, gutter cleaning, carpet cleaning, and maid service shops; mostly small teams of 2-10 employees.
- Pricing: From $179-$199/mo flat rate; free trial available.
- Does well: Built for speed on simple services. Customers get instant online quotes, book themselves on your calendar, and the system runs email, SMS, voicemail, and postcard follow-up automatically. Connects to Jobber, Housecall Pro, and The Customer Factor. Shops report it pays for itself in recovered follow-up alone.
- Gaps: It is designed for standardized, repeatable services, so anything needing a real estimate (HVAC replacement, roofing, remodeling) outgrows it fast. The follow-up is template-driven sequences, not a live conversation, so a hesitant customer with a real question gets a drip email instead of an answer. And it can't pick up the phone: missed calls during a job still go to voicemail. Automation covers the simple stuff; the conversations that need a human still leak.
- Pitch tailoring: "ResponsiBid is doing the right work automating your quotes and follow-up, and your online booking clearly earns its keep. We work alongside it as the live layer: real answers when a customer has a question the templates can't handle, and every call answered when your crew is on a ladder."

### Leap (Leap SalesPro)
- What: Full in-home sales platform: estimates, good-better-best packages, dynamic contracts, e-signature, financing, and payment capture, all working offline.
- Typical user: Home improvement contractors with dedicated sales teams: roofing, siding, windows, HVAC, painting, remodeling; scales from small shops to enterprises.
- Pricing: Vendor lists SalesPro starting at $99/mo; third parties report per-user tiers of $49/$99/$125; premium plans run around $750/mo for 6 users (plus $99 per extra user); Leap CRM is $249/mo for 3 users. Higher tiers quote-based.
- Does well: Covers the entire appointment, not just the quote. Real-time estimates at the kitchen table, built-in financing from multiple lenders, remote signatures, and payment capture before the rep leaves. Offline mode keeps reps selling in dead zones, and measurement integrations pull in Hover and EagleView data.
- Gaps: It is an in-home tool, so everything between appointments is manual. Reps still decide which leads to call back, and Leap's own new "enforced resulting" feature admits reps skip logging outcomes. Missed office calls, no-show rebooking, and stale pipeline cleanup all fall on office staff. It also demands real admin work: product libraries and price lists need constant maintenance. The appointment is flawless; the week between appointments is where deals die.
- Pitch tailoring: "SalesPro clearly runs a tight in-home process. Financing to signature in one visit is the gold standard. We sit alongside it covering everything outside the appointment: instant lead response, follow-up on every unsold bid, and rebooking no-shows so your reps walk into fuller calendars."

### SalesRabbit
- What: Field sales management for door-to-door teams: territory mapping, pin-based lead tracking, route planning, gamification, and digital contracts.
- Typical user: Residential D2D sales teams: roofing, solar, pest control, HVAC, lawn care; from small crews to large canvassing operations.
- Pricing: Lite free (single user); Team $39/user/mo annual ($59 monthly, 5-user minimum); Pro $49/user/mo annual ($75 monthly); add-ons (DataGrid AI, Digital Contracts, Weather, Mover Leads) $13-$31/user/mo each; RoofLink bundle $120/user/mo; Enterprise custom.
- Does well: Purpose-built for canvassing. Reps see exactly which doors to knock, managers track rep location and performance in real time, and the Amplify gamification keeps teams competitive. DataGrid AI adds homeowner data for smarter targeting, and the RoofLink acquisition brought roofing-specific CRM and estimating into the fold.
- Gaps: Canvassing creates leads, but conversion still depends on office follow-up that SalesRabbit doesn't do: no call answering, no nurture for doors that didn't answer, no reactivation of old territory. Add-on costs stack up fast (a real deployment often doubles the base price), and it is residential-D2D shaped, so inbound web leads and commercial work fit awkwardly. The knock is tracked; the callback isn't.
- Pitch tailoring: "SalesRabbit clearly runs a disciplined canvassing operation, which is rare and valuable. We work alongside it on the part the app can't touch: every lead your team generates gets immediate, persistent follow-up, and every inbound call gets answered while your reps are knocking doors."

## Top 3 gaps (strongest PING Social angles in this category)

- Estimate follow-up is the universal leak. Every quoting tool here (SumoQuote, Leap, Hover, EagleView) produces the proposal and then goes silent. Reps do not follow up consistently, and nobody re-engages cold quotes. This is PING's strongest wedge: persistent, human-feeling follow-up on every open estimate until it signs or dies.

## Accounting, Payments, and Financing Tools Used by Contractors

Money in a contracting business never sits still. A tech finishes a job and the office has to invoice fast, collect before the customer forgets, reconcile the bank at month end, and offer financing so a $9,000 HVAC swap does not die on the kitchen table. Most small shops run two separate money systems that barely talk: an accounting tool (QuickBooks, Xero) for the books and taxes, and a payment or financing rail (Stripe, Square, PaySimple, Wisetack, Hearth, Service Finance, GreenSky) for collecting. The result is a mess of manual entry, unpaid invoices nobody chases, and financing offers that never reach the homeowner at the right moment. The tools below are solid at their core job; the gaps sit in the handoffs between invoicing, collecting, and following up, which is where cash flow quietly leaks.

### QuickBooks (Online/Desktop)
- What: The dominant small business accounting platform in the US, used for invoicing, estimates, expense tracking, payroll, and tax prep.
- Typical user: Nearly every trade; solo operators up to 25-employee shops. Desktop Enterprise skews toward larger plumbing, HVAC, and electrical shops.
- Pricing: Online Simple Start $38/mo, Essentials $85/mo, Plus $140/mo, Advanced $340/mo (August 2026 price increase). Desktop Pro/Premier are no longer sold; Desktop Enterprise Silver starts around $1,873/year for one user.
- Does well: It is the industry standard, so every bookkeeper and CPA knows it, bank feeds reconcile cleanly, and invoicing plus estimate workflows are mature. Integration ecosystem is unmatched.
- Gaps: QuickBooks records money; it never collects it. Payment reminders exist but are generic and easy to ignore, so unpaid invoices age without persistent follow-up, and nobody reconnects a declined or abandoned payment link. Bookkeeping still requires hours of categorization and reconciliation that a busy owner defers until tax time. Most important line: QuickBooks tells you who owes you money, but nothing in it chases them.
- Pitch tailoring: "QuickBooks is the right backbone, everybody's accountant speaks it, but it only tells you who hasn't paid. We work alongside QuickBooks by following up on your unpaid invoices automatically so your receivables don't sit at 60 days."

### Xero
- What: Cloud accounting platform popular with growth-stage small businesses, known for unlimited users on every plan and a large app marketplace.
- Typical user: Smaller contractors (3 to 15 employees), especially shops with office staff, and trades like remodeling, landscaping, and painting where multiple people touch the books.
- Pricing: Early $25/mo, Growing $55/mo, Established $90/mo. US payroll runs through Gusto (about $49/mo plus per-employee), so real cost often includes add-ons.
- Does well: Unlimited users for one flat fee makes it cheaper than QuickBooks for teams, the interface is cleaner, and the 1,000-plus app integrations connect payments, quoting, and banking reasonably well.
- Gaps: Like QuickBooks, Xero's invoice reminders are basic templated emails with no persistence, so late payers slip through while the owner is on a jobsite. Reconciliation and categorization still demand steady admin time, and its US accountant network is thinner, which means fewer bookkeepers to rescue a messy file. Most important line: Xero makes invoicing easy but the money still waits on the customer deciding to pay.
- Pitch tailoring: "Xero's a smart choice, unlimited users and clean books at a fair price. We work alongside Xero to chase the unpaid invoices automatically, so you stop paying office staff to be a collections department."

### Stripe
- What: Online payment processing and invoicing platform, the default rail behind most modern invoicing and quote software contractors use.
- Typical user: Tech-comfortable contractors using Field Service Management tools like Housecall Pro or Jobber; also bigger shops that embed payments in their own quoting sites.
- Pricing: No monthly fee. Online 2.9% plus 30 cents per transaction; in-person via Terminal 2.7% plus 5 cents; manually keyed 3.4% plus 30 cents; instant payouts 1.5%.
- Does well: Rock solid reliability, transparent pricing, excellent payment links and invoicing, and instant payouts keep cash flowing. It powers the checkout of most contractor software, so it "just works" behind the scenes.
- Gaps: Stripe processes the payment but does nothing when a customer stalls, abandons a payment link, or ignores an invoice; retries and dunning are minimal out of the box and most contractors never configure them. Funds also take two business days to land unless you pay for instant, and disputes are entirely on the merchant. Most important line: Stripe is a great toll road, but nobody flags down the drivers who never go through the gate.
- Pitch tailoring: "Stripe moves your money reliably and you've probably got it humming already. We work alongside Stripe by following up on abandoned payment links and unpaid invoices, so more of what you bill actually lands."

### Square
- What: All-in-one payments plus point-of-sale, card readers, invoicing, and scheduling; the classic choice for mobile tradespeople who collect on the spot.
- Typical user: Solo and small crews in carpet cleaning, handyman, landscaping, junk hauling, and mobile services who swipe cards at the jobsite.
- Pricing: No monthly fee. In-person 2.6% plus 15 cents; online invoices 3.3% plus 30 cents; manually entered 3.5% plus 15 cents. ACH via invoices 1% ($1 minimum). Hardware from a $59 reader up to a $799 register.
- Does well: Dead simple to start, free magstripe reader, next-day deposits, and it bundles POS, invoices, and scheduling in one app. Collecting payment at the curb on a phone is genuinely frictionless.
- Gaps: The friction moves upstream: Square only helps if you send the invoice or run the card, and techs finish jobs, forget to charge, and leave money on the truck. Manual entry fees (3.5%) punish the most common contractor workflow, taking a card over the phone, and invoice reminders are basic. Most important line: Square makes getting paid on the spot easy, but nothing in it makes sure the invoice actually gets sent and followed up on.
- Pitch tailoring: "Square's perfect for collecting right at the jobsite. We work alongside Square so the invoice goes out the minute the tech is done and gets followed up automatically, not when someone remembers back at the office."

### Wisetack
- What: Embedded consumer financing for contractors, offering monthly payment options at the point of sale, deeply integrated with Housecall Pro, Jobber, and ServiceTitan.
- Typical user: HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, and septic contractors selling $500 to $25,000 jobs where financing decides the close.
- Pricing: No setup fee; merchant pays 3.9% per transaction (optional extended 0% APR plans add 4.9 to 9.9% when customers select them). Customer gets prequalified in minutes with a soft credit check.
- Does well: The monthly-payment figure appears right on the quote, which lets homeowners say yes to the full repair instead of the patch. Fast approval, the contractor is paid in full 1 to 3 business days after job completion, and it lives inside the FSM tools techs already use.
- Gaps: Financing only works if the tech actually offers it at the table, and most techs are trained to fix things, not present payment options, so attach rates leak badly. Prequalification still requires the homeowner to complete a separate application, and anyone who starts it but stalls gets no follow-up. Most important line: Wisetack removes the price objection, but only if somebody remembers to bring it up and follows through when the customer hesitates.
- Pitch tailoring: "Wisetack's financing is a deal-saver when the price is the objection. We work alongside Wisetack by making sure financing gets offered on every big quote and following up with customers who started an application but went quiet."

### Hearth
- What: Home improvement financing marketplace plus digital invoicing, quotes, and payments, sold as an annual subscription to contractors.
- Typical user: Remodelers, roofers, HVAC, and general contractors running $5,000-plus projects who want financing, quoting, and invoicing in one subscription.
- Pricing: Essentials $1,499/year, Pro $1,799/year, Elite $4,999/year, plus a one-time $99 setup fee. 0% intro APR credit card options add $199/year. No free trial.
- Does well: One subscription bundles a financing marketplace, branded quotes and contracts, invoicing, and payments with a QuickBooks integration. Contractors who show monthly payment options on every quote close bigger jobs and get paid faster.
- Gaps: The subscription price means shops pay whether or not anyone uses the financing, and adoption still depends on salespeople presenting it on every quote. Hearth has payment reminders, but a shop running on Pro or Elite is already paying for an "everything" tool while invoicing, quoting, and follow-up live in different tabs nobody opens after hours. Most important line: Hearth is a strong kit, but it's shelfware unless the team uses it every day and follows up on every quote.
- Pitch tailoring: "You're paying for a full Hearth toolkit, which is great, but it only works when the team presents financing on every quote. We work alongside Hearth so every big quote goes out with payment options attached and gets followed up, protecting the subscription you're already paying for."

### Service Finance (and GreenSky)
- What: Point-of-sale home improvement lenders offering promotional financing (deferred interest, fixed-rate plans) through enrolled contractor networks.
- Typical user: Established HVAC, roofing, window, and siding companies with in-home sales teams; larger shops that run financing as part of their sales process.
- Pricing: Contractor enrollment is typically free; the merchant pays a dealer fee per funded loan (rates vary by plan and are not publicly published). GreenSky is now owned by a Sixth Street-led consortium (since March 2024); Service Finance is a subsidiary of Truist Bank.
- Pricing detail: No public merchant price sheets exist for either program, so treat pricing as quote-based. Homeowner loan amounts run up to $65,000 with GreenSky and $150,000 with Service Finance.
- Does well: These are the heavyweights of promotional financing; instant credit decisions at the kitchen table, long promotional terms, and big credit lines let closers sell the full system instead of the repair. GreenSky has financed nearly 6 million consumers through over 10,000 merchants.
- Gaps: The financing is powerful, but the contractor still has to apply the customer, present the terms, and manage the purchase through clunky merchant portals or phone apps. Financing drop-off happens when a sales rep half-explains the terms, the customer applies and gets confused by deferred-interest math, or nobody follows up after approval before the offer window expires. Most important line: the loan is the easy part; the fragile part is the human sales process around it, where approvals stall and homeowners go cold.
- Pitch tailoring: "Service Finance and GreenSky give you the muscle to close big jobs, no question. We work alongside your financing program by following up with approved-but-unbooked customers and making sure the payment option gets presented clearly on every sales call."

### PaySimple
- What: Payment, billing, and customer management platform built for recurring and repeat-service businesses, with ACH, invoicing, and autopay features.
- Typical user: Carpet cleaning, pest control, lawn care, and cleaning services that bill monthly or per-visit; shops with membership or maintenance plans.
- Pricing: Advertised credit card processing 2.9% plus 30 cents per transaction plus monthly fees; ACH about 1% plus 30 cents. Partner integrations (e.g., ServiceFusion, ImproveIt 360) carry custom rates around $10 to $15/month with their own fee schedules. Exact plan pricing is quote-based.
- Does well: Strong on recurring billing and autopay, which is exactly what subscription-style home services need. ACH support cuts processing costs on repeat billing, and invoice automation is better than most peers.
- Gaps: Recurring billing is only as good as the payment method on file; cards expire, ACH returns happen, and PaySimple's dunning is email-based, so failed payments quietly turn into lost customers. Shops also discover a pile of nickel-and-dime fees (statement, batch, PCI, on-file fees) they didn't model. Most important line: autopay collects beautifully until a card fails, and then nobody notices until the customer is three months behind and gone.
- Pitch tailoring: "PaySimple's autopay is the right engine for a recurring-revenue business. We work alongside PaySimple by jumping on failed payments immediately with real follow-up, before an expired card turns into a lost customer."

## Top 3 gaps

- Unpaid invoice follow-up dies in the office. Every accounting tool sends a reminder email or two and stops; nobody persists, nobody escalates, and receivables age out while the owner is on a jobsite. This is the single biggest cash leak across QuickBooks, Xero, Square, Stripe, and PaySimple users.
- Financing is offered inconsistently at the point of sale. Wisetack, Hearth, GreenSky, and Service Finance all work well mechanically, but techs and salespeople skip the pitch, customers start applications and stall, and approved-but-unbooked deals go cold with no follow-up. The tool exists; the discipline doesn't.
- Failed and abandoned payments slip through silently. Expired cards on PaySimple autopay, abandoned Stripe payment links, ignored Square invoices, and jobsites where the tech "forgets" to charge all look identical from the owner's chair: revenue that was earned but never collected.

After hours and weekends are the worst: emergency calls like burst pipes and dead furnaces go to voicemail, and callers hang up and dial the next contractor on the list. Industry data puts the missed-call rate at 40 percent or higher for small shops. The tools below are how contractors try to plug that hole today: human answering services that pick up the phone, and call tracking software that measures which marketing actually makes it ring.

For contractors, reputation and marketing run on two parallel tracks. ## Part 6: Lead Marketplaces, Website Chat, and Online Booking

Most contractors do not have a lead problem, they have a lead handling problem. Leads arrive from marketplaces, Google, and the website, and the shop that calls back in under five minutes wins the job while everyone else pays for the privilege of losing. Speed-to-lead is the single biggest variable in lead economics: a $35 shared lead can cost about $583 per signed job when the contractor is first to call with a four-minute callback, and roughly $1,556 when third in rotation with a twelve-minute response time. After the lead arrives, the handoff is the leak. Chat messages sit unanswered, booking forms go nowhere, and follow-up depends on whoever is in the office that day.

### Angi (HomeAdvisor)
- What: The largest home-services lead marketplace; HomeAdvisor's pro side is now branded Angi Leads.
- Typical user: Remodeling, roofing, plumbing, HVAC, electrical, and general contractors of all sizes.
- Pricing: Membership plus per-lead fees, roughly $15 to $120+ per shared lead by trade, with many contractors reporting 12-month contract terms. Exact pricing varies by market and is not published as a single schedule.
- Does well: Volume is real. Angi spends enormous amounts on its own advertising, so contractors can buy a steady flow of project requests without building their own marketing. Name recognition with homeowners is high.
- Gaps: Each lead is sold to 3-5+ competing shops, so the contractor pays for a footrace they usually lose. Close rates on shared leads run 6-10%, making cost per booked job the number that matters, not cost per lead. The FTC issued a final order against HomeAdvisor in 2023 over deceptive lead-marketing claims, and many contractors complain about paying for bad or recycled leads. Most important line: you are renting a shared queue, not buying customers, and speed-to-lead decides whether that rent pays off.
- Pitch tailoring: "Angi gets you in front of homeowners, which is the part you can buy. The part you cannot buy is answering first. PING Social works alongside Angi by responding to every new lead in seconds so you stop being the shop that paid and lost."

Like Angi, the shared-lead footrace applies, and most small shops respond far too slowly because the owner is on the tools. Most important line: on Thumbtack the first human response almost always wins, and that is exactly where small shops leak money.
- Pitch tailoring: "Thumbtack is honest about what it charges you per contact, which is more than most platforms give you. The gap is that every contact you paid for is also paid for by four other shops. PING Social works alongside Thumbtack to make sure you are always the first reply."

## How to detect a prospect's stack from public signals

Never claim a shop uses a tool without evidence. Grade everything you find using Nolan's scale. When in doubt, the grade drops, never rises.

- **CONFIRMED**: the vendor's name or domain appears on the shop's own site. Examples: a booking button that loads from booking.servicetitan.com, housecallpro.com/booking, or a Jobber booking URL; a footer line like "Powered by Housecall Pro"; a chat widget loading from Podium's domain; a review link pointing to a Podium, Birdeye, or Grade.us URL; an "Angi Super Service Award" badge or Thumbtack profile link they published themselves; a "financing available through Wisetack/Hearth" line on their site.
- **STRONG INDICATION**: evidence from a source the shop controls but outside their homepage. Examples: a job posting asking for "ServiceTitan experience"; a Facebook post mentioning their CompanyCam photos; a Google Business profile review the owner replied to via Birdeye or Podium (the reply signature sometimes names the platform).
- **WEAK INDICATION**: circumstantial signals. Examples: a "Call for a free estimate" site with no booking widget (suggests no FSM, but proves nothing); a 1-800 number that rotates by source (suggests call tracking, but could be the carrier); industry-norm usage for their trade and size.
- **UNKNOWN**: the default. Most Western Colorado owner-operators live here: phone-first shops with a simple website. Do not assume software exists.

Where to look, in order of signal quality:
1. Their website: footers, "book online" buttons, chat widgets, review links, financing badges, contact forms. View the page source for vendor domains (booking.servicetitan.com, housecallpro.com, getjobber.com, podium.com widgets).
2. Their Google Business profile and reviews: reply patterns, review-request links.
3. Their Facebook page: posts mentioning software, review links they share.
4. Job postings: Indeed or Craigslist ads naming tools.
5. Their own marketing: "As seen on Angi," Yelp badges, Nextdoor mentions.

What a stack usually means for the pitch:
- FSM present (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, etc.): the shop is operationally serious. The gap is never the software, it is the unanswered moments around it. Lead with missed-call capture and quote follow-up working alongside their FSM.
- Answering service present (Ruby, Smith.ai, PATLive): they already feel the missed-call pain and pay premium per-minute rates for messages. Lead with cost and the message-that-dies-in-an-inbox problem.
- Review tool present (Podium, Birdeye, NiceJob): they care about reputation. Lead with the fact that slow response and missed calls are what generate the bad reviews they are paying to collect.
- Lead marketplace heavy (Angi, Thumbtack, LSA): they pay per lead. Lead with speed-to-lead: the first responder wins, everyone else donates.
- Nothing detectable (phone-only, thin website): the best prospect. Do not pitch software at all. Pitch the outcome: every call answered, every request captured, no admin drag, owner stays in control.

Note for Nolan: Quo (formerly OpenPhone) ships its own AI receptionist add-on called Sona, with published per-call credit pricing. Shops already on Quo may have Sona available. The honest position is that Sona is a phone-system feature, not a workflow study: it answers, but nobody verified calls become jobs. That missing layer is PING's opening, and Nolan knows Quo firsthand, which is a credibility advantage.

## The 5 gaps to lead with

Researched across all 65 tools, September 2026. These are the openings that show up no matter what the shop already pays for.

1. **Missed and after-hours calls go nowhere.** Nearly every FSM logs the missed call and stops. Answering services take a message into an inbox. AI bots produce a transcript and quit. The caller who reaches voicemail at 7 PM calls the next name on Google. No tool in a contractor's stack reliably turns an unanswered call into a booked job. This is the single biggest revenue leak and PING's strongest opening.

2. **Quotes die without follow-up.** Every quoting and FSM tool sends beautiful proposals and then goes silent. Reps do not follow up consistently, cold quotes never get re-engaged, and estimate follow-up features are tier-gated, manual, or absent. A persistent follow-up layer on top of whatever the shop already uses converts revenue they already quoted.

3. **Paid leads with no speed-to-lead.** Marketplaces sell the same lead to 3 to 5 shops and charge whether the shop answers or not. Google LSA charges per lead at a ~44% book rate. The first responder wins the job; everyone else pays to lose. Most contractors respond in 30-plus minutes because the owner is on a jobsite. Instant response is the highest-ROI layer PING can add.

4. **Answering services meter by the minute and only take messages.** Ruby overage runs $3.30 to $5.40 per minute. Smith.ai human plans run $300 to $2,100 a month. Every service delivers caller info to an inbox or portal, and none of them qualify, book, or follow up. The shop pays premium rates for messages that still depend on the office catching up.

5. **Marketing and review spend leaks at the inbound handoff.** Agencies like Scorpion and Blue Corona drive traffic and calls, then hand off at the worst moment: shops lose 20 to 30 percent of inbound before anyone responds. Review platforms collect reviews for experiences the shop did not fix: missed calls and slow estimates are what generate the bad reviews they pay to manage. PING protects the ad spend and the reputation by owning the response.

*Rule of thumb: one gap per conversation. Name the leak, praise the tool they already pay for, and position PING as the layer that works alongside it so the owner stays in control.*
