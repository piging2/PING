## Ecosystem notes

### Solar (Western Slope, mid-September 2026)
Federal tax credits expired 12/31/2025 under the 2025 federal budget bill (stated on Solar Design Group's own site), so every remaining lead costs more to win, which is the core argument for better capture and follow-up. La Plata Electric Association issued a February 2024 news release responding to increased solar generation in Durango, signaling utility rate and net-metering pressure in the Four Corners area; interconnection and net-metering terms vary by utility across the Western Slope (Holy Cross Energy, Delta-Montrose Electric Association, Grand Valley Power, Yampa Valley Electric Association, Gunnison County Electric Association, Xcel territory), and installers like Atlasta and Brightside build permit and interconnection handling into their sales process. Installer rebate paperwork (GEO, LPEA) handled by shops like Durango Solar Works adds admin load that fits automation. Orphaned-system repair is a real sub-market: Sunsense Solar markets rescue of systems installed by other firms, and Shaw Solar and High Noon Solar both sell maintenance contracts, a good recurring-revenue angle for PING service reminders. Segment heat: strong. Western Slope solar proved the richest new segment of the round with 9 qualified candidates; shops are small, consultative, and permit-heavy, which is exactly the lead-follow-up profile PING targets.

### Insulation (Western Slope, mid-September 2026)
The insulation pool is genuinely thin under the own-site email/form rule: 1 qualified candidate after a full sweep. Grand Junction has the densest cluster (Accurate Insulation, EnergyPro Insulation, Pantuso Insulation, plus directory-only names like Alpine Insulation, Ace Insulation, Grand Valley Home Insulation), but most established firms run phone-only own sites or exist only in directories. Durango/Pagosa Springs has a lively spray foam scene tied to mountain-town winterization and second-home work, plus the 4CORE Home Upgrade program network in the southwest that lists many of these same firms as preferred contractors. That program connection is a real pressure point: rebate and energy-efficiency questions create qualification and paperwork load even when the contractor does not expose any rebate software publicly. Seasonal timing is favorable (mid-September pre-heating-season weatherization rush: attic top-ups, blown-in work, air sealing, ventilation, ice-dam prevention), but digitally contactable prospects are scarce. Pitch note for the segment: insulation shops are field-heavy (crews on spray foam rigs, attics, rooflines) and tend to publish same-day or 24-hour callback promises, which makes instant-response lead capture a strong, concrete pitch angle even when their visible software is nothing. Expect future insulation batches to stay small.

### Flooring / hardwood / tile (Western Slope, mid-September 2026)
Also thin under the strict rule: 3 qualified from a broad sweep. Market pattern: flooring here is dominated by dealer-network showrooms (Carpets Plus Flooring America affiliates, Gallagher's Flooring in GJ, Carpetime in GJ), independent installer shops that are phone-only (The Flooring Guy Durango, Home Roots Hardwood Flooring Durango, Yanon's Hardwood Flooring Montrose, Colorado Hardwood Floors Montrose, Walk On Wood Durango, Carpets Plus Steamboat), and Thumbtack-only operators. Seasonal timing: mid-September is the pre-winter install window in mountain towns (Aspen, Steamboat, Telluride), with second-home owners rushing projects before ski season and short-term rental owners turning units over. Pitch angles: free in-home estimate requests that die unanswered while crews sand floors; out-of-town second-home owner coordination; sample-showroom appointment scheduling. NWFA and Bona certifications are common trust signals (Yanon's, Western Valley Flooring Designs, The Flooring Guy). Skip patterns: Flooring America dealer sites render no direct email, many independents are phone-only, and several "flooring" domains are dead or repurposed (Alpenglow's site 404s).
