What is a DWAI in Colorado?

Driving While Ability Impaired (DWAI) is a generally less severe charge than a DUI, but it is still a criminal charge with potentially serious penalties. DWAI is when you operate a vehicle with a blood alcohol content of greater than 0.05% but less than 0.08%.

Traffic Violations

A person may lose their driving privileges if there is an accumulation of too many penalty points within a set period of time. In Colorado, an adult driver has 12 points in any 12 consecutive months and 18 points in any 24 consecutive months if no violations have occurred. Because some violations carry as many as 12 points, if you receive a traffic ticket or are involved in an accident, it is imperative that you attempt to get the ticket reduced to a lesser charge or have the charge deferred (meaning that no action will be taken on the ticket if you comply with the requirements issued by the Court) to avoid penalty points against your license. We can help you through the legal process and do our best to save you points and help you to maintain your driving privileges.

Understanding Grand Junction Case Flow: Your Step-by-Step Guide

 

Navigating criminal law in Grand Junction, CO, requires familiarity with local procedures and timelines. At Hand Law, P.C., we believe that the more you understand the unique flow of cases in our community, the better prepared you’ll be for your legal journey. Below is a step-by-step guide to help you anticipate what comes next and make the most of your initial consultation with a criminal law attorney.

Step 1: Initial Arrest or Citation

 

Once you are arrested or cited, your next step is often an initial court appearance within a short period. Know the location of the courthouse and keep all paperwork organized.

Step 2: Advisement and Bond Hearing

 

At your first appearance, the judge will inform you of your rights and charges and may set bond. Prepare for this by gathering documents that demonstrate your ties to the community, such as employment records and proof of local residence.

Step 3: Pre-trial Conferences

 

You may attend several pre-trial conferences. It’s helpful to keep track of the dates and understand the expectations for each meeting. Written notes and a clear timeline will assist in communication with your attorney.

Step 4: Discovery Process

 

Ask your criminal law attorney to explain the discovery materials and highlight items you think are important to your defense.

Step 5: Negotiation and Plea Discussions

 

Local Grand Junction prosecutors may offer plea deals. Discuss the pros and cons of each offer in detail and request a breakdown of how similar situations have been resolved locally.

Step 6: Trial Preparation

 

If your case proceeds to trial, familiarize yourself with the jury selection process. Be proactive in preparing statements and identifying witnesses who can support your case.

Tips and Tricks for Consultation Readiness

 

  • Prepare a Chronology: Create a timeline of events relating to your situation.

 

  • Collect Relevant Documents: Bring police reports, citations, and any related paperwork.

 

  • Formulate Questions: Write down any questions for your attorney about criminal law.

 

  • List Potential Witnesses: Identify people who could testify on your behalf.

 

  • Clarify Goals: Be honest about your desired outcome to help your attorney advocate for you.
