## STRATEGIC ASYMMETRY (CODA)

There is one architectural distinction to defend particularly hard now: FYD should not become the canonical database of somebody's business; it should become the durable compiler of evidence + owner intent into useful, authorized projections. That is what makes provider optionality possible.

ATProto is a good current example: its permission system can authorize specific collections/actions and progressively request more access later. FYD should be able to map its capability model onto that without redefining the business. MCP similarly gives you an increasingly standardized way to expose tools/resources to AI clients, but it should remain a projection of PING capabilities, not their source of truth. Automerge may eventually solve concurrent/local owner editing, but current FYD does not need to inherit a CRDT architecture simply because that option exists.

That's the strategic asymmetry: other systems can become inputs, outputs, editors, networks, or transports without becoming FYD's foundation. The persistent entity, its evidence, human intent, capabilities, relationships, and history remain continuous while everything around them can change.
