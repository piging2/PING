# Voice-clone options comparison for Nolan's voice
**Date:** 2026-09-19 · **Purpose:** read-only research (no accounts created, nothing uploaded) · **Use cases:** (1) short voice notes (latency matters), (2) AI podcast in his voice (long-form stability matters) · **Reference material:** ~2.8 h of verified Nolan speech (24 kHz mono WAVs on the Pig)

## Premise correction first
There is no "CozyVoice" from Tencent. The open-source voice-clone family you were thinking of is **CosyVoice** (spelled with one "o" after "C"), built by the **FunAudioLLM team** (Alibaba-affiliated researchers). Its repo already lives in Nolan's Docker experiments (there is a `Dockerfile.cosyvoice` on the Pig). Options 1 and 2 below are therefore one option with two generations; this note keeps the numbering honest rather than comparing a real product against a phantom one.

## Qwen3-TTS: why his earlier attempts likely failed, and whether to revisit
The output folder on the Pig shows clone attempts labeled FAILED, but the failure reasons are not documented there. Documented community failure modes for exactly this setup are (1) **sample-rate mismatch**, described as the #1 silent failure: Qwen3-TTS requires 24 kHz reference audio, and training/inference with other rates degrades output without an error; (2) **default fine-tune hyperparameters are wrong for single-speaker data**: default learning rate 2e-5 produces noise (use ~2e-6), and default LoRA scale 1.0 oversteers (use ~0.3-0.35); (3) reference transcripts must match the reference audio exactly. Any one of these produces output that sounds broken enough to label FAILED. Given his corpus is already 24 kHz mono and he now has an evaluated reference file (SRC-VOICE-000011), this path is worth revisiting: Qwen3-TTS 1.7B-12Hz currently posts the best published content-consistency numbers of the open options and is the most streaming-native, which serves both voice notes and podcasting.

## Recommendation (quality-first)
Build the primary clone **self-hosted on the Pig with CosyVoice 3** (and evaluate **Qwen3-TTS 1.7B zero-shot** alongside it; both are Apache-2.0, both take his 24 kHz WAVs as-is, and the environment is already half-built from his September experiments). This keeps his biometric voice data at home, costs nothing per generation, and gives him instruct-mode prosody control plus ~150 ms streaming latency for voice notes, with sentence-chunked batch generation for podcast episodes. Then, because "perfect" needs a benchmark, buy one month of **ElevenLabs Creator ($22)** and build a **Professional Voice Cloning** version from his 2.8 h of material as the hosted reference standard: generate the same scripts from all three, run the blind A/B against his real voice, and let the listening test decide. Use **Cartesia Sonic 3.5** only if voice-note latency at scale ever becomes the bottleneck (sub-90 ms, cheaper per minute). Avoid Fish Audio's self-serve tier for this use case: its perpetual irrevocable license and no training opt-out are a poor fit for cloning your own voice.
