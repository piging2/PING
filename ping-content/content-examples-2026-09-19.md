## 1. Funny + educational (EDUCATIONAL + HUMOR/CULTURE, humor 3)

**Hook:** "Your website says 24/7. Your phone says voicemail."

**Caption draft:** Your site promises 24/7 emergency service. A homeowner
locked out at 2 AM calls. Voicemail. They do not leave a message, they call
the next listing. That 24/7 badge just became a 24/7 liability, because now
the customer knows you did not pick up. The fix is not a bigger ad budget. It
is an after-hours net: something that answers, books, and confirms while you
sleep. PIGING means the 2 AM caller becomes a 8 AM job on your calendar, not
a competitor's.

**Factual basis:** Round-32 research: several Western Colorado locksmiths
advertise 24/7 or always-on-call with no email, form, or booking path.
OBSERVATION (their sites show phone-only intake). INFERENCE (a 2 AM caller
dials the next listing). No business named.

**Humor mechanism:** absurd contrast (the badge vs the voicemail).

**Educational value:** teaches that advertised availability without intake
is worse than no promise at all.

**PING connection:** after-hours call capture as the product; PING builds it.

**CTA:** none in-post (educational); profile carries the Page.

**Risk:** low. No business named, no fabricated numbers.

**Why shareable:** every trade owner has lived the 2 AM missed call; tags a
friend with "this is us."

## 2. Hot take (HOT TAKE, humor 2)

**Hook:** "Most businesses do not have a lead problem. They have a
response-time problem."

**Caption draft:** Everyone says you need more leads. FACT: the five market
gaps in our software playbook all point the same way. OBSERVATION:
after-hours calls rarely become booked jobs; paid leads go to whoever answers
first; answering services mostly take messages. INFERENCE: the leads already
exist, they leak at the handoff. PING OPINION: stop buying lead gen until
your response time is under five minutes, because every new lead just finds
the same hole faster. Fix the bucket before you buy more water.

**Factual basis:** software-playbook.md five market gaps (documented from
prospect research). FACT/OBSERVATION/INFERENCE/OPINION labeled in the copy.

**Humor mechanism:** dry aphorism ("fix the bucket before you buy more
water").

**Educational value:** reframes marketing spend as a response-time problem.

**PING connection:** PING's consultative positioning: diagnose before
prescribing; PING builds the response layer.

**CTA:** soft: "If your phone is the hole, PING can help."

**Risk:** medium-low. Contrarian but evidence-labeled; may draw "but my
market is different" replies, which is discussion potential, not danger.

**Why shareable:** challenges a dominant assumption with a quotable line;
owners forward it to their marketing agency.

## 3. Current-event translation (BREAKING/CURRENT EVENT, humor 2)

**Hook:** "Google just answered your customer's question. You were not in
the answer."

**Caption draft:** NEWS: AI answers now sit on top of local search results.
WHAT HAPPENED: the customer asks "best plumber near me" and gets a paragraph,
not ten blue links. WHY EVERYONE IS TALKING ABOUT IT: the click moved from
your website to the answer box. WHAT IT ACTUALLY MEANS: Google and the AI
models can only cite businesses they understand, which means clear services,
consistent name/address/phone, real reviews, and structured info. WHO SHOULD
CARE: every shop whose site is a phone number and a prayer. WHAT TO DO: one
page per service, one consistent business name everywhere, FAQs in plain
language. Be the business the answer quotes.

**Factual basis:** publicly documented shift to AI-generated answers in
search (Google AI Overviews and equivalents); our own AI-discoverability
field (manual 16h) measures exactly this. ILLUSTRATIVE framing: the
production version must cite the specific event and date at publish time.

**Humor mechanism:** dry dread ("a phone number and a prayer").

**Educational value:** translates a platform shift into a three-step
checklist for a plumber.

**PING connection:** PING's AEO/AI-discoverability assessments; the
translator identity in action.

**CTA:** none in-post; follow for the next translation.

**Risk:** low-medium. Must be re-verified against the actual current event
before publishing; never publish this draft on stale news.

**Why shareable:** "send this to every business owner you know" energy;
timely and actionable.

## 4. Competitor/industry clown + teaching (COMPETITOR/MARKET ANALYSIS, humor 3)

**Hook:** "Congratulations, you bought an AI receptionist. It answers the
phone. It just does not make you money."

**Caption draft:** CLAIM: "Never miss another call." SOURCE: every AI
receptionist landing page ever. VERIFICATION: our playbook's market gap #4,
documented across prospect research: answering services meter time and
usually only take messages. WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE: an answered call that
becomes a message in an inbox is a missed call with extra steps. BUSINESS
IMPACT: you paid per minute to find out someone called. PING'S
INTERPRETATION: answering is table stakes; the job is booking, confirming,
and following up. PRACTICAL ALTERNATIVE: demand the full loop, answered to
booked to confirmed, or keep your voicemail and save the subscription.

**Factual basis:** software-playbook.md market gap #4 (answering services
meter time, usually only take messages), observed across rounds. Industry
practice, no company named, no invented numbers.

**Humor mechanism:** the clown+teach formula; "a missed call with extra
steps" as the punchline that carries the insight.

**Educational value:** teaches buyers to evaluate the full loop, not the
greeting.

**PING connection:** PING builds answered-to-booked infrastructure; the
adult-in-the-room credibility play.

**CTA:** none; the teaching is the pitch.

**Risk:** medium. Clowning an industry invites defensive replies from
vendors; that is acceptable discussion as long as claims stay documented.
Never name a company without receipts.

**Why shareable:** buyers forward it to partners before signing a contract;
vendors argue in the comments, which is reach.

## 5. Serious/inspiring (INSPIRATIONAL, humor 1)

**Hook:** "Nobody writes headlines about the shop that showed up on time."

**Caption draft:** Forty-plus Western Colorado trade shops in our research
are one to five people. No marketing department. No dispatch software. Just
someone who answers the phone between jobs and shows up when they said they
would. That reliability is the whole business, and it is invisible until it
breaks. Technology should protect that, not replace it. The best automation
is the kind nobody notices: the call answered, the job booked, the reminder
sent, while the owner is under a sink being good at the actual job. Here is
to the shops that keep the lights on, literally.

**Factual basis:** observed prospect base (Western CO owner-operated trade
shops, 1-5 people, documented across rounds). No individual named; no
fabricated story.

**Humor mechanism:** light only ("keep the lights on, literally"); the
posture is sincere.

**Educational value:** reframes automation as protection of craft, not
replacement.

**PING connection:** PING's core positioning (technology strengthens the
worker); values statement, not a pitch.

**CTA:** none. This post exists to establish values.

**Risk:** low. Sincere and general.

**Why shareable:** tradespeople share what honors the trade; high save rate
among owners.

## 7. Highly shareable practical tip (PRACTICAL HOW-TO, humor 2)

**Hook:** "A 30-second audit that will annoy you."

**Caption draft:** Saturday, 7 PM. Call your own business from your personal
phone. Count the rings. If voicemail picks up, hang up and ask yourself: how
many customers did that this week? You do not need analytics for this one.
You need one Saturday evening and the willingness to hear what your customers
hear. If the answer bothers you, fix the after-hours net before you spend
another dollar on ads. The cheapest lead-gen audit in existence, and it costs
exactly one phone call.

**Factual basis:** the missed-call pattern observed across dozens of Western
CO prospects (phone-only intake, no after-hours net). The audit itself is a
real action any owner can take; no statistics invented.

**Humor mechanism:** playful dare ("will annoy you"); the wink is the hook.

**Educational value:** a concrete self-diagnostic; teaches lead leakage
without jargon.

**PING connection:** the conversion ladder step 4 (diagnose it); PING builds
the after-hours net.

**CTA:** soft: "If the audit bothers you, PING can help."

**Risk:** low. No claims, no stats, just an experiment.

**Why shareable:** dares get forwarded ("do this tonight"); saves as a
checklist item; owners tag partners.
